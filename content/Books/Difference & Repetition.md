The book I keep coming back to. Deleuze tries to think difference *in itself*, not as the gap between two things that were already the same, and repetition as the return of that difference rather than of the identical. The negative, for him, is only "the image of difference, but a flattened and inverted image, like the candle in the eye of the ox", which is probably my favourite sentence in philosophy and turns up in [[Attention & Difference]] and [[Image-Metaphors|What Is an Image?]].

Deleuze later said the third chapter, "The Image of Thought", was the most necessary: a critique of the picture of thinking that philosophy takes for granted (recognition, common sense, error as thought's only enemy) and an argument that what really forces us to think is an *encounter*, something that "can only be sensed". That chapter is more or less the model for what I'm trying to do with images.
