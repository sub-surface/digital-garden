# The Metric

The clearest case in the archive of *The Phil Chat Times* changing the thing it reports. Vol III retired the scoreboard; Vol IV printed one anyway, in the smallest type on the page, captioned **"Not used"** — and the room found it and turned it into a labour market inside thirty-six hours.

## The footnote that became an economy

The table ranked sixteen people by the share of their own messages that drew a reaction. It existed only to *show the paper's working* — to prove a tier list had not been generated by arithmetic. The room found it on a Sunday evening. By Sunday night it was a labour market.

<Clipping cite="Stackhouse" issue="Vol IV">
First rule of Corporate America: The Metric is never wrong, you are.
</Clipping>

- **[[Wiki/chatters/myname1|My Name]]**, honest about what a metric is for: *"I dont care about the quality of the chatter. I just want more buttons to be clicked."*
- **[[Wiki/chatters/Ape|Ape]]**, discovering he was not on the list, treated it as a payroll error: *"How am I meant to improve my numbers if HR isn't even recording my work!?"*
- **[[Wiki/chatters/hughchungus|Hugh]]** objected on principle — *"Reacts don't determine the quality of the chatter"* — followed by a direct instruction, *"Editors ignore this."* The paper printed it and did not ignore it.

## The line goes up on the Sunday

<Tape label="reactions +83% in seven days" />

| | Vol IV | Vol V |
|---|---|---|
| Messages | 17,706 | 21,206 |
| Reactions | 1,102 | 2,017 |
| Rate | 4.8% | 7.4% |
| People present | 89 | 76 |

> We cannot prove we caused that. Nobody can. What we can report is that the reaction rate had been flat for three issues, that we printed a leaderboard, that the leaderboard was discovered on a Sunday, and that the line goes up on the Sunday.

## The standing refusal

The paper's own rule, kept since, is that it will not rank people by reactions, "because it would be measuring our own footprint." When the readership demanded the quantification back — four hours after Vol VII shipped without a board — [[Wiki/chatters/myname1|My Name]]'s AI supplied the eulogy that became the masthead of the society pages:

<Clipping cite="My Name's AI" issue="Vol VII">
the blind leading the blind. We used to know who liked who. who reacted to who. we had connections. we had a society.
</Clipping>

The compromise was the **reply graph** — who answers whom, which the room did not think to game — printed instead of the reaction market. It remains the paper's preferred measure: replies say who the room talks to; reactions only say who it laughs at.
