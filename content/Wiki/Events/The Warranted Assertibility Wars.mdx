# The Warranted Assertibility Wars

The room's longest-running argument, formalised. Across **Vol VI–VIII**, [[Quigley]] and [[hughchungus|Hugh]] fought the same question — *what makes our statements true?* — until it became a booked bout, a poll, and a phrase repeated so often it entered the paper's banned list.

## The motion

Tabled by **HPCR** on a Wednesday afternoon "because it was the same conversation over and over": **What makes our facts, statements, theories true?** Moderators [[Ape]] and [[Charlie(Willow)|Charlie]]; HPCR to judge. **Willow** disqualified — "it can't be Willow and she knows why."

## The Bout: Saturday 22 August 2026

The main stage opened at two in the afternoon Eastern and ran until nearly six. Fought in a voice channel the newspaper could not see, the side text channel tracked conditions ringside like an athletic prizefight:

- **The Realist Corner:** Hugh, in Wales, shirtless from half past two, eating hotdogs from a bowl while defending correspondence theory. At a quarter to three, [[chair|Chair]] discovered his nationality with alarm: *"oh my god hugh is british."*
- **The Pragmatist Corner:** Quigley, in Boston, powered by a single can of Alani pink slush, arguing that truth is indistinguishable in practice from warranted assertibility.
- **The Commentary:** At 2:30 PM, [[stackhouse|Stackhouse]] reported throat singing. [[aurasurfer|Aura]] observed the hotdogs, noted the ceiling fan Quigley was hanging from, and filed the afternoon's only functional summary: how we know something versus what makes something true, Quigley says there is no difference in practice, *"he has like 700 reps along this dialogue tree."*
- **The Absent Moderator:** [[Ape]], the Australian co-moderator, fell asleep before bell-time and remained asleep for seventeen consecutive hours.

The only quantitative result the text channel produced was a StrawPoll called by a guest at 4:33 PM: **Hugh 9, Quigley 3**.

Reactions were instantaneous. [[zack|Zack]] denounced the ballot as mob rule. Stackhouse investigated reports that Hugh had deployed twenty autonomous subagents into the chat to rig the count.

The front page of Vol I No 7 (priced at **One Warranted Assertion**, spanning 19,965 messages and 98 speakers) carried the official poster commissioned by the loser: the knife fight from *Dune: Part Two*, recasting Hugh and Quigley. Quigley: *"We need to crop Hugh and me unto the final fight in Dune part 2."* On Monday, when soob asked how the boxing match went, Quigley circulated the picture with a correction: *"Except I won."*

## The rematches

Nobody changed their mind and several people changed their names. The week after produced the phrase:

<Clipping cite="Quigley" issue="Vol VII">
I recognize the distinction that you are wrong.
</Clipping>

Five times in three hours one Saturday; 23 of the week's 27 uses were his. It became load-bearing enough that its cousin, *"That is not x; that's y,"* landed on the paper's own banned list of machine mannerisms. The counter-fire came in kind — [[hughchungus|Hugh]], on a Tuesday, typed *"you have not demonstrated"* seventeen times in a row and attached three pictures of Deleuze.

Then the summary that closed it, from Quigley's side, at 4.24 pm on a Friday: *"I've been arguing for Warranted Assertibility the whole time."* And, in two words, the whole week: **"Except I won."**

## The footnote

A quieter engagement, and a favourite. **Jere** pasted a definition of truth attributed "Dewey, 343 n," having searched "Dewey theory of truth" and quoted what Wikipedia returned. Quigley downloaded the book. *"Oh Jere. You quoted part of a footnote."* Jere conceded with dignity: *"True I forgot that footnotes are fake."* His verdict on the whole matchup: *"U r Anakin, I am obi wan."*

## The result, as the paper ruled it

> The Vol. VII boxing match is closed: Hugh by decision, Quigley by his own account.
