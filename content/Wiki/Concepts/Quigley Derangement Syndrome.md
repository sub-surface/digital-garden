# Quigley Derangement Syndrome

**Quigley Derangement Syndrome (QDS)** is *The Phil Chat Times*' name for a measured social phenomenon: [[Quigley]] is answered more carefully than anyone else in the room and rewarded less than almost anyone. It is the only condition in the archive named after a chatter, and the paper treats it as a clinical fact rather than a joke, because it is one — the numbers are unusually clean.

## The measurement

> Everybody answers him. Nobody laughs.

- **Vol V:** ninetieth percentile for effort, thirtieth for being enjoyed.
- **Vol VI:** answered **78.4 times per hundred messages** — the highest figure in the room, on 804 messages — against a reaction percentile of **26**.

The paper calls that gap "the closest thing to a clinical measurement of Quigley Derangement Syndrome anyone has produced." The diagnosis is not that he is disliked; it is that he is *engaged with* at a rate no one else commands and *laughed with* at a rate that does not follow.

## Aetiology

The paper's reading locates the cause not in the sincerity — which it defends — but in the procedure. In a channel whose entire social technology is the retreat into irony, Quigley arrives every day with an earnest position, states his profession, location and political programme unprompted, does the reading, and cites it inside sixty seconds of being asked. The room has developed a condition about exactly that. When he loses an argument he goes and gets an adjudicator (see the god machine, in [[Quigley|his profile]]); when the adjudicator rules against him he annexes it.

## Prognosis

Documented as recurrent and self-limiting. When Quigley left in Vol II — covered "as an act of God" — **Pearl** filed the only forecast that came true: *"just wait a month or so."* He returned six days later with the paper's own coinage as an exit line: *"Let the QDS unravel."* Vol III's correction: "We accept that we no longer own the coinage and note that we never did."
