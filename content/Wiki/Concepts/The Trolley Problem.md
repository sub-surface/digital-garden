# Concept: The Trolley Problem

<div className="dropcap">

**The Trolley Problem** is a foundational ethical thought experiment introduced by British philosopher [[Philippa Foot]] in 1967 and substantially expanded by [[Judith Jarvis Thomson]]. Originally devised to investigate the moral permissibility of abortion and the **Doctrine of Double Effect**, the scenario probes the conflict between utilitarian consequentialism (maximizing net lives saved) and deontological constraints against intentionally harming an innocent person as a means to an end.

</div>

---

## 1. Classical Formulations

### Case 1: The Switch (Foot, 1967)
A runaway trolley is speeding down a railway track toward five workers who cannot escape in time. You are standing next to a lever. If you pull the lever, the trolley will be diverted onto a side track where a single worker is working.
* If you do nothing, five workers die.
* If you pull the lever, one worker dies.
* **Empirical Consensus**: Over 85% of surveyed respondents (and 70% of professional philosophers in the 2020 PhilPapers survey) judge that it is morally permissible (or obligatory) to pull the switch.

### Case 2: The Footbridge / Fat Man (Thomson, 1976)
The same runaway trolley is hurtling toward five workers. You are standing on a footbridge spanning the track next to a very heavy stranger. The only way to stop the trolley is to push this stranger off the bridge onto the track below; his bulk will stop the train, saving the five, but he will certainly die.
* If you do nothing, five workers die.
* If you push the stranger, one person dies and five live.
* **Empirical Consensus**: Over 80% of respondents judge that pushing the man is strictly **morally impermissible**, despite the arithmetic of casualties being identical to The Switch (1 death vs. 5 deaths).

### Case 3: The Transplant Surgeon
A surgeon has five patients each dying of different organ failures (heart, lungs, liver, kidneys). A healthy traveler comes in for a routine checkup. The surgeon could painlessly kill the traveler and transplant his organs into the five patients.
* Almost universally judged as heinous murder, demonstrating that pure aggregate welfarism violates deeply held intuitions regarding individual bodily rights.

## 2. Ethical Explanations

Moral philosophers have developed several principles to explain why our moral intuitions diverge between The Switch and The Footbridge:

### 1. The Doctrine of Double Effect (DDE)
Rooted in the medieval scholasticism of Thomas Aquinas, the DDE states that it is sometimes permissible to cause a serious harm as an unintended, foreseen **side effect** of promoting a good end, but impermissible to cause harm as an **intended means** to that end:
* In *The Switch*, the death of the single worker on the sidetrack is a tragic, foreseen side effect of steering the train away from five people. If the worker somehow escaped, the rescuer would be delighted.
* In *The Footbridge*, the collision with the heavy stranger is the direct, intended physical means by which the train is halted. If the stranger dodged, the five workers would die.

### 2. Kant's Formula of Humanity
Immanuel Kant's Categorical Imperative dictates: *"Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."* Pushing the stranger treats his physical body as a physical roadblock—a mere tool.

### 3. Killing versus Letting Die (Negative vs. Positive Duties)
Philippa Foot argued that agents have a stringent **negative duty** not to inflict injury upon others, which typically outweighs the **positive duty** to provide aid. In The Footbridge, pushing the man initiates a lethal physical battery; in The Switch, the agent redirects an existing threat.

## 3. Cognitive Neuroscience & Modern Applications

* **Dual-Process Moral Theory**: Joshua Greene demonstrated via fMRI studies that personal moral dilemmas (like The Footbridge) activate emotional and social brain regions (amygdala, medial prefrontal cortex), triggering rapid deontological vetos. Impersonal dilemmas (The Switch) engage cold deliberative calculation (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex).
* **Autonomous Vehicles and Machine Ethics**: The trolley problem has transitioned from abstract thought experiment to engineering specification for self-driving vehicle algorithms: should a vehicle swerve into a barrier (killing its passenger) to avoid hitting a school bus?
