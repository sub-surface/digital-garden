# Concept: Epistemic Injustice

<div className="dropcap">

**Epistemic Injustice** is a foundational concept in contemporary social epistemology and feminist philosophy, formulated by British philosopher [[Miranda Fricker]] in her seminal 2007 book *Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing*. It designates a distinctive form of moral and political harm done to an individual specifically in their **capacity as an epistemic subject**—that is, in their capacity as a knower, a reliable informant, or an interpreter of their own lived experience. Fricker demonstrated that social power dynamics do not merely affect political rights and economic distribution, but structurally warp who is believed, whose testimony is heard, and what experiences can be collectively understood.

</div>

## 1. The Two Primary Forms of Epistemic Injustice

Fricker identifies two distinct, asymmetrical ways in which epistemic injustice manifests:

```
                          THE TWO FORMS OF EPISTEMIC INJUSTICE
                                  EPISTEMIC INJUSTICE
                                (Wrong done to a knower)
                                           │
                    ┌──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
                    ▼                                             ▼
          TESTIMONIAL INJUSTICE                         HERMENEUTICAL INJUSTICE
   ┌───────────────────────────────────┐         ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
   │ • Communicative exchange          │         │ • Pre-communicative gap           │
   │ • Identity prejudice deflates     │         │ • Collective interpretive pool    │
   │   speaker's credibility           │         │   lacks concepts for experience   │
   │ • Speaker silenced / disbelieved  │         │ • Experience obscured / untellable│
   │ • Example: Tom Robinson on trial  │         │ • Example: Pre-1970s harassment   │
   └───────────────────────────────────┘         └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Testimonial Injustice

**Testimonial Injustice** occurs when prejudice on the part of a hearer leads them to award a **deflated level of credibility** to a speaker's word:

$$\text{Testimonial Injustice} = \text{Identity Prejudice} \times \text{Credibility Deficit}$$

* **The Mechanism**: It is not merely an honest epistemic misjudgment or error in calculating probabilities; it is driven by negative identity prejudices (such as racism, sexism, classism, or homophobia) that operate as unconscious cognitive stereotypes.
* **Canonical Literary Example (Tom Robinson)**:
  * In Harper Lee's *To Kill a Mockingbird*, Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused of raping a white woman in 1930s Alabama, gives clear, calm, truthful testimony in court.
  * Despite the obvious physical impossibilities of the prosecution's claims, the all-white jury completely discounts Robinson's testimony due to deep-seated racial prejudice, assigning his word near-zero credibility.
* **The Harm**:
  * **Primary Harm**: The speaker is insulted and diminished in their fundamental human dignity as a rational knower. They are treated not as a truth-transmitting human subject, but as a manipulative, defective object.
  * **Secondary Harm**: Practical and legal disaster (loss of liberty, imprisonment, economic marginalization, or epistemic loss of self-confidence).[^fricker-2007]

## 3. Hermeneutical Injustice

**Hermeneutical Injustice** occurs at a deeper, pre-communicative structural level:
> *"The injustice of having some significant area of one's social experience obscured from collective understanding owing to a structural prejudice in the collective hermeneutical resource."*

* **The Mechanism**: Society's shared conceptual vocabulary and interpretive tools (language, academic frameworks, media tropes) are disproportionately authored by socially dominant groups. Consequently, marginalized groups often endure experiences of harm that they cannot adequately communicate—or even fully make sense of to themselves—because the culture lacks the shared conceptual apparatus.
* **Historical Example (The Coining of "Sexual Harassment")**:
  * Prior to the mid-1970s, women in the workforce who were subjected to unwanted sexual advances, coercion, and humiliation by male superiors had no recognized legal or cultural term for their ordeal.
  * When Carmita Wood resigned from Cornell University in 1975 after suffering severe stress from her supervisor's predatory behavior, her unemployment claim was denied because the state viewed her departure as "personal" and voluntary.
  * Wood and feminist activists at Cornell gathered to articulate the common structure of this abuse, coining the term **"sexual harassment."**
  * Prior to the invention of that phrase, victims of such harassment suffered hermeneutical injustice: they were trapped in an agonizing cognitive gap where their suffering was rendered invisible, isolated, or pathologized as private hysteria.

## 4. Epistemic Virtues as Corrective Mechanisms

To combat epistemic injustice, Fricker argues that knowers must cultivate specific reflexive epistemic virtues:

1. **Testimonial Justice**: A virtuous hearer learns to critically monitor their own unreflective reactions. When evaluating testimony from a marginalized speaker, the hearer reflexively compensates for potential structural prejudices, neutralizing credibility deficits.
2. **Hermeneutical Justice**: A virtuous hearer exercises reflexive hermeneutical humility: when an interlocutor struggles to articulate an experience that sounds obscure or non-standard, the hearer does not instantly dismiss it as nonsense, but recognizes that the collective conceptual resources of society may be impoverished.

## 5. Subsequent Expansions & Contemporary Developments

Since 2007, philosophers have expanded Fricker's architecture:
* **Kristie Dotson (Epistemic Silencing & Testimonial Smothering)**: Analyzed how marginalized knowers, anticipating that their audience is unwilling or ill-equipped to comprehend their testimony, preemptively censor and compress their own speech (*testimonial smothering*).
* **José Medina (The Epistemology of Resistance)**: Explored **Epistemic Privilege** born of oppression—how marginalized subjects often develop a dual consciousness, seeing structural realities that dominant groups remain comfortable ignoring (*epistemic blindness*).
* **Miranda Fricker & Institutional Epistemology**: Extending the critique to automated algorithms, medical diagnosis, and predictive policing systems that encode historical biases into automated credibility scoring.
