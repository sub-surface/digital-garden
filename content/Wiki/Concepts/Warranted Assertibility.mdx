# Warranted Assertibility

**Warranted assertibility** is John Dewey's proposed successor to the classical notion of truth. On this view the philosophically useful property of a belief is not that it *corresponds* to a mind-independent reality, but that it has been arrived at by a competent process of inquiry and has so far survived it. A claim is warrantedly assertible when the inquiry that produced it holds up: it resolves the problem that prompted it, its consequences bear out, it coheres with other settled claims, and it remains open to further challenge.

> "A theory of the nature of the operations of inquiry... the term 'warranted assertibility' is preferred to the terms belief and knowledge." — Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (1938)

## In Phil Chat

Warranted assertibility is the hill [[Quigley]] has defended, at length and daily, across most of the paper's run. His formulation is deflationary about "truth" as a substantive relation and pragmatic about what replaces it: truth, he holds, is the name we give to whatever explanation currently best fits the data, and the interesting question is never *does it correspond?* but *is the assertion warranted?*

The opposing corner is usually [[Wiki/chatters/hughchungus|Hugh]], who defends a broadly representationalist, correspondence-flavoured position and treats the appeal to inquiry as changing the subject. Their exchange became [[Wiki/Events/The Warranted Assertibility Wars|a formal bout]]. Hugh's stock objection, filed by repetition — "you have not demonstrated," typed seventeen times in a row on one occasion — is the charge that a coherent, surviving, fruitful inquiry can still fail to *establish* anything about the world. Quigley's stock reply is that "establishing something about the world" is not a further step available to anyone, on any theory, and demanding it is the incoherent part.

## Why it matters here

The position has an unusual social property: it is very hard to lose an argument from inside it. Any objection ("but is it *really* true?") can be re-described as a demand for a criterion the objector cannot meet either. The paper's finding is that this is both its strength and the source of the room's exasperation — see [[Wiki/Concepts/Quigley Derangement Syndrome|Quigley Derangement Syndrome]]. Quigley made the immunity explicit during the rematches: *"I recognize the distinction, Hugh, that you will always be incorrect in holding to this system. Now I'm immune."*
