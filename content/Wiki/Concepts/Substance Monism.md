# Substance Monism

<div className="dropcap">

**Substance Monism** is the metaphysical doctrine that reality in its entirety consists of exactly **one fundamental substance**. In contrast to **Substance Dualism** (which posits two irreducibly distinct kinds of substances, such as mind and matter in [[René Descartes]]) and **Substance Pluralism** (which posits countless independent substances, such as Leibnizian monads or Democritean atoms), substance monism maintains that all diverse objects, minds, bodies, and events are merely dependent modes, aspects, or manifestations of a single, indivisible cosmic totality.

</div>

Throughout the history of philosophy, substance monism has appeared in several distinct and profound formulations across Western and Eastern traditions.

### Parmenides of Elea: Ontological Monism
In the fifth century BCE, [[Parmenides of Elea]] formulated the earliest Western argument for monism based on pure logical deduction from the concept of Being:
* Non-Being (*to me on*) cannot exist, be conceived, or be named.
* Therefore, What-Is (*To Eon*) cannot be divided, for there is no void or non-being to separate one part from another.
* What-Is is homogeneous, continuous, motionless, ungenerated, and **strictly One** (DK 28 B 8).

### Baruch Spinoza: *Deus sive Natura*
The definitive early modern formulation was achieved by [[Baruch Spinoza]] in Part I of the *Ethics* (1677)[^spinoza-monism-ref]:
1. **Definition**: Substance is defined as that which is in itself and is conceived through itself (*Ethics* Id3).
2. **Self-Sufficiency**: If two substances had different attributes, they could have nothing in common, and one could not be the cause of another.
3. **Uniqueness**: God is an absolutely infinite substance possessing infinite attributes, each expressing eternal and infinite essence (*Ethics* Id6).
4. **Conclusion**: *"Except God, no substance can be or, consequently, be conceived"* (*Ethics* Ip14).

In Spinoza's monism, God and Nature are identical (**Deus sive Natura**). You, your computer, the stars, and your thoughts are not independent substances; they are finite **modes** (*modi*) of the single divine substance, expressing its essence under the attributes of Thought and Extension.

[^spinoza-monism-ref]: Baruch Spinoza, *Ethics*, Part I. See [[Bibliography#Spinoza-Curley]].

## 2. Eastern Parallels: Advaita Vedanta

In classical Indian philosophy, [[Advaita Vedanta]]—systematized by [[Nagarjuna|Adi Shankara]]—presents an absolute metaphysical monism:
* ***Brahman*** is the sole, non-dual reality (*advaita*), characterized as pure Being, Consciousness, and Bliss (*Sat-Chit-Ananda*).
* The apparent multiplicity of the world is a superimposition (*adhyāsa*) generated by cosmic illusion (*maya*).
* The individual self (*Atman*) is fundamentally identical with Brahman: *"Tat Tvam Asi"* ("That Thou Art").

## 3. Neutral Monism: Mach, James, and Russell

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Ernst Mach, William James, and Bertrand Russell formulated **Neutral Monism**:
* The ultimate fabric of the universe is neither mental (as idealists claim) nor physical (as materialists claim).
* Reality consists of one basic, "neutral" stuff (sensory elements, events, experiences).
* Arranged in one relational context, these neutral elements constitute **Physical Objects**; arranged in another relational context (associated with memory and nervous systems), the very same elements constitute **Minds**.
* Neutral monism dissolves the Cartesian mind-body divide by treating mind and matter as two different organizational perspectives on a single neutral substrate.

## 4. Contemporary Priority Monism: Jonathan Schaffer

In contemporary analytic metaphysics, philosopher Jonathan Schaffer (*"Monism: The Priority of the Whole"*, 2010) revived monism by distinguishing between:
* **Existence Monism**: The radical claim that there exists only one concrete object (denying that chairs, particles, and planets exist).
* **Priority Monism**: The thesis that while plural parts (particles, planets, humans) genuinely exist, the **Cosmos as a whole is ontologically prior** to its parts. 

Drawing support from quantum entanglement (where the quantum state of an entangled whole cannot be reduced to the independent states of its individual particles), priority monists argue that the universe as a unified whole is the fundamental substance from which all localized phenomena dependently derive.
