# Concept: The Asymmetry of Pleasure and Pain

<div className="dropcap">

**The Asymmetry of Pleasure and Pain** (or the **Benatarian Asymmetry**) is a foundational axiological proof in contemporary population ethics and philosophical pessimism, formulated by South African philosopher [[David Benatar]] in *Better Never to Have Been* (2006). The argument demonstrates that because the absence of pain is intrinsically good while the absence of pleasure is *not bad* (unless there exists someone for whom that absence is a felt deprivation), non-existence possesses a systematic, insurmountable evaluative advantage over existence. As a consequence, bringing any sentient creature into existence always inflicts a serious moral harm.

</div>

## 1. Overview & The Four-Quadrant Matrix

Benatar begins with four widely accepted, common-sense axiological judgments regarding pain and pleasure:

```
                      BENATAR'S AXIOLOGICAL MATRIX
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
│        SCENARIO A: X EXISTS          │     SCENARIO B: X NEVER EXISTS       │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ (1) Presence of Pain                 │ (3) Absence of Pain                  │
│     Evaluation: BAD                  │     Evaluation: GOOD                 │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ (2) Presence of Pleasure             │ (4) Absence of Pleasure              │
│     Evaluation: GOOD                 │     Evaluation: NOT BAD              │
│                                      │     (unless someone is deprived)     │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The crucial structural pivot occurs between Quadrants (3) and (4):
* **Quadrant (3) — Absence of Pain is Good**: When an entity never exists, the total absence of pain and suffering is an unambiguous, positive good—even though there is no concrete person enjoying that absence (we celebrate that nobody suffers on the lifeless surface of Mars).
* **Quadrant (4) — Absence of Pleasure is Not Bad**: When an entity never exists, the absence of pleasure is **not bad** unless there is an actual existing subject for whom that absence constitutes an active deprivation. Nobody weeps for the billions of potential children who are never conceived, because there is no subject languishing in a limbo of unfulfilled joy.[^benatar-bnhb]

### The Evaluative Comparison:
When we compare Scenario A (Existing) with Scenario B (Never Existing):
* **Pain Comparison**: Scenario B is strictly superior to Scenario A (Good vs. Bad).
* **Pleasure Comparison**: Scenario A is Good, while Scenario B is *Not Bad*. But because Scenario B's condition is "Not Bad," it is not disadvantaged by the lack of pleasure.
* **Sum Total**: Non-existence has an absolute advantage regarding pain, and suffers no genuine disadvantage regarding pleasure:
$$\text{Non-existence strictly dominates Existence in all cases.}$$

## 2. Four Common-Sense Intuitions Supporting the Asymmetry

Benatar argues that his formal matrix is not an eccentric theoretical novelty, but the only framework that can coherently explain four universally held moral convictions:

1. **The Asymmetry of Procreative Duties**:
   * We have a powerful moral obligation **not** to create a child who we know will endure a life of agonizing suffering, congenital torture, and misery.
   * However, we have **no corresponding moral duty** to create a child who we expect will live an exceptionally happy, prosperous life. Creating happy people is at best supererogatory; avoiding suffering is a strict moral obligation.
2. **Prospective Beneficence**:
   * It makes perfect sense to say of an unconceived person that deciding not to bring them into existence is done "for their sake" (sparing them cancer, grief, and death).
   * Yet it is nonsensical to say we must bring a child into existence "for their own sake," because prior to conception there is no subject awaiting the gift of life.
3. **The Grief of Retrospective Assessment**:
   * When looking back upon a catastrophic life full of tragedy, sorrow, and disease, we often lament: *"It would have been better had they never been born."*
   * We never look at a barren, unpopulated valley or uninhabited planet and grieve for the absence of people who could have been enjoying themselves there.
4. **The Asymmetry of Global Extinction**:
   * We mourn disasters that cause immense, catastrophic suffering to living humans.
   * However, if human reproduction were to cease peacefully through universal voluntary choice, the resulting state of a depopulated Earth would not be bad: there would be zero suffering, and nobody left behind to suffer from the lack of human pleasures.

## 3. The Quality of Life Illusion (The Pollyanna Principle)

A common objection to Benatar's thesis is that most living people claim to enjoy their lives, reporting high subjective happiness. Benatar refutes this by deploying cognitive psychology:

* **The Pollyanna Principle (Optimism Bias)**: Human beings are biologically hardwired by evolutionary natural selection to overestimate their well-being and remember positive events far more vividly than negative ones. Organisms that accurately assessed the horrific statistics of disease, physical vulnerability, and mortality would be less likely to reproduce.
* **Adaptation and Hedonic Treadmills**: Humans rapidly adapt to chronic degradation, comparing themselves only to immediate peers rather than objective standards of flourishing.
* **The Asymmetry of Physical Experience**:
  * The most intense physical pleasures (such as sexual orgasm or quenching thirst) are ephemeral, lasting seconds or minutes.
  * The most severe physical pains (kidney stones, bone cancer, third-degree burns, chronic clinical depression) can persist for months, years, or decades.
  * You can easily imagine tortures so severe that no amount of past or future pleasure could ever compensate for them; there is no pleasure so intense that one would willingly endure five minutes of medieval torture to experience it.

## 4. Critical Objections & Responses

```
                           THE AXIOLOGICAL DISPUTE
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│     BENATAR'S ASYMMETRY (2006)       │  │      THE SYMMETRY DEFENSE            │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤  ├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Absence of pain = Good             │  │ • Value requires an existing subject │
│ • Absence of pleasure = Not Bad      │  │ • If absence of pleasure isn't bad   │
│ • Non-existence dominates existence  │  │   because no one is deprived, then   │
│ • Conclusion: Antinatalism           │  │   absence of pain isn't good!        │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘  └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **The Incoherence Objection (Parfit / McMahan)**:
   Critics argue that Benatar makes an illicit double-standard in the use of the term "Good." If the absence of pain in Scenario B is "Good," it must be good *for someone*. But in Scenario B, there is no person to enjoy that good. If the absence of a subject neutralizes the badness of lacking pleasure, it must equally neutralize the goodness of lacking pain.
2. **Benatar's Counter**: Benatar clarifies that his evaluations are made **impersonally**: we evaluate states of affairs. An empty room containing no suffering is objectively better than a room containing a tortured child, regardless of whether a third party is present to witness the empty room.

## 5. Significance in Phil Chat Lore

The Asymmetry of Pleasure and Pain is the primary theoretical weapon deployed against utilitarian optimists on the server:

* **The Pleasure Box Refutation**: When [[jere|Jere]] proposed [[The Pleasure Box]]—an artificial container experiencing infinite cosmic utils to mathematically outweigh all human misery combined—critics invoked Benatar's asymmetry: suffering is an absolute negative weight that cannot be morally offset by manufacturing surplus pleasure for an artificial entity.
* **The Spawnpill Debate**: In debates over whether starting conditions allow genuine ethical flourishing ([[Spawnpill]]), pessimistic chatters cite the asymmetry to argue that existence itself is an unchosen deficit from which human agents spend their entire lives attempting to recover.
