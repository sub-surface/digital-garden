# The Teletransporter

> **Derek Parfit**, *Reasons and Persons* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1984)[^parfit-rp-ed].

<div className="dropcap">

First introduced in Derek Parfit's 1984 masterpiece *Reasons and Persons*, **The Teletransporter Paradox** is the quintessential thought experiment of contemporary analytic metaphysics of personal identity. Designed to challenge our deeply ingrained "Ego Theory" of the self, the puzzle examines whether an individual survives when their physical body is scanned, vaporized, and atomically reconstructed across space. Parfit demonstrates that when teletransportation goes awry, our ordinary concept of personal identity collapses into unresolvable contradictions, proving his radical thesis: **Identity is not what matters in survival**.

</div>

## I. The Two Teletransportation Cases

```
                           CASE 1: SIMPLE TELETRANSPORTATION
   [EARTH]                                                                [MARS]
┌────────────┐     DESTRUCTIVE SCAN      DATA BEAM       ATOMIC REBUILD┌────────────┐
│ PERSON (A) ├─────────────────────────►~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~►──────────────►│ PERSON (B) │
└────────────┘   (Vaporized instantly)     (Light speed)  (New atoms)  └────────────┘
                              Does Person A survive as Person B?
```

### Case 1: Simple Teletransportation
You enter a teletransporter booth on Earth to travel to Mars. When you press the button:
1. An ultra-precise scanner records the exact physical, chemical, and subatomic configuration of every molecule and synaptic connection in your brain and body.
2. The scanner simultaneously and painlessly vaporizes your original body on Earth.
3. The structural data is beamed at the speed of light to Mars.
4. A receiver on Mars synthesizes new carbon, water, and organic matter into an exact molecular replica: someone who wakes up with all your memories, character traits, beliefs, and intentions.

Most physicalists and reductionists initially judge that this is a rapid and convenient form of travel: Person B on Mars is identical to Person A on Earth.

```
                           CASE 2: THE BRANCH-LINE CASE
   [EARTH]
┌────────────┐     NON-DESTRUCTIVE SCAN  DATA BEAM       ATOMIC REBUILD┌────────────┐
│ PERSON (A) ├─────────────────────────►~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~►──────────────►│ PERSON (B) │ [MARS]
└─────┬──────┘   (Body remains intact!)    (Light speed)  (New atoms)  └────────────┘
      │
      ▼
"Cardiac failure in two days."
                              Which one is really YOU?
```

### Case 2: The Branch-Line Paradox
Technology improves: the Earth scanner no longer needs to destroy your physical body to read its molecular blueprint. You step into the booth on Earth and press the button:
* The scanner reads your data and beams it to Mars, where Replica B walks out into the Martian colony with your exact memories and life-plans.
* Meanwhile, you step out of the scanner on Earth. The technician informs you: *"The scan was successful, but the equipment has damaged your cardiac tissue. You will die of painless heart failure in two days. But you shouldn't worry—your replica on Mars is flourishing!"*

Here our standard intuitions break down:
* If you are on Earth, watching your replica on a video feed, you do not expect to experience his sensations. When you die in two days, your conscious perspective on Earth ends.
* If Replica B on Mars was "you" in Case 1, how can the mere *continued survival* of your organic body on Earth render Replica B a "mere copy" in Case 2? How can a fatal cardiac condition on Earth retroactively alter the metaphysical identity of a human being on Mars?

## II. The Theories of Personal Identity

To analyze the paradox, philosophers deploy three competing frameworks:

| Theory | Criterion of Persistence | Judgment on Case 1 | Judgment on Case 2 |
|--------|--------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **The Somatic / Biological View** (Animalism) | Biological continuity of the animal organism (*Homo sapiens*) | **Death** (Organism was vaporized; B is a newly created twin) | **Survival on Earth** (You are the dying animal on Earth; B is a distinct organism) |
| **The Psychological Continuity View** (Lockean) | Memory chains, overlapping character traits, and intentions | **Survival** (B is psychologically continuous with A) | **Identity Failure** (One person cannot be identical to two distinct individuals) |
| **Parfit's Reductionist Bundle View** | There is no underlying Cartesian Ego; only casual streams of experiences (**Relation R**) | **Survival with R** (As good as ordinary survival) | **Fission** (Both branches possess Relation R; identity is an arbitrary label) |

---

## III. Parfit's Radical Conclusion: Relation R

Parfit resolves the paradox by severing **survival** from **numerical identity**:

$$\text{Identity is a 1-to-1 relation that cannot hold in degrees: you are either 100\% identical or not.}$$
$$\text{Survival (Relation R) is a matter of degree and can branch into multiple streams.}$$

### 1. Relation R: Psychological Connectedness and Continuity
* **Psychological Connectedness**: The holding of particular direct psychological connections (remembering yesterday's breakfast, executing a plan formed an hour ago).
* **Psychological Continuity**: The holding of overlapping chains of strong connectedness over time.
* **The Claim**: In both Case 1 and Case 2, Replica B has **Relation R with any cause** to Person A. Because what we actually care about in prospective survival is the continuation of our psychological projects, memories, and consciousness, *Replica B has everything that matters in survival*.

### 2. The Fission Dilemma
Suppose your brain's two hemispheres are surgically separated and transplanted into two distinct donor bodies (Person L and Person R), each waking up with your complete memories and personality:
1. Did you survive as Person L? (Arbitrary: why L and not R?)
2. Did you survive as Person R? (Arbitrary: why R and not L?)
3. Did you die? (Absurd: how can having *two* flourishing survivors be equivalent to death?)
4. Are you both L and R? (Logically impossible: L and R can later live separate lives and diverge).

Parfit's answer: **You do not survive as either in terms of identity, yet neither is death**. You have branched. To ask "Which one is *really* me?" is an empty, unanswerable question—like asking whether a club that splits into two factions is "the very same club."

---

## IV. Ethical Implications

Parfit's dismantling of the substantial self carries profound ethical consequences:
* **Diminished Egocentrism**: If the future self is merely someone psychologically continuous with my present state, the boundary between "my future self" and "other people" is a matter of degree rather than metaphysical absolute.
* **Weakening of Self-Interest**: Extreme egoism loses its rational foundation. Prudence toward one's distant future self becomes structurally identical to altruism toward strangers.
* **Consolation regarding Death**: Death is not the abrupt destruction of an indivisible soul, but merely the gradual attenuation of psychological connections.
