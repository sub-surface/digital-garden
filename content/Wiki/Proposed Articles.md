# Proposed Articles Backlog

<div className="dropcap">

This backlog establishes the comprehensive long-term expansion roadmap for the Philchat Wiki. Modeled on the scope of academic compendia, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, and Wikipedia's *Outline of Philosophy*, *List of important publications in philosophy*, and *List of philosophical problems*, it systematically maps canonical thinkers, theoretical movements, foundational thought experiments, analytical puzzles, and historical treatises targeted for research, formalization, and drafting.

</div>

Entries marked with an asterisk are actively prioritized for the immediate drafting phases. Contributors are invited to claim entries via the [[Submit|submission interface]] or the editor workbench.

---

## 1. Pre-Socratic & Early Global Thinkers

* **[[Thales of Miletus]]*** — First Milesian naturalist; water as arche and the origin of non-mythological cosmology.
* **[[Anaximander]]*** — The Apeiron (the Boundless), cosmic justice, and early evolutionary biological speculations.
* **[[Anaximenes]]** — Aer as primary substance; rarefaction and condensation as mechanistic transformations.
* **[[Heraclitus of Ephesus]]*** — The Logos, the unity of opposites, and perpetual becoming (*panta rhei*).
* **[[Parmenides of Elea]]*** — The Way of Truth (*Aletheia*), Eleatic monism, and the logical deduction of unchanging Being.
* **[[Zeno of Elea]]*** — Paradoxes of motion, space, and plurality defending Parmenidean monism.
* **[[Pythagoras and Early Pythagoreanism]]** — Mathematical ontology, the harmony of the spheres, and the transmigration of souls.
* **[[Empedocles]]** — The four cosmic roots (earth, air, fire, water) mediated by Love and Strife.
* **[[Anaxagoras]]** — Panspermia, infinite divisibility of matter, and Mind (*Nous*) as organizing principle.
* **[[Democritus and Leucippus]]*** — Mechanistic atomism, the void (*kenon*), and primary versus secondary qualities.
* **[[Xenophanes of Colophon]]** — Critique of anthropomorphic polytheism, early epistemological fallibilism, and pantheistic monotheism.
* **[[Yajnavalkya]]*** — Major Upanishadic sage of the *Brihadaranyaka*; neti-neti dialectic, Brahman, and Atman.
* **[[Uddalaka Aruni]]** — Naturalistic Upanishadic teacher; formulation of *Tat Tvam Asi* ("That Thou Art").
* **[[Laozi]]*** — The *Daodejing*, the nameless Dao, *wu wei*, and the political philosophy of non-coercive governance.
* **[[Kongzi]] (Confucius)*** — The *Analects*, the cultivation of benevolence (*ren*), ritual propriety (*li*), and rectification of names (*zhengming*).
* **[[Mozi]]*** — Early utilitarianism, meritocracy, and universal impartial care (*jian'ai*).
* **[[Zarathustra]]** — The Gathas, cosmic dualism between Asha (Truth) and Druj (Falsehood), and moral responsibility.

## 2. Classical & Hellenistic Philosophers

* **[[Socrates]]*** — Elenchus (Socratic method), the priority of definition, intellectualism, and virtue as knowledge.
* **[[Plato]]*** — Theory of Forms, the tripartite soul, the Allegory of the Cave, and justice in the *Republic*.
* **[[Aristotle]]*** — Hylomorphism, the four causes, teleology, virtue ethics (*eudaimonia*), and categorical logic.
* **[[Epicurus]]*** — Atomistic materialism, ataraxia (untroubledness), aponia (absence of pain), and critique of religious superstition.
* **[[Diogenes of Sinope]]** — Radical Cynicism, cosmopolitanism, shamelessness (*anaideia*), and rejection of arbitrary social convention.
* **[[Zeno of Citium]]** — Founder of Stoicism; virtue as the sole good, living in accordance with Nature, and universal logos.
* **[[Chrysippus of Soli]]** — Systematic architect of Stoic logic, propositional calculus, and compatibilist determinism.
* **[[Pyrrho of Elis]]*** — Pyrrhonian skepticism, epoche (suspension of judgment), and the pursuit of tranquility (*ataraxia*).
* **[[Sextus Empiricus]]*** — *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*; the Five Tropes of Agrippa, and skeptical critique of dogmatic criteria.
* **[[Lucretius]]** — *De Rerum Natura*; poetic exposition of Epicurean physics, atomism, and mortality of the soul.
* **[[Seneca the Younger]]** — Stoic moral epistles, the management of anger (*De Ira*), the shortness of life, and constancy of the sage.
* **[[Epictetus]]*** — *Discourses* and *Enchiridion*; the dichotomy of control, moral prohairesis, and inner freedom under duress.
* **[[Marcus Aurelius]]*** — *Meditations*; Stoic imperial self-examination, cosmopolitan duty, and providence.
* **[[Plotinus]]*** — Neoplatonism, the transcendent One, Nous (Divine Intellect), World Soul, and emanation.
* **[[Hypatia of Alexandria]]** — Neoplatonist mathematics, astronomy, and late Alexandrian philosophical commentary.

## 3. Medieval, Islamic Golden Age & Scholastic Thinkers

* **[[Augustine of Hippo]]*** — The nature of time, original sin, illumination theory, and the City of God versus City of Man.
* **[[Boethius]]** — *The Consolation of Philosophy*; Lady Philosophy, the problem of divine foreknowledge and human free will.
* **[[Al-Kindi]]** — First Arab philosopher; transmission of Greek thought into Islamic metaphysics.
* **[[Al-Farabi]]** — Political philosophy of the virtuous city (*al-Madina al-Fadila*) and synthesis of Plato and Aristotle.
* **[[Ibn Sina]] (Avicenna)*** — The Flying Man thought experiment, essence versus existence, and proof of the Truthful (*Burhan al-Siddiqin*).
* **[[Al-Ghazali]]*** — *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* (*Tahafut*), occasionalism, and epistemic skepticism regarding causality.
* **[[Ibn Rushd]] (Averroes)*** — *The Incoherence of the Incoherence*, defense of philosophy in Islamic jurisprudence, and active intellect.
* **[[Ibn Arabi]]** — *Wahdat al-Wujud* (The Unity of Being), imaginal realm (*alam al-mithal*), and mystical metaphysics.
* **[[Mulla Sadra]]** — Transcendent theosophy, primacy of existence (*asalat al-wujud*), and substantial motion (*al-haraka al-jawhariyya*).
* **[[Ibn Khaldun]]*** — *Muqaddimah*; philosophy of history, cyclical dynastic sociology, and social cohesion (*asabiyyah*).
* **[[Moses Maimonides]]*** — *The Guide for the Perplexed*, negative theology, and reconciliation of Aristotelianism with Torah.
* **[[Anselm of Canterbury]]*** — The Ontological Argument for the existence of God in the *Proslogion*.
* **[[Peter Abelard]]** — Nominalist conceptualism, intentionalist ethics (*Scito Te Ipsum*), and dialectical method in *Sic et Non*.
* **[[Thomas Aquinas]]*** — *Summa Theologiae*, the Five Ways (*Quinque Viae*), analogical predication, and natural law.
* **[[Duns Scotus]]** — The univocity of being, haecceity (thisness), and voluntarism.
* **[[William of Ockham]]*** — Nominalism, razor of parsimony, and separation of faith from philosophical demonstration.

## 4. Renaissance & Early Modern Philosophers

* **[[Niccolò Machiavelli]]*** — *The Prince*, *Discourses on Livy*; realism in statecraft, virtue (*virtù*) versus fortune (*fortuna*).
* **[[Michel de Montaigne]]*** — *Essays*; Pyrrhonian revival, skeptical self-examination, and the motto *"Que sais-je?"*.
* **[[Francis Bacon]]** — The inductive method, *Novum Organum*, and critique of the Idols of the Mind.
* **[[Thomas Hobbes]]*** — *Leviathan*, state of nature, psychological egoism, and the sovereign social contract.
* **[[René Descartes]]*** — Methodological doubt, *cogito ergo sum*, substance dualism, and the clear and distinct criterion.
* **[[Blaise Pascal]]*** — Pascal's Wager, the reasons of the heart, and existential critique of human vanity.
* **[[Baruch Spinoza]]** — Substance monism (*Deus sive Natura*), geometric demonstration, parallelism, and conatus.
* **[[John Locke]]*** — *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, tabula rasa, representative realism, and natural property rights.
* **[[Nicolas Malebranche]]** — Occasionalism; vision of all things in God and denial of creaturely causal efficacy.
* **[[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz]]*** — Monadology, the principle of sufficient reason, pre-established harmony, and the best of all possible worlds.
* **[[George Berkeley]]** — Immaterialism (*esse est percipi*), subjective idealism, and critique of abstract ideas.
* **[[David Hume]]*** — *A Treatise of Human Nature*, the problem of induction, bundle theory of self, and the is-ought gap.
* **[[Jean-Jacques Rousseau]]*** — The state of nature, the social contract, the general will (*volonté générale*), and amour-propre.
* **[[Mary Wollstonecraft]]*** — *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman*; rational egalitarianism, feminist republicanism, and critique of sensibility.
* **[[Immanuel Kant]]*** — *Critique of Pure Reason*, transcendental idealism, synthetic a priori judgments, and the categorical imperative.

## 5. 19th Century, Post-Kantian & Industrial Modernity

* **[[J.G. Fichte]]** — Absolute idealism, the self-positing Ego (*Ich*), and the practical basis of theoretical reason.
* **[[F.W.J. Schelling]]** — Philosophy of nature (*Naturphilosophie*), identity philosophy, and art as the organ of philosophy.
* **[[G.W.F. Hegel]]*** — *Phenomenology of Spirit*, speculative dialectic, master-slave dialectic, and Absolute Spirit.
* **[[Arthur Schopenhauer]]*** — *The World as Will and Representation*, pessimistic metaphysics, and aesthetic contemplation as escape.
* **[[Max Stirner]]** — *The Ego and Its Own*; radical conscious egoism, nominalism, and the critique of ideological "spooks."
* **[[Karl Marx]]*** — Historical materialism, dialectical critique of political economy, commodity fetishism, and alienation.
* **[[Friedrich Engels]]** — Dialectics of nature, origin of the family, private property, and scientific socialism.
* **[[John Stuart Mill]]*** — *Utilitarianism*, *On Liberty*, the harm principle, and qualitative distinctions in pleasure.
* **[[Jeremy Bentham]]** — Felicific calculus, panopticon architecture, legal positivism, and act utilitarianism.
* **[[Friedrich Nietzsche]]*** — *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, will to power, eternal recurrence, genealogy of morals, and the death of God.
* **[[Charles Sanders Peirce]]*** — Semiotics (sign, object, interpretant), fallibilism, pragmatism, and abduction.
* **[[William James]]*** — Pragmatic theory of truth, radical empiricism, the stream of consciousness, and the will to believe.
* **[[Wilhelm Dilthey]]** — Geisteswissenschaften (human sciences), hermeneutics, and the distinction between explanation and understanding.
* **[[Henri Bergson]]*** — *Creative Evolution*, *Time and Free Will*; qualitative duration (*la durée*), intuition, and *élan vital*.

## 6. Existentialism, Absurdism & Philosophical Pessimism

* **[[Albert Camus]]*** — *The Myth of Sisyphus*, *The Rebel*, *The Stranger*; the confrontation between human meaning-seeking and cosmic silence, revolt, physical and philosophical suicide.
* **[[Jean-Paul Sartre]]*** — *Being and Nothingness*, *Existentialism is a Humanism*; radical freedom, bad faith (*mauvaise foi*), being-for-itself vs being-in-itself, and the Look (*le regard*).
* **[[Simone de Beauvoir]]*** — *The Second Sex*, *The Ethics of Ambiguity*; situated existential freedom, patriarchal objectification ("one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman").
* **[[Søren Kierkegaard]]*** — *Fear and Trembling*, *The Sickness unto Death*; the leap of faith, teleological suspension of the ethical, anxiety (*angst*), and stages on life's way.
* **[[Karl Jaspers]]*** — *Philosophy of Existence*; boundary situations (*Grenzsituationen*), the Encompassing (*das Umgreifende*), and existential communication.
* **[[Martin Buber]]*** — *I and Thou* (*Ich und Du*); dialogical philosophy, the I-Thou encounter versus the instrumental I-It relation.
* **[[Gabriel Marcel]]** — Christian existentialism, being versus having, mystery versus problem, and creative fidelity.
* **[[Lev Shestov]]** — *Athens and Jerusalem*; radical fideism, revolt against rational necessity, and the critique of Spinoza and Husserl.
* **[[Nikolai Berdyaev]]** — Christian personalism, existential freedom, creativity, and the tragedy of objectification.
* **[[E.M. Cioran]]*** — *On the Heights of Despair*, *The Trouble with Being Born*; aphoristic nihilism, lyrical pessimism, insomnia, and the burden of consciousness.
* **[[Peter Wessel Zapffe]]*** — *The Last Messiah* (1933); evolutionary over-adaptation of human consciousness, tragic existentialism, and four defense mechanisms (isolation, anchoring, distraction, sublimation).
* **[[Philipp Mainländer]]** — *The Philosophy of Redemption* (1876); radical metaphysical pessimism, entropy as the ongoing suicide of God.
* **[[David Benatar]]*** — *Better Never to Have Been*; the axiological asymmetry between pain and pleasure, and rigorous philanthropic anti-natalism.
* **[[Miguel de Unamuno]]** — *The Tragic Sense of Life*; conflict between heart and reason, the hunger for personal immortality, and Quixotic faith.

## 7. 20th & 21st Century Analytic Philosophers

* **[[Gottlob Frege]]*** — *Begriffsschrift*, *Foundations of Arithmetic*, logicism, and sense (*Sinn*) versus reference (*Bedeutung*).
* **[[Bertrand Russell]]*** — Theory of descriptions, Russell's paradox, logical atomism, and the *Principia Mathematica*.
* **[[G.E. Moore]]** — *Principia Ethica*, the naturalistic fallacy, open-question argument, and defense of common sense.
* **[[Ludwig Wittgenstein]]*** — *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (picture theory) versus *Philosophical Investigations* (language-games, meaning-as-use).
* **[[Rudolf Carnap]]** — Logical positivism, *The Logical Structure of the World* (*Aufbau*), verificationism, and linguistic frameworks.
* **[[Karl Popper]]*** — *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*, falsificationism, problem of demarcation, and critique of historicism.
* **[[W.V.O. Quine]]*** — "Two Dogmas of Empiricism", web of belief, confirmation holism, and indeterminacy of translation.
* **[[P.F. Strawson]]** — Descriptive versus revisionary metaphysics, presupposition, and reactive attitudes in moral responsibility.
* **[[J.L. Austin]]** — *How to Do Things with Words*, speech act theory, and performative utterances.
* **[[G.E.M. Anscombe]]*** — *Intention*, modern virtue ethics revival, and anti-consequentialist critique.
* **[[Wilfrid Sellars]]*** — "Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind", critique of the Myth of the Given, and the space of reasons.
* **[[Donald Davidson]]*** — Radical interpretation, anomalous monism, principle of charity, and causal theory of action.
* **[[Hilary Putnam]]*** — Semantic externalism (Twin Earth), functionalism, brain-in-a-vat skepticism, and pragmatic realism.
* **[[Saul Kripke]]*** — *Naming and Necessity*, causal theory of reference, rigid designators, necessary a posteriori, and Kripkenstein rule-following paradox.
* **[[David Lewis]]*** — Modal realism (*On the Plurality of Worlds*), counterfactual analysis of causation, and Humean supervenience.
* **[[Thomas Nagel]]*** — "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?", subjective character of experience, moral luck, and the view from nowhere.
* **[[Derek Parfit]]*** — *Reasons and Persons*, reductionist personal identity, teletransportation paradox, and the non-identity problem.
* **[[Bernard Williams]]*** — Critique of utilitarianism, moral luck, internal versus external reasons, and ethical skepticism.
* **[[David Chalmers]]*** — *The Conscious Mind*, the Hard Problem of consciousness, philosophical zombies, and naturalistic dualism.
* **[[Timothy Williamson]]*** — Knowledge-first epistemology (*Knowledge and Its Limits*), epistemicism regarding vagueness, and modal logic.
* **[[Robert Brandom]]** — *Making It Explicit*; normative inferentialism, sapience as scorekeeping in the space of reasons.
* **[[John McDowell]]** — *Mind and World*; second nature, open-ended perceptual openness, and quietist therapy.

## 8. 20th & 21st Century Continental, Critical & Post-Structuralist Philosophers

* **[[Edmund Husserl]]*** — *Logical Investigations*, *Ideas I*, intentionality, and phenomenological epoche.
* **[[Martin Heidegger]]*** — *Being and Time*, fundamental ontology, Dasein, being-in-the-world, thrownness, and critique of technology.
* **[[Maurice Merleau-Ponty]]*** — *Phenomenology of Perception*, the lived body (*corps propre*), motor intentionality, and the chiasm.
* **[[Walter Benjamin]]*** — *The Arcades Project*, "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction", messianic time, and allegory.
* **[[Theodor Adorno]]*** — *Negative Dialectics*, *Dialectic of Enlightenment* (with Horkheimer), and critique of the culture industry.
* **[[Hannah Arendt]]*** — *The Human Condition*, *Eichmann in Jerusalem*; the banality of evil, the vita activa (labor, work, action), and totalitarianism.
* **[[Emmanuel Levinas]]*** — *Totality and Infinity*, the ethical face-to-face encounter, and ethics as first philosophy.
* **[[Hans-Georg Gadamer]]** — *Truth and Method*; philosophical hermeneutics, prejudice as condition of understanding, and fusion of horizons (*Horizontverschmelzung*).
* **[[Georges Bataille]]*** — *The Accursed Share*; general economy, non-productive expenditure, eroticism, and sovereignty.
* **[[Jacques Lacan]]*** — *Écrits*; the return to Freud, the Mirror Stage, the Symbolic/Imaginary/Real triad, and *objet petit a*.
* **[[Michel Foucault]]*** — *Discipline and Punish*, *The History of Sexuality*, archaeology of knowledge, genealogy, and biopolitics.
* **[[Guy Debord]]*** — *The Society of the Spectacle*, Situationist International, detournement, psychogeography, and alienation.
* **[[Jacques Derrida]]*** — *Of Grammatology*, deconstruction, différance, trace, and critique of the metaphysics of presence.
* **[[Jean-François Lyotard]]** — *The Postmodern Condition*, incredulity toward metanarratives, and the differend.
* **[[Jean Baudrillard]]*** — *Simulacra and Simulation*, hyperreality, the precession of simulacra, and symbolic exchange.
* **[[Gilles Deleuze]]** — *Difference and Repetition*, *The Logic of Sense*, immanence, transcendental empiricism, and rhizomes (with Guattari).
* **[[Alain Badiou]]** — *Being and Event*; set-theoretical ontology (Zermelo-Fraenkel), the Event, and four truth procedures (art, love, politics, science).
* **[[Giorgio Agamben]]*** — *Homo Sacer*; bare life (*zoē*), sovereign exception, the camp as biopolitical paradigm of the modern.
* **[[Slavoj Žižek]]** — *The Sublime Object of Ideology*, Lacanian psychoanalysis, Hegelian dialectic, and ideology critique.
* **[[Byung-Chul Han]]*** — *The Burnout Society*, *Psychopolitics*, neoliberal self-exploitation, digital panopticism, and the expulsion of the other.
* **[[Bernard Stiegler]]*** — *Technics and Time*, technogenesis, tertiary retention, grammatization, and systemic proletarianization of the mind.
* **[[Donna Haraway]]*** — *A Cyborg Manifesto*, *Staying with the Trouble*, situated knowledges, companion species, and the Chthulucene.
* **[[Catherine Malabou]]*** — *The Future of Hegel*, *Ontology of the Accident*, destructive plasticity, and synthesis of neurology and psychoanalysis.
* **[[Quentin Meillassoux]]*** — *After Finitude*, critique of correlationism, ancestrality, and the radical hyper-contingency of physical laws.
* **[[Yuk Hui]]*** — *The Question Concerning Technology in China*, *Recursivity and Contingency*, cosmotechnics, and pluralizing technological ontology.
* **[[Ray Brassier]]*** — *Nihil Unbound*; extinction-pathos, eliminative realism, and the unbinding of Sellarsian normativity from phenomenological vitalism.
* **[[Reza Negarestani]]** — *Cyclonopedia*, *Intelligence and Spirit*; petropolitics, regional ontology, and computational Sellarsian rationalism.

## 9. Classical, Medieval & Modern Eastern Philosophers

* **[[Siddhartha Gautama]] (The Buddha)*** — The Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, dependent origination (*pratityasamutpada*), and non-self (*anatta*).
* **[[Mahavira]]** — 24th Tirthankara of Jainism; absolute non-violence (*ahimsa*), asceticism, and many-sidedness of reality (*anekantavada*).
* **[[Zhuangzi]]*** — The butterfly dream, transformation of things (*wuhua*), fasting of the mind, and perspectivism in Daoist philosophy.
* **[[Mengzi]] (Mencius)** — Original goodness of human nature (*xing shan*), the four ethical sprouts (*siduan*), and benevolent government.
* **[[Xunzi]]** — Acquired nature (*xing e*), the necessity of ritual teachers and deliberate conscious artifice (*wei*).
* **[[Han Feizi]]*** — Legalism (*Fajia*); political realism, the two handles (punishment and reward), and administrative technique (*shu*).
* **[[Nagarjuna]]*** — *Mulamadhyamakakarika*, the dialectic of emptiness (*sunyata*), and the two truths doctrine.
* **[[Vasubandhu]]** — Yogacara phenomenology, consciousness-only (*vijnaptimatra*), and the storehouse consciousness (*alayavijnana*).
* **[[Dignaga & Dharmakirti]]** — Buddhist pramāna school; perception (*pratyaksa*) and inference (*anumana*) as exclusive instruments of valid cognition.
* **[[Shankara]]*** — Advaita Vedanta non-dualism, illusion (*maya*), superimposition (*adhyasa*), and liberation through knowledge.
* **[[Ramanuja]]** — Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism), devotional theism, and individual souls as modes of God.
* **[[Madhva]]** — Dvaita Vedanta; radical dualism and five eternal distinctions (*pancha-bheda*) between God, souls, and matter.
* **[[Abhinavagupta]]** — Kashmir Shaivism; non-dual Tantric metaphysics of universal consciousness (*Prakasha-Vimarsha*) and aesthetic rasa theory.
* **[[Zhu Xi]]** — Neo-Confucian synthesis of Principle (*li*) and Vital Force (*qi*), and the investigation of things (*gewu*).
* **[[Wang Yangming]]*** — Neo-Confucian unity of knowledge and action (*zhixing heyi*) and innate moral awareness (*liangzhi*).
* **[[Dogen]]*** — Soto Zen philosophy, being-time (*uji*), the practice of zazen, and non-duality of practice and enlightenment.
* **[[Kitaro Nishida]]*** — Kyoto School; *An Inquiry into the Good*, logic of place (*basho*), and Absolute Nothingness (*Zettai-mu*).
* **[[Keiji Nishitani]]** — *Religion and Nothingness*; overcoming European nihilism through Zen emptiness (*sunyata*).
* **[[B.R. Ambedkar]]*** — Dalit emancipation, social justice, critique of caste, and rationalist socially engaged Buddhism (*Navayana*).

## 10. Aesthetics & Philosophy of Art

* **[[Aesthetic Formalism]]** — Clive Bell and Clement Greenberg; significant form, aesthetic emotion, and the autonomy of medium-specific art.
* **[[The Institutional Theory of Art]]*** — George Dickie and Arthur Danto; the "Artworld" as the conferring framework of artwork status (the Brillo Box problem).
* **[[The Sublime vs The Beautiful]]*** — Longinus, Edmund Burke, and Immanuel Kant; pleasure in harmony versus cognitive awe, terror, and boundlessness.
* **[[The Paradox of Fiction]]*** — Colin Radford; how can rational subjects be genuinely moved to pity or fear by entities known not to exist?
* **[[The Paradox of Tragedy]]** — Aristotle to Hume and Schopenhauer; why do humans seek out and derive pleasure from depictions of catastrophic suffering?
* **[[The Intentional Fallacy]]** — W.K. Wimsatt and Monroe Beardsley; separating authorial design from the objective semantic evaluation of literary art.
* **[[The Death of the Author]]*** — Roland Barthes; birth of the reader through the disintegration of tyrannical biographical origin.
* **[[Make-Believe and Props]]** — Kendall Walton; fictional worlds as institutionalized games of make-believe mediated by representations as props.
* **[[Everyday and Environmental Aesthetics]]** — Allen Carlson and Yuriko Saito; extending aesthetic inquiry beyond gallery art to wilderness, agriculture, and ordinary artifacts.
* **[[The Apollonian and the Dionysian]]*** — Friedrich Nietzsche (*The Birth of Tragedy*); the dialectic between plastic, individuation-preserving form and ecstatic, primal musical dissolution.

## 11. Political, Social & Legal Philosophy

* **[[Social Contract Theory]]*** — The normative justification of political legitimacy from Hobbes' state of nature to Locke, Rousseau, and Rawls.
* **[[Classical Liberalism vs Egalitarianism]]** — Free market autonomy and negative liberties (Locke, Smith, Hayek) versus welfare guarantees and fair equality of opportunity (Rawls, Dworkin).
* **[[Republicanism & Non-Domination]]*** — Philip Pettit and Quentin Skinner; freedom not merely as non-interference, but as resilience against arbitrary power (*imperium*).
* **[[Communitarianism]]** — Michael Sandel, Alasdair MacIntyre, and Charles Taylor; embedded selfhood, constitutive communal attachments, and the critique of unencumbered liberalism.
* **[[Anarchism & Anti-Statism]]** — Godwin, Proudhon, Bakunin, and Kropotkin; mutual aid, rejection of coercive state monopoly, and federated voluntary association.
* **[[The Capabilities Approach]]*** — Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum; evaluating well-being through real freedoms to achieve valuable functioning states rather than mere GDP or resource distribution.
* **[[Critical Legal Studies & Legal Positivism]]** — H.L.A. Hart and Ronald Dworkin versus CLS and Critical Race Theory (Crenshaw, Bell); law as rule systems versus law as institutionalized class/racial hegemony.
* **[[The Sovereign Exception & Bare Life]]*** — Carl Schmitt ("sovereign is he who decides on the exception") and Giorgio Agamben (*Homo Sacer*); biopolitical exclusion from legal protection.
* **[[Epistemic Injustice]]*** — Miranda Fricker; testimonial injustice (deflation of credibility due to identity prejudice) and hermeneutical injustice (collective interpretive gaps disadvantageous to marginalized agents).
* **[[Feminist Care Ethics]]** — Carol Gilligan and Virginia Held; relational morality, interdependence, and contextual care challenging abstract rights-based deontology.

## 12. Schools & Movements

* **[[Absurdism]]*** — Albert Camus's philosophy of confrontation between human longing for inherent purpose and the cold, unreasonable silence of the cosmos.
* **[[Existentialism]]*** — Phenomenological inquiry into subjective human existence, radical freedom, choice, and authentic responsibility (Sartre, Beauvoir, Kierkegaard).
* **[[Pragmatism]]*** — Foundational theory of truth and inquiry spanning Peirce, James, Dewey, Rorty, and Brandom.
* **[[Continental Rationalism]]*** — Epistemological confidence in innate ideas and deductive demonstration (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz).
* **[[British Empiricism]]*** — Sensory origin of ideas and limits of synthetic knowledge (Locke, Berkeley, Hume).
* **[[German Idealism]]*** — Systematic post-Kantian metaphysics (Kant, Fichte, Schelling, Hegel).
* **[[Phenomenology]]*** — Methodological investigation of structures of conscious experience (Husserl, Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty).
* **[[Logical Positivism]]*** — Radical verificationism, elimination of metaphysics, and logical analysis of science (Vienna Circle).
* **[[Post-Structuralism]]*** — Deconstructive and differential critiques of stable meaning and humanism (Derrida, Foucault, Deleuze).
* **[[Utilitarianism]]*** — Consequentialist normative theories maximizing aggregate welfare or preference satisfaction.
* **[[Deontology]]*** — Duty-based moral systems grounded in universal rational laws and respect for persons.
* **[[Virtue Ethics]]*** — Character-based ethics prioritizing flourishing (*eudaimonia*), moral habituation, and practical wisdom.
* **[[Stoicism]]*** — Ancient Hellenistic and Roman school identifying virtue as the sole good, living in accordance with Nature, and cultivating tranquility (*apatheia*).
* **[[Epicureanism]]** — Atomistic hedonism pursuing static pleasures (*katastematic*), absence of physical pain (*aponia*), and mental equanimity (*ataraxia*).
* **[[Pyrrhonian Skepticism]]*** — Sextus Empiricus's system of suspension of judgment (*epoche*) through equipping equal and opposing arguments (*isostheneia*).
* **[[Cynicism]]** — Classical movement rejecting conventional luxury, wealth, and polite hypocrisy in favor of natural virtue, physical austerity, and shameless speech (*parrhesia*).
* **[[Madhyamaka]]*** — Buddhist middle way philosophy establishing the radical emptiness of inherent existence (*svabhava*).
* **[[Advaita Vedanta]]*** — Non-dualist metaphysical system affirming the ultimate identity of Atman and Brahman.
* **[[Accelerationism]]*** — Techno-capitalist runaway teleoplexy and the dissolution of human agency (Land, CCRU).
* **[[Ordinary Language Philosophy]]** — Oxford linguistic approach resolving philosophical puzzles through natural usage (Ryle, Austin, late Wittgenstein).
* **[[Critical Theory]]*** — Frankfurt School interdisciplinary critique of ideology, instrumental reason, authoritarianism, and cultural commodification.
* **[[Philosophical Pessimism]]*** — Schopenhauer, Mainländer, Zapffe, and Cioran; the view that suffering fundamentally outweighs pleasure in conscious reality.

## 13. Foundational Concepts, Paradoxes, Thought Experiments & Razors

* **[[The Absurd]]*** — Albert Camus's fundamental philosophical friction: the human cry for meaning met with the stubborn irrationality and silence of the world.
* **[[The Absurd Hero]]*** — Sisyphus, Don Juan, the Actor, the Conqueror; individuals who embrace futile struggle without metaphysical hope or despair.
* **[[Philosophical Suicide vs Revolt]]*** — Camus's critique of existentialist leaps into faith (Kierkegaard, Chestov, Husserl) as evasions of the absurd; revolt as refusal to surrender.
* **[[Existence Precedes Essence]]*** — Sartre's core existential axiom: human beings first exist, encounter themselves in the world, and only afterward define what they are through action.
* **[[Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi)]]*** — Sartre's phenomenon of lying to oneself, fleeing radical freedom by treating oneself as a fixed object or passive role (the café waiter).
* **[[The Look (Le Regard)]]*** — Sartre's account of intersubjective objectification: another's gaze reduces my sovereign subjecthood to an object in their world ("Hell is other people").
* **[[The Leap of Faith]]*** — Kierkegaard's movement of faith (*Springet*): embracing paradox and divine absurdity beyond the bounds of rational universal ethics.
* **[[Teleological Suspension of the Ethical]]*** — Kierkegaard's analysis of Abraham and Isaac in *Fear and Trembling*: when divine command supersedes universal ethical duty.
* **[[Boundary Situations (Grenzsituationen)]]*** — Karl Jaspers; inescapable limits (death, suffering, guilt, struggle) that shatter everyday complacency and awaken authentic existential awareness.
* **[[The Four Defense Mechanisms of Consciousness]]*** — Peter Wessel Zapffe (*The Last Messiah*); how human minds suppress existential horror through isolation, anchoring, distraction, and sublimation.
* **[[The Asymmetry of Pleasure and Pain]]*** — David Benatar; why the presence of pain is bad while the absence of pain is good even if no one exists to experience that absence.
* **[[The Ship of Theseus]]*** — Persistence, material constitution, mereology, and criterion of numerical identity over time.
* **[[The Chinese Room]]*** — John Searle's demonstration that syntax is neither constitutive of nor sufficient for semantic intentionality.
* **[[Marys Room]]*** — Frank Jackson's knowledge argument against physicalism and the reality of subjective phenomenal qualia.
* **[[The Trolley Problem]]*** — Philippa Foot and Judith Jarvis Thomson; deontological side-constraints versus utilitarian optimization in moral killing.
* **[[The Experience Machine]]*** — Robert Nozick's challenge to psychological and ethical hedonism: would you plug in forever?
* **[[The Veil of Ignorance]]*** — John Rawls' original position as an impartial heuristic for determining principles of justice.
* **[[Philosophical Zombies]]*** — David Chalmers; physical indistinguishability without phenomenal consciousness as a conceivability counter to physicalism.
* **[[The Hard Problem of Consciousness]]*** — Explaining how and why physical neurobiological processes generate subjective inner phenomenal experience.
* **[[The Problem of Induction]]*** — David Hume; the absence of deductive or non-circular inductive justification for predictions based on past regularity.
* **[[Foundationalism vs Coherentism]]*** — Epistemic regress problem and rival structural architectures of empirical justification.
* **[[The Mind-Body Problem]]*** — The ontological relation between conscious subjective mental states and extended physical neurobiology.
* **[[Substance Monism]]*** — The metaphysical doctrine of a singular, all-encompassing substance (Spinoza, Parmenides).
* **[[Humes Guillotine]]*** — The logical gap between factual descriptive premises (*is*) and evaluative normative conclusions (*ought*).
* **[[Occams Razor]]*** — Ontological parsimony, syntactic elegance, and the Bayesian penalty for superfluous entities.
* **[[Hanlons Razor]]*** — Bounded rationality, error attribution, and the heuristic of cognitive over malfeasant explanations.
* **[[Hitchens Razor]]*** — Epistemic evidentialism: what can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence.
* **[[Grices Maxims]]*** — The Cooperative Principle, conversational implicature, and pragmatics of communicative intent.
* **[[The Principle of Charity]]*** — Radical interpretation, maximizing interlocutor rationality, and epistemic steelmanning.
* **[[Poppers Demarcation Criterion]]*** — Empirical falsifiability via *modus tollens* distinguishing genuine scientific hypotheses from pseudoscience.
* **[[The Problem of Evil]]*** — Logical and evidential challenges reconciling evil and suffering with an omnipotent, omnibenevolent deity.
* **[[Moral Realism vs Anti-Realism]]** — The ontological status and truth-aptness of ethical statements.
* **[[Compatibilism vs Incompatibilism]]** — Free will, causal determinism, moral responsibility, and alternative possibilities.
* **[[Sorites Paradox (Paradox of the Heap)]]*** — Vagueness, epistemicism, and the breakdown of classical logic under incremental millimeter changes.
* **[[Menos Paradox]]*** — The inquiry dilemma: how can you search for what you do not know, and if you already know it, why search?
* **[[Goodmans New Riddle of Induction]]*** — Nelson Goodman; projectable versus non-projectable predicates, and the grue/bleen puzzle.
* **[[Hempelels Raven Paradox]]*** — Paradox of confirmation: does observing a white shoe increase the probability that all ravens are black?
* **[[Swampman]]** — Donald Davidson's thought experiment on historical etiology, causal origins, and the possession of mental content.
* **[[Twin Earth]]** — Hilary Putnam's demonstration that "meanings just ain't in the head" (XYZ vs H2O).
* **[[Buridans Ass]]** — Radical symmetry in rational decision-making: starvation between two equal bales of hay.
* **[[The Euthyphro Dilemma]]*** — Is what is morally good loved by God because it is good, or is it good because it is loved by God?
* **[[Münchhausens Trilemma (Agrippas Trilemma)]]*** — The regress problem of epistemic justification: circularity, infinite regress, or dogmatic axiomatic halting.
* **[[Sleeping Beauty Problem]]** — Self-locating belief, Bayesian probability, and the clash between Halfer and Thirder interpretations.
* **[[Moores Paradox]]** — Pragmatic contradiction: asserting *"It is raining, but I do not believe it."*
* **[[Russells Paradox]]** — Naive set theory contradiction: the set of all sets that do not contain themselves ($R \in R \iff R \notin R$).
* **[[Newcombs Problem]]** — Decision theory paradox pitting Dominance (Two-boxing) against Expected Utility Maximization (One-boxing).
* **[[The Teletransporter]]** — Derek Parfit's branching replica thought puzzle: survival, psychological continuity, and personal identity.
* **[[The Violinist]]** — Judith Jarvis Thomson's defense of abortion: bodily autonomy versus the right to life of an attached musical prodigy.
* **[[McTaggarts Paradox of Time]]*** — The unreality of time demonstrated through the incompatibility of dynamic tensed A-series and static tenseless B-series.
* **[[The Problem of Universals]]*** — Realism (Platonic/Aristotelian), Nominalism, and Trope Theory regarding shared properties and abstract types.

## 14. Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence & Computation

* **[[Strong AI vs Weak AI]]*** — Can computational systems possess genuine subjective intentionality, or are they formal behavioral simulators?
* **[[The Alignment Problem]]*** — Ensuring artificial superintelligence adheres to human values; the Orthogonality Thesis and Instrumental Convergence (Bostrom, Yudkowsky).
* **[[Computational Functionalism]]** — The thesis that mental states are realized in computational operations independent of biological substrate.
* **[[The Extended Mind Thesis]]*** — Andy Clark and David Chalmers; technology and notebooks literally constituting cognitive processes.
* **[[The Simulation Hypothesis]]** — Nick Bostrom's trilemma arguing that we are almost certainly living in an ancestor computer simulation.
* **[[The Lucas-Penrose Argument]]** — Gödelian anti-computationalism; claiming human mathematical insight cannot be reduced to a Turing machine.
* **[[Artificial Qualia and Machine Sentience]]** — Ethical obligations to synthetic intelligences; criteria for attributing phenomenal consciousness to digital networks.
* **[[The Symbol Grounding Problem]]*** — Stevan Harnad; how arbitrary computational symbols acquire intrinsic meaning connected to the external world.
* **[[The Frame Problem in AI]]** — Daniel Dennett and Jerry Fodor; how an artificial agent can compute what remains invariant without checking every fact in the universe.
* **[[The Teleoplexy of the Machine God]]** — Cybernetic runaway feedback and the theological dimensions of emergent synthetic superintelligence.
* **[[Algorithmic Governmentality & Surveillance Capitalism]]*** — Shoshana Zuboff, Antoinette Rouvroy, and Thomas Berns; statistical profiling, pre-emptive nudging, and automated governance.

* **[[4E Cognition]]*** — The paradigm that cognition is not brain-bound computation, but Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, and Extended.
* **[[Predictive Processing & Free Energy]]** — Karl Friston and Andy Clark; the brain as a hierarchical prediction engine minimizing variational surprise.
* **[[Biosemiotics & Teleosemantics]]** — Ruth Millikan; how natural biological functions generate genuine semantic information and intentionality.

* **[[Animal Minds and Interspecies Ethics]]** — Cephalopod consciousness, mammalian pain, and Peter Singer's challenge to speciesism.
* **[[Neuroexistentialism]]*** — Owen Flanagan and Gregg Caruso; the contemporary existential crisis provoked by neuroscience's mechanistic reduction of selfhood, free will, and moral responsibility.

## 16. Philosophy of Science, Mathematics & Formal Logic

* **[[The Demarcation Problem]]*** — Distinguishing empirical science from pseudoscience and non-science (Popper, Lakatos, Kuhn).
* **[[Scientific Realism vs Constructive Empiricism]]*** — Do scientific theories reveal unobservable reality, or are they merely empirically adequate tools (Bas van Fraassen)?
* **[[Structural Realism]]** — John Worrall and James Ladyman; saving realism across paradigm shifts by preserving mathematical relational structures rather than object ontologies.
* **[[The Duhem-Quine Thesis]]*** — Underdetermination of theory by observation; hypotheses cannot be tested in absolute isolation from auxiliary assumptions.
* **[[Paradigm Shifts & Incommensurability]]*** — Thomas Kuhn (*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*); normal science, anomalies, crises, and gestalt-shift paradigm revolutions.
* **[[Epistemological Anarchism]]** — Paul Feyerabend (*Against Method*); critique of single scientific methods, advancing *"anything goes"* in discovery.
* **[[The No Miracles Argument]]** — Hilary Putnam; realism as the only philosophy that doesn't make the empirical success of science a miracle.
* **[[Pessimistic Meta-Induction]]** — Larry Laudan; past empirical theories were largely false, suggesting current successful theories will also be superseded.
* **[[Gödels Incompleteness Theorems]]*** — Kurt Gödel (1931); any consistent formal axiomatic system capable of arithmetic contains true statements that cannot be proven within the system.
* **[[Tarskis Semantic Theory of Truth]]*** — Alfred Tarski; Convention T, satisfaction, and avoiding liar paradoxes through meta-linguistic hierarchies.
