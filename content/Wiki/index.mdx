<Tape label="THE ARCHITECTURE OF PHILOSOPHY" />

Philosophy on the wiki is organized across four foundational domains, connecting historical systems to contemporary analytic and continental disputes:

* **Metaphysics & Epistemology**: Being, substance, mind-brain identity, skepticism, and theories of justification. See [[Substance Monism]], [[The Mind-Body Problem]], [[The Hard Problem of Consciousness]], [[Foundationalism vs Coherentism]], and [[The Problem of Induction]].
* **Value Theory & Normative Ethics**: The criteria of right action, moral psychology, and political justice. See [[Deontology]], [[Utilitarianism]], [[Virtue Ethics]], [[The Veil of Ignorance]], and [[Humes Guillotine|Hume's Guillotine]].
* **Logic, Language & Mind**: Meaning, truth, reference, syntax versus semantics, and artificial agency. See [[Warranted Assertibility]], [[The Chinese Room]], [[Marys Room]], and [[Philosophical Zombies]].
* **Historical Lineages & World Traditions**: The continuous dialectical progression from ancient naturalism to contemporary critical theory. See [[A Map of Philosophy|The Complete Map of Philosophy]].

<Tape label="CANONICAL TEXT ANALYSES" />

Masterwork textual blueprints mapping proposition architectures, axioms, and translation notes:

* **[[Logic of Sense (Deleuze)|Gilles Deleuze: The Logic of Sense (1969)]]** — Complete 34-series proposition architecture: denotation, manifestation, signification, and the surface topology of sense.
* **[[Ethics (Spinoza)|Baruch Spinoza: Ethics, Demonstrated in Geometrical Order (1677)]]** — Axioms, definitions, propositions, substance monism, parallelism, and conatus.
* **[[Critique of Pure Reason (Kant)|Immanuel Kant: Critique of Pure Reason (1781/1787)]]** — The Copernican revolution, synthetic a priori, transcendental aesthetic, categories, and antinomies.
* **[[Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (Wittgenstein)|Ludwig Wittgenstein: Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921)]]** — The seven propositions, the picture theory of meaning, saying vs. showing, and silence.
* **[[Being and Time (Heidegger)|Martin Heidegger: Being and Time (1927)]]** — The question of Being, Dasein, ready-to-hand equipmentality, anxiety, and temporality.
* **[[A Theory of Justice (Rawls)|John Rawls: A Theory of Justice (1971)]]** — The original position, veil of ignorance, the two principles of justice, and reflective equilibrium.
