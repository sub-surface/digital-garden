# Philosopher: Søren Kierkegaard

> "Anxiety is the dizziness of freedom."

## Quick Info
* **Dates:** 1813 – 1855
* **Nationality:** Danish
* **Philosophical Tradition:** 19th-Century Continental Philosophy / Christian Existentialism
* **Areas of Specialization (AOS):** Philosophical Psychology, Philosophy of Religion, Christian Ethics
* **Notable For:** *Fear and Trembling*, *Either/Or*, *The Concept of Anxiety*, *The Sickness unto Death*, *Concluding Unscientific Postscript*

## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Søren Aabye Kierkegaard was a Danish philosopher, theologian, poet, and religious author widely acclaimed as the foundational pioneer of modern existentialism. Raised in Copenhagen under the austere, melancholic piety of his wealthy merchant father, Kierkegaard studied theology and philosophy at the University of Copenhagen. In 1840, he broke his engagement to his beloved Regine Olsen—a traumatic biographical event that catalyzed a torrential outpouring of brilliant pseudonymous literature exploring romantic sacrifice, faith, and ethical duty. Fiercely opposing the dominant Hegelian rationalism of the European academy and the complacent bourgeois Christianity of the Danish State Church (*Folkekirken*), Kierkegaard spent his personal fortune publishing scathing tracts and psychological masterpieces under diverse pseudonyms. He collapsed in the streets of Copenhagen in October 1855, dying a month later at the age of forty-two.

</div>

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### 1. Truth as Subjectivity: The Anti-Hegelian Revolt
Kierkegaard's most radical philosophical intervention was directed against [[G.W.F. Hegel|G.W.F. Hegel's]] totalizing speculative system:
* In Hegel's dialectic, individual human existence is swallowed up by the grand, objective unfolding of World Spirit (*Weltgeist*).
* In *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846), writing as Johannes Climacus, Kierkegaard retorted:
  $$\text{"Truth is Subjectivity."} \quad (\text{CUP } 189)$$
* Objective reflection turns the human subject into an accidental spectator interested in abstract historical results. But for an existing individual who must make concrete, agonizing life choices, objective certainty is an illusion. Genuine religious and existential truth is an **objective uncertainty held fast in the most passionate personal inwardness**.[^kierkegaard-cup]

### 2. The Stages on Life's Way (*Stadier paa Livets Vei*)
In *Either/Or* (1843) and subsequent works, Kierkegaard structured human existence into three qualitative spheres:

```
                          THE THREE SPHERES OF EXISTENCE
         ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
         │                    3. THE RELIGIOUS SPHERE                   │
         │   • The Leap of Faith (*Springet*)                           │
         │   • Knight of Faith; direct relation to the Divine Paradox   │
         │   • Teleological suspension of the ethical                   │
         └──────────────────────────────▲───────────────────────────────┘
                                        │  LEAP OF FAITH
         ┌──────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
         │                     2. THE ETHICAL SPHERE                    │
         │   • Judge William; universal moral duty, marriage, civic law │
         │   • Commitment and constancy over time                       │
         │   • Collapses when confronted with Guilt and Sin             │
         └──────────────────────────────▲───────────────────────────────┘
                                        │  DESPAIR / CHOICE
         ┌──────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
         │                    1. THE AESTHETIC SPHERE                   │
         │   • The Seducer; pursuit of sensory pleasure and novelty     │
         │   • Immediate immersion, irony, and dread of boredom         │
         │   • Inevitably collapses into Melancholy and Despair         │
         └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3. The Concept of Anxiety (*Angst*)
In *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844), Kierkegaard provided the first systematic phenomenological anatomy of existential dread:
* **Fear vs. Anxiety**: Fear is directed at a specific, determinate object in the external world (a venomous spider, an executioner).
* **Anxiety is directed at Nothing (*das Nichts*)**: It is the sheer awareness of one's own radical freedom and infinite possibility. Looking into an abyss, a person experiences not merely the fear of falling, but the terrifying realization that they possess the physical freedom to throw themselves over. Anxiety is the "dizziness of freedom."

### 4. The Leap of Faith & The Knight of Faith
In *Fear and Trembling* (1843), writing as Johannes de silentio, Kierkegaard analyzes the biblical narrative of Abraham commanded by God to sacrifice his son Isaac on Mount Moriah:
* From the universal ethical perspective (the Hegelian moral law), Abraham is a common murderer.
* Abraham cannot justify his act to society through rational language; he must undergo a **Teleological Suspension of the Ethical**—stepping outside the universal moral law to stand in an absolute, solitary relation to the Absolute God.
* Abraham is the supreme **Knight of Faith**: unlike the "Knight of Infinite Resignation" who surrenders all earthly joy, the Knight of Faith gives up Isaac while simultaneously believing *by virtue of the absurd* that he will receive Isaac back in this earthly life.

### 5. Indirect Communication & Pseudonymous Authorship
To prevent readers from adopting his ideas as an intellectual dogma, Kierkegaard constructed an intricate theater of pseudonyms (Johannes de silentio, Victor Eremita, Johannes Climacus, Anti-Climacus). Each pseudonym embodied a distinct psychological perspective. Through **indirect communication**, Kierkegaard sought not to impart theoretical doctrine, but to shock the reader into personal, autonomous self-examination.

## Literary Form & The Rhetoric of Irony: The Pseudonymous Carnival

Kierkegaard is widely recognized as one of the supreme stylists of European literature, for whom philosophy was inseparable from ironic masquerade:
* **The Polyphonic Pseudonyms**: Rather than authoring systematic academic textbooks, Kierkegaard staged an elaborate polyphonic carnival of fictional personas (Victor Eremita, Johannes de silentio, Johannes the Seducer, Johannes Climacus, Anti-Climacus). Each pseudonym speaks from within a distinct existential horizon, possessing its own idiosyncratic prose cadence, intellectual vanity, and moral blind spots. Kierkegaard refused to endorse any of them as his direct mouthpiece.
* **Indirect Communication (*Den Indirekte Meddelelse*)**: While factual information (such as geometry or municipal timetables) can be communicated directly, **existential truth** cannot be taught like school curriculum:
  > *"Direct communication requires certainty; but certainty is the impossible for someone who is in the process of becoming."*
  To awaken a reader who is complacently lulled by state religion or Hegelian abstraction, the writer must deploy **irony**, humor, and aesthetic seduction—deceiving the reader into the truth by forcing them to confront their own radical inwardness.
* **The Aphoristic Blade (*Diapsalmata*)**: In the opening pages of *Either/Or*, Kierkegaard introduced the voice of the tragic aesthete through razor-sharp aphorisms that anticipate Kafka and Cioran:
  > *"What is a poet? An unhappy person who conceals profound anguish in his heart, but whose lips are so formed that as sighs and cries pass over them, they sound like beautiful music... And people crowd around the poet and say: 'Sing for us again soon!'"*

## Major Works

1. *Either/Or* (*Enten-Eller*, 1843) — The foundational split between the aesthetic and ethical spheres of existence.
2. *Fear and Trembling* (*Frygt og Bæven*, 1843) — Landmark meditation on Abraham, faith, and the teleological suspension of the ethical.
3. *The Concept of Anxiety* (*Begrebet Angest*, 1844) — Psychological treatise diagnosing anxiety as the dizziness of freedom.
4. *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (*Afsluttende uvidenskabelig Efterskrift*, 1846) — Climax of the pseudonymous authorship establishing truth as subjectivity.
5. *The Sickness unto Death* (*Sygdommen til Døden*, 1849) — Masterpiece analyzing despair as the structural disorientation of the self.
