## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Gilles Deleuze was one of the most inventive and influential figures in late-20th-century French philosophy. From early monographs reconstructing the history of philosophy through heterodox lenses (Spinoza, Nietzsche, Bergson, Hume, Kant) to the monumental ontology of *Difference and Repetition* (1968) and his decades-long collaboration with psychoanalyst Félix Guattari (*Anti-Oedipus*, *A Thousand Plateaus*), Deleuze sought to free philosophy from the "dogmatic image of thought" grounded in identity, representation, and transcendence.

</div>

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### Difference in Itself
Deleuze attacks the Aristotelian tradition that subordinates difference to identity (where difference is merely the comparison between already-constituted species under a genus). Instead, he conceives of *pure difference* as primary, generative, and productive. Being is univocal: it is said of all things in a single, identical sense, but what it affirms is radical, divergent difference.

### The Virtual and the Actual
Rejecting the opposition between the possible and the real, Deleuze distinguishes between the *virtual* (which is fully real, differential, and unactualized) and the *actual* (the empirical, individuated states that crystallize through processes of differenciation).

### Rhizome and Lines of Flight (with Guattari)
In *A Thousand Plateaus*, Deleuze and Guattari propose the *rhizome* as an open, non-hierarchical network with multiple entryways and exits, contrasting it with the centralized, binary "arborescent" model of Western thought. Within assemblages of desire and power, *lines of flight* (*lignes de fuite*) represent vectors of escape, deterritorialization, and creative mutation.

## Significance in Phil Chat

Deleuze is the ubiquitous mascot and recurring debate token of Phil Chat. Whenever a debate between analytic pragmatism and continental materialism reaches an impasse, participants invariably summon Deleuze:

- During the four-day Warranted Assertibility bout between [[Quigley]] and Hugh, Hugh replied to Quigley's demands for correspondence criteria by typing *"you have not demonstrated"* seventeen times in a row and attaching three identical pictures of Deleuze.
