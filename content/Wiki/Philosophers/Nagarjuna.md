# Philosopher: Nagarjuna

> "Whatever is dependently co-arisen, that is explained to be emptiness. That, being a dependent designation, is itself the middle way."

## Quick Info
* **Dates:** c. 150 – 250 CE
* **Nationality:** Indian (Satavahana era)
* **Philosophical Tradition:** Classical Indian Philosophy / Mahayana Buddhism
* **Areas of Specialization (AOS):** Epistemology, Metaphysics, Buddhist Dialectics
* **Notable For:** *Mulamadhyamakakarika*, founding the Madhyamaka school, systematic deconstruction of intrinsic nature (*svabhava*)

---

## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Nagarjuna was an Indian Buddhist philosopher and dialectician widely regarded as one of the greatest thinkers in Asian history, often referred to in the Mahayana tradition as "the Second Buddha." Born into a Brahmin family in southern India during the Satavahana dynasty, Nagarjuna served as abbot at Nalanda University according to Tibetan and Chinese biographical chronicles. Encountering the *Prajnaparamita* (Perfection of Wisdom) sutras, he formulated a dialectical philosophy designed to rescue early Buddhist teaching from the scholastic reifications of Abhidharma dogmatists. His masterwork, the *Mulamadhyamakakarika* (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way), provided the philosophical foundation for Mahayana Buddhism throughout India, Tibet, China, and Japan.

</div>

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### 1. Emptiness (*Sunyata*) and the Refutation of Essence (*Svabhava*)
The target of Nagarjuna's critique is **Svabhava**—the philosophical concept of an independent, self-sufficient, immutable essence or intrinsic identity:
* In everyday life and rival philosophical schools (Nyaya, Sarvastivada), entities are assumed to possess an intrinsic nature that makes them what they are.
* Nagarjuna demonstrates that if an entity had an intrinsic nature, it could neither change, arise, nor interact with anything else (since having an independent nature means being uncaused and unconditioned).
* Therefore, all phenomena—physical objects, mental events, time, causality, the Buddha, and even Nirvana—are **empty** (*sunya*) of intrinsic nature.

### 2. Emptiness as Dependent Origination (*Pratityasamutpada*)
Nagarjuna famously identifies emptiness not as nihilistic nothingness, but as the universal interdependence of reality:
$$\text{Dependent Origination} \equiv \text{Emptiness of Inherent Nature} \equiv \text{The Middle Way}$$
Because everything is dependently originated (conditioned by causes, parts, and mental designation), everything is empty of standalone existence. Far from preventing things from working, emptiness is the very condition that allows change, life, cause, effect, and liberation to occur.

### 3. The Doctrine of Two Truths (*Satyadvaya*)
To refute the charge of nihilism, Nagarjuna distinguishes two levels of truth:
* **Conventional Truth (*Samvriti-Satya*)**: The pragmatic, transactional language of empirical life. Fire burns, chairs exist, moral actions carry consequences.
* **Ultimate Truth (*Paramartha-Satya*)**: The realization that at the fundamental level, no entity possesses intrinsic essence. Crucially, **emptiness itself is empty** (*sunyata-sunyata*)—it is not an absolute metaphysical substrate (like Brahman in Advaita Vedanta) lurking behind appearances, but a corrective conceptual therapy.[^nagarjuna-mmk]

### 4. The Method of Reductio (*Prasanga*) & The Tetralemma
Nagarjuna does not construct speculative metaphysical systems; he practices radical critical philosophy. Through the **Tetralemma** (*Catushkoti*), he systematically interrogates propositions across four truth possibilities:
1. $P$ is true.
2. $P$ is false.
3. $P$ is both true and false.
4. $P$ is neither true nor false.
By demonstrating that all four assertions involve unexamined presuppositions of *svabhava* that lead to self-contradiction, Nagarjuna induces cognitive stillness (*samatha*) and liberation from conceptual proliferation (*prapanca*).

## Major Works

1. *Mulamadhyamakakarika* (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way) — Foundational deconstruction of causality, motion, senses, and Nirvana.
2. *Vigrahavyavartani* (The Dispeller of Doubts) — Epistemological defense answering charges that asserting emptiness is self-refuting.
3. *Sunyatasaptati* (Seventy Verses on Emptiness) — Concise distillation of dependent origination.
4. *Ratnavali* (The Precious Garland) — Practical ethics, social philosophy, and political advice for kings.
