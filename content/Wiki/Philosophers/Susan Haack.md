# Philosopher: Susan Haack

> "Evidence is like a crossword puzzle: experiential evidence is like the clues, and already-filled-in entries are like reasons."

## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Susan Haack is Distinguished Professor of Humanities and Professor of Philosophy and Law at the University of Miami. Educated at Oxford and Cambridge, where she was supervised by David Pears and studied logic under Michael Dummett, Haack emerged in the 1990s as one of the most formidable voices in analytic epistemology. Deeply influenced by Charles Sanders Peirce, she carved out an intermediate position between rigid foundationalism and circular coherentism called *foundherentism*.

</div>

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### Foundherentism
In *Evidence and Inquiry* (1993), Haack identifies a false dilemma in traditional epistemology:
1. **Foundationalism:** Claims all justified beliefs trace back to non-inferential basic beliefs (the "foundation"), which faces difficulty explaining how sensory experience non-inferentially justifies propositional belief without falling into the "Myth of the Given."
2. **Coherentism:** Claims beliefs are justified solely by how well they cohere in a mutual web, which risks severing justification from the external world (a completely coherent fairy tale is still a fairy tale).

Haack's **foundherentism** allows experiential evidence to play a direct role in justifying belief (like foundationalism) while admitting that beliefs mutually support and reinforce one another without a strict one-way hierarchy (like coherentism).

### The Crossword Puzzle Metaphor
Haack illustrates foundherentism with a crossword puzzle:
- The **clues** correspond to experiential input from the senses.
- The **intersecting words** correspond to background beliefs and reasons.
- Whether a word entry is reasonable depends both on whether it fits the clue (experiential evidence) and whether it meshes with the surrounding letters already penciled in (coherence). The more interlocking words fit, the higher the justification.

### Defense of Inquiry
Haack vigorously defends science and truth against vulgar pragmatists (such as Richard Rorty) and fashionable literary deconstructionists who reduce truth to "what our peers let us get away with saying." She champions Peirce's *contrite fallibilism*: our beliefs may be mistaken, but genuine inquiry aims at objective truth.
