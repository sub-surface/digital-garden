# Philosopher: Immanuel Kant

> "Two things fill the mind with ever new and increasing admiration and reverence, the more often and steadily we reflect upon them: the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me."

## Quick Info
* **Dates:** 1724 – 1804

* **Areas of Specialization (AOS):** Epistemology, Metaphysics, Moral Philosophy
* **Notable For:** *Critique of Pure Reason*, *Critique of Practical Reason*, *Critique of the Power of Judgment*, the Categorical Imperative

## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Immanuel Kant was a German philosopher whose critical philosophy revolutionized Western thought by synthesizing [[Continental Rationalism]] and [[British Empiricism]]. Living his entire life in the Baltic port city of Königsberg in East Prussia, Kant served as a professor of logic and metaphysics at the University of Königsberg. Shocked out of his "dogmatic slumber" by David Hume's skeptical challenge to causation, Kant spent a decade in silent contemplation before publishing his monumental *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781). His "Copernican Revolution" demonstrated that the human mind does not passively conform to objects, but rather actively structures the objective conditions under which any experience is possible.

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### 1. The Copernican Revolution & Transcendental Idealism
Kant resolved the deadlock between rationalism and empiricism:
* **The Claim**: While all our knowledge begins *with* experience, it does not follow that it all arises *out of* experience.
* **Transcendental Aesthetic**: Space and time are not physical containers existing mind-independently, but **a priori forms of human sensible intuition**—the subjective spectacles through which the mind receives sensory data.
* **Transcendental Analytic**: Raw sensory intuitions are structured by twelve pure a priori **Categories of the Understanding** (such as Causality, Substance, Plurality, and Necessity). As Kant formulated:
  $$\text{Thoughts without content are empty; intuitions without concepts are blind.}$$
* **Phenomena versus Noumena**: We can achieve rigorous scientific knowledge of **phenomena** (objects as they appear to our structured cognitive faculties), but we can have zero knowledge of **noumena** (the "thing-in-itself", *Ding an sich*, as it exists unmediated by human sensibility).[^kant-cpr]

### 2. Synthetic *A Priori* Judgments
Prior to Kant, judgments were considered either:
* **Analytic A Priori**: Tautologies true by definition, uninformative about the world ($All\ bachelors\ are\ unmarried$).
* **Synthetic A Posteriori**: Contingent empirical observations informative about the world ($The\ cat\ is\ on\ the\ mat$).

Kant established a revolutionary third category: **Synthetic A Priori** judgments—claims that are universal, necessary, and known prior to experience, yet genuinely informative about reality (e.g., $7 + 5 = 12$, and *"every event must have a cause"*). This category provides the necessary foundation for mathematics and natural physics.

### 3. Deontological Ethics: The Categorical Imperative
In *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785), Kant derived an uncompromising moral law based entirely on rational autonomy:
* **Good Will**: The only thing good without qualification is a good will—acting out of pure duty for the sake of the moral law, not out of inclination, fear, or expected consequences.
* **The Formula of Universal Law**: *"Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."*[^kant-gmm]
* **The Formula of Humanity as an End in Itself**: *"Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."*

## Major Works

1. *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781 / 2nd ed. 1787) — Epistemology, space/time, categories, and limits of reason.
2. *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785) — Derivation of the Categorical Imperative.
3. *Critique of Practical Reason* (1788) — Moral autonomy, freedom, and the postulates of practical reason.
4. *Critique of the Power of Judgment* (1790) — Teleology and aesthetic judgment of the beautiful and the sublime.
5. *Toward Perpetual Peace* (1795) — Cosmopolitan political theory and republican federation of nations.
