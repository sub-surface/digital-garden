# Philosopher: Aristotle

> "All human beings by nature desire to know."

## Quick Info
* **Dates:** 384 – 322 BCE
* **Nationality:** Greek (Stagirite)
* **Philosophical Tradition:** Classical Antiquity / Aristotelianism
* **Areas of Specialization (AOS):** Metaphysics, Logic, Biology, Ethics
* **Notable For:** *Nicomachean Ethics*, *Metaphysics*, *Prior Analytics*, inventing formal deductive logic

## Biography Summary

<div className="dropcap">

Aristotle was a towering ancient Greek philosopher and polymath whose intellectual system encompassed logic, physics, biology, metaphysics, ethics, politics, and rhetoric. Born in Stagira in northern Greece, Aristotle studied under Plato at the Academy in Athens for twenty years before tutoring Alexander the Great in Macedonia. In 335 BCE, Aristotle returned to Athens to establish his own school, the **Lyceum** (the Peripatetic school). Diverging from Plato's transcendent Forms, Aristotle anchored philosophy in empirical observation of the natural world, constructing the first comprehensive taxonomy of animal species and the first formal system of deductive logic, which dominated Western and Islamic thought for two millennia.

</div>

## Key Philosophical Contributions

### 1. Hylomorphism & Substance
Rejecting Plato's separate intelligible realm, Aristotle argued that forms do not exist apart from particulars. In *Metaphysics*, he introduced **Hylomorphism**: physical objects are composites of **Matter** (*hyle*, the potential underlying substrate) and **Form** (*morphe*, the actualizing structure and essence that makes a thing what it is). A bronze statue is bronze (matter) sculpted into a statue (form). Primary substances (*ousiai*) are individual concrete entities—this particular horse or this particular man.

### 2. The Four Causes (*Aitia*)
To fully comprehend any natural entity or change, Aristotle argued that one must answer four fundamental causal questions:
1. **Material Cause**: That out of which a thing is made (e.g., the bronze of a statue).
2. **Formal Cause**: The structure, definition, or pattern of the thing (e.g., the shape of the statue).
3. **Efficient Cause**: The primary agent or force initiating the change (e.g., the sculptor wielding the chisel).
4. **Final Cause (*Telos*)**: The end, purpose, or goal for which the thing exists (e.g., to honor a deity). Nature is intrinsically teleological—everything develops toward its mature fulfillment.

### 3. Nicomachean Ethics: *Eudaimonia* & The Doctrine of the Mean
In the *Nicomachean Ethics*, Aristotle established virtue ethics:
* **The Teleological Human Good**: The ultimate human end is **Eudaimonia** (human flourishing or living well), defined through the **Function Argument**: the characteristic function of human beings is rational activity of the soul in accordance with virtue (*arete*).
* **The Doctrine of the Mean**: Moral virtues are stable character states situated between two extremes—a vice of excess and a vice of deficiency. For instance, **Courage** is the golden mean between Cowardice (deficiency) and Rashness (excess). Practical wisdom (*phronesis*) is required to discern the right action in particular situations.[^aristotle-ne]

### 4. Formal Syllogistic Logic (*The Organon*)
In the *Prior Analytics*, Aristotle pioneered the formal analysis of deductive validity through the **Categorical Syllogism**:
* Premise 1: All humans are mortal.
* Premise 2: Socrates is a human.
* Conclusion: Therefore, Socrates is mortal.
He formulated the fundamental laws of logic: the Law of Identity ($A = A$), the **Law of Non-Contradiction** (a statement cannot be both true and false at the same time in the same respect), and the Law of the Excluded Middle.

## PhilPapers Survey Profile

*Projected stance based on Aristotle's corpus:*

### Metaphysics & Mind
* **Abstract objects:** Moderate Realism / Immanent Realism (universals exist only instantiated in concrete particulars, *in re*; firmly rejecting Platonic transcendent Forms)
* **External world:** Direct Realism (perceptual faculties receive the sensible forms of mind-independent physical objects without matter)
* **Free will:** Voluntary Agency (*hekousion*; action originating within the agent under the guidance of deliberation, *bouleusis*)
* **Mind:** Hylomorphic Non-Reductionism (*De Anima*; the soul is the substantial form and primary actuality of an organic body possessing life potentially)
* **Personal identity:** Hylomorphic Composite of Form and Matter (individual personal survival ceases upon biological death, though active intellect, *nous poietikos*, may be transpersonal)
* **Time:** Relational (*Physics* IV; time is the number and measure of motion according to the before and after)

### Epistemology
* **Epistemic justification:** Demonstrative Foundationalism (*Posterior Analytics*; scientific demonstration rests upon indemonstrable first principles grasped by *nous*)
* **Knowledge:** Empirical Induction (*epagoge*) ascending from sensible particulars to universal definitions, completed by rational intellectual grasp
* **Truth:** Classical Correspondence (*Metaphysics* IV; truth is asserting of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not)

### Value Theory
* **Aesthetic value:** Objective Realism (*Poetics*; beauty consists in order, symmetry, and definite magnitude, producing catharsis)
* **Meta-ethics:** Ethical Naturalism / Teleological Realism (the human good is objectively determined by our species-defining function, *ergon*)
* **Moral judgment:** Cognitivism (practical wisdom, *phronesis*, delivers objective truths about virtuous action in concrete situations)
* **Moral motivation:** Internalism (moral habituation harmonizes appetitive desires with right reason, *orthos logos*)
* **Normative ethics:** Virtue Ethics (the doctrine of the mean, *mesotes*; *eudaimonia* as activity of soul in accord with complete virtue)
* **Political philosophy:** Civic Republicanism (*Politics*; the *polis* exists by nature and is prior to the individual; humanity is by nature a political animal, *zoon politikon*)

### Logic, Language, & Science
* **Logic:** Classical Syllogistic Deductive Logic (*Organon*; formulated the square of opposition and axiomatic term logic)
* **Science:** Teleological Realism (complete explanation requires the four causes: material, formal, efficient, and final)

### Metaphilosophy & Religion
* **Aim of philosophy:** Theoretical Contemplation (*theoria*; the divine exercise of philosophical contemplation is the highest form of human flourishing)
* **God:** Deism / The Prime Mover (*Metaphysics* XII; pure actuality, uncaused first cause, thought thinking itself, *noesis noeseos*)
* **Philosophical methods:** Surveying reputable opinions (*endoxa*), dialectical resolution of puzzles (*aporiai*), biological taxonomy and empirical dissection

## Major Works

1. *Nicomachean Ethics* (c. 340 BCE) — Systematic treatise on virtue, moral habituation, and eudaimonia.
2. *Metaphysics* (c. 335 BCE) — First philosophy: substance, hylomorphism, and the Unmoved Mover.
3. *Prior & Posterior Analytics* — Foundations of deductive syllogisms and scientific demonstration.
4. *De Anima* (On the Soul) — The hylomorphic account of vegetative, sensitive, and rational souls.
5. *Politics* — The zoon politikon (political animal) and classification of constitutional regimes.
