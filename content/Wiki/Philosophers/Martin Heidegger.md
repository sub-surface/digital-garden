# Martin Heidegger

<div className="dropcap">

**Martin Heidegger** (1889–1976) was a towering German philosopher whose 1927 masterwork, *Being and Time* (*Sein und Zeit*)[^heidegger-bt-ref], revolutionized twentieth-century continental philosophy, existentialism, hermeneutics, literary theory, and cognitive science. Student and successor to [[Edmund Husserl]] at the University of Freiburg, Heidegger transformed transcendental phenomenology into a profound existential **Fundamental Ontology**.

</div>

Heidegger argued that Western philosophy since Plato had fallen into the catastrophic **Oblivion of Being** (*Seinsvergessenheit*): confusing the ontic inquiry into specific entities (*Seiendes*, physical things, particles, minds) with the primordial ontological inquiry into **Being itself** (*Sein*—that by virtue of which any entity is revealed as existing at all).

---

## 1. Dasein and Being-in-the-World

To reopen the Question of Being (*die Seinsfrage*), Heidegger analyzes the unique entity for whom its own Being is an issue: the human being, designated by the ontological term ***Dasein*** (literally, "Being-there"):

$$\text{Dasein: The entity whose very Being is an issue for it.}$$

Heidegger rejects the Cartesian tradition of conceiving the human subject as an isolated, disembodied mind (*res cogitans*) gazing across an epistemic gap at an external physical world. Dasein is primordially **Being-in-the-World** (*In-der-Welt-sein*):
* We are always already immersed, involved, and practically engaged within a meaningful web of equipment, cultural practices, and projects before we ever step back to engage in detached scientific reflection.

```
                  THE CARTESIAN TRAP vs. HEIDEGGERIAN DASEIN
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
 │          Cartesian Epistemology      │          Heideggerian Dasein         │
 ├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Isolated subject (res cogitans)      │ Being-in-the-world (In-der-Welt-sein)│
 │ World is outside, doubted, proved    │ World is the pre-reflective clearing │
 │ Detached spectator gazing at objects │ Practical engagement using equipment │
 │ "I think, therefore I am"            │ "Care is the being of Dasein"        │
 └──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Equipment: Ready-to-Hand versus Present-at-Hand

In Chapter 3 of *Being and Time*, Heidegger demonstrates that our primary encounter with objects is practical and instrumental, rather than theoretical:

```
                      THE MODES OF ENGAGEMENT
              ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │           READY-TO-HAND                │
              │          (Zuhandenheit)                │
              │  - Hammering with a hammer in the shop │
              │  - Tool is "transparent" in action     │
              │  - We focus on the project (the chair) │
              └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                  │
                                  ▼  TOOL BREAKS OR GOES MISSING
              ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │          PRESENT-AT-HAND               │
              │          (Vorhandenheit)               │
              │  - The hammer head flies off the handle│
              │  - Tool becomes an obstinate object    │
              │  - We step back to inspect it: weight, │
              │    iron, wood (Theoretical Science)    │
              └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Ready-to-Hand** (*Zuhandenheit*): When a carpenter hammers, the hammer is not an object with objective physical properties (mass, chemical composition); it withdraws into fluent, transparent usage. Equipment belongs to a holistic "equipmental totality" (*Zeugganzes*), referencing other tools, materials, and purposes.
2. **Present-at-Hand** (*Vorhandenheit*): Only when the hammer breaks, goes missing, or gets in the way does it lose its ready-to-hand fluency. It stands before us as an isolated, detached physical object possessing brute properties.
* Traditional philosophy made the fatal error of treating the *present-at-hand* as primary, mistaking the detached scientific attitude for the foundational ground of human existence.

[^heidegger-bt-ref]: Martin Heidegger, *Being and Time*, translated by Joan Stambaugh, revised by Dennis J. Schmidt (Albany: State University of New York Press, 2010). See [[Bibliography#Heidegger-BT-1927]].

## 3. Thrownness, The "They", and Inauthenticity

Dasein does not choose where, when, or into which culture it is born. We find ourselves in **Thrownness** (*Geworfenheit*): delivered over into a concrete historical culture, language, and family situation with predetermined norms.

In everyday life, Dasein lives in **Inauthenticity** (*Uneigentlichkeit*), dominated by **The "They"** (*Das Man*):
* *"We enjoy ourselves and take part in recreation as one [the They] enjoys oneself; we read, see, and judge of literature and art as one sees and judges; likewise we shrink back from the 'great mass' as one shrinks back..."* (*SZ*, §27)
* The "They" relieves Dasein of personal responsibility, soothing existential dread through idle talk (*Gerede*), curiosity, and ambiguity.

## 4. Anxiety and Being-toward-Death (*Sein-zum-Tode*)

Authentic existence (*Eigentlichkeit*) is triggered when everyday absorption breaks down through **Anxiety** (*Angst*):
* Unlike fear (which has a specific, identifiable object in the world, such as a rabid dog or a fire), **Anxiety has no object**: its object is Being-in-the-world itself.
* In anxiety, the everyday world of social roles and trivial distractions loses its meaning; Dasein confronts its own radical freedom, finitude, and mortality.

This culminates in the realization that Dasein is **Being-toward-Death** (*Sein-zum-Tode*):
* Death is not a future biological event that happens to happen at the end of life; death is the constant, looming possibility of the impossibility of any existence at all.
* My death is non-relational: no one can die my death for me.
* By resolutely facing one's mortality in **Anticipatory Resoluteness** (*Vorlaufende Entschlossenheit*), Dasein strips away the comfortable illusions of the "They" and assumes authentic responsibility for its finite existence.

## 5. The Later Philosophy: The Turn and Technology (*Gestell*)

In his later thought after the 1930s (*Die Kehre* / The Turn), Heidegger shifted from human Dasein to the historical clearing of Being itself.

In his prophetic 1954 lecture *"The Question Concerning Technology"* (*Die Frage nach der Technik*)[^heidegger-tech-ref], Heidegger warned that modern essence of technology is not merely a collection of useful instruments, but a mode of revealing reality: **Enframing** (*Gestell*):
* Technology orders nature to reveal itself as **Standing-Reserve** (*Bestand*): raw material, energy, and supply to be stockpiled, processed, and maximized for efficiency.
* The Rhine river is no longer experienced as a sacred landscape or natural river; it is enframed as a water-power provider feeding electric turbines.
* The supreme danger is that human beings themselves become enframed as mere "human resources" within the cybernetic technosphere.

[^heidegger-tech-ref]: Martin Heidegger, *The Question Concerning Technology and Other Essays*, translated by William Lovitt (New York: Harper & Row, 1977).

## 6. Language, the Poetic Word, and the Fourfold (*Das Geviert*)

Following his Kehre, Heidegger turned from the analytic of Dasein to the historical disclosure of Being through language and art:

* **The Destruction of the History of Ontology (*Destruktion*)**: Heidegger emphasizes that *Destruktion* does not mean nihilistic demolition. It is the deconstructive dismantling of the hardened, unexamined conceptual categories of metaphysics (substance, subject, object, representation) in order to rediscover the original, unthought Greek encounter with Being as unconcealment (***aletheia***).
* **"Language is the House of Being"**: In his *Letter on Humanism* (1947)[^heidegger-humanism], Heidegger famously declared: *"Die Sprache ist das Haus des Seins. In ihrer Behausung wohnt der Mensch."* Language is not an instrumental tool manufactured by human animals to transmit thoughts. Language speaks through humanity (*Die Sprache spricht*); humans dwell poetically by listening to what language discloses.
* **Hölderlin and Poetic Naming**: In *Poetry, Language, Thought* (1950)[^heidegger-plt], Heidegger examines the poetry of Friedrich Hölderlin. The poet is the messenger who stands between mortals and gods, gathering the world into the **Fourfold** (***Das Geviert***)—Earth and Sky, Mortals and Divinities—manifested in the humble presence of a thing (a bridge spanning a river, an earthenware jug pouring wine).

---

## 7. Politics, Cybernetics, and the "End of Philosophy"

* **The Political Catastrophe**: In 1933, Heidegger joined the Nazi party and accepted the rectorate of the University of Freiburg, delivering his notorious *Rectorate Address*. While he resigned the rectorate after a year, the posthumous publication of his private *Black Notebooks* (*Schwarze Hefte*, 1931–1948)[^heidegger-sh] revealed an alarming ontological anti-Semitism that viewed world-historical events through a mythologized combat of Being against rootless modern technology. The question of how deeply this politics infects his ontology remains the most fiercely contested dispute in contemporary continental philosophy.
* **Cybernetics as the Completion of Metaphysics**: In his 1964 lecture *"The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking"*, Heidegger proclaimed that the 2,500-year cycle of Western philosophy had reached its end. Philosophy dissolves into the empirical sciences, whose unified planetary regime is **Cybernetics**—the totalized algorithmic steering, calculation, and information processing of nature and society. Thinking must step back from calculation to prepare a new beginning for Being.
