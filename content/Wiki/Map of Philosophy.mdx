## 1. Archaic Dawn: Pre-Socratic & Early World Thinkers

Systematic philosophy originates not in isolated ivory towers, but at the fringes of ancient trading networks and cosmological reassessments between the 8th and 5th centuries BCE—a period Karl Jaspers termed the "Axial Age."

### The Milesian Naturalists (Ionia)
In Miletus on the coast of Asia Minor, early thinkers broke with mythopoetic explanations of nature (*physis*), seeking a single underlying material principle (*arche*):
* **[[Thales of Miletus]]** (c. 624–548 BCE): Identified water as the fundamental substrate; famously predicted an eclipse and founded natural inquiry.
* **[[Anaximander]]** (c. 610–546 BCE): Rejected concrete elements in favor of the *Apeiron* (the Boundless or Indefinite), out of which opposing forces separate and balance through cosmic justice.
* **[[Anaximenes]]** (c. 586–526 BCE): Posited air as the primary substance, introducing condensation and rarefaction as mechanical processes generating the physical world.

### Being, Flux, and Eleatic Monism
* **[[Heraclitus of Ephesus]]** (c. 535–475 BCE): The philosopher of perpetual becoming. All things exist in tension (*polemos* as father of all), governed by an immanent ordering principle (*Logos*) and symbolized by living fire.
* **[[Parmenides of Elea]]** (c. 515–450 BCE): The founder of ontology. In *On Nature*, Parmenides deduced that Being (*to eon*) is ungenerated, indestructible, continuous, and unchanging. Non-being is unthinkable; change and multiplicity are mortal illusions.
* **[[Zeno of Elea]]** (c. 490–430 BCE): Defended Parmenidean monism through reductio ad absurdum paradoxes of motion and plurality (Achilles and the Tortoise, the Arrow).

### Pluralists and Atomists
* **[[Empedocles]]** (c. 494–434 BCE): Reconciled Parmenides and Heraclitus by positing four immutable roots (fire, air, water, earth) driven by two alternating cosmic forces: Love (*Philia*) and Strife (*Neikos*).
* **[[Anaxagoras]]** (c. 500–428 BCE): Maintained that "everything is in everything," set in motion by Cosmic Intellect (*Nous*).
* **[[Democritus]] and Leucippus** (5th c. BCE): Developed mechanistic atomism—reality consists exclusively of indivisible particles (*atoma*) colliding in the infinite void (*kenon*).

### Early Eastern and Trans-Eurasian Foundations
Parallel developments across Asia challenged ritual and cosmological orthodoxy:
* **Early Upanishadic Thinkers (India)**: In texts such as the *Brihadaranyaka* and *Chandogya Upanishads* (c. 800–500 BCE), sages such as **[[Yajnavalkya]]** and **[[Uddalaka Aruni]]** inquired into the ultimate reality (*Brahman*) and its identity with the innermost self (*Atman*), formulating early accounts of karma, rebirth, and liberation (*moksha*).
* **Early Daoism (China)**: Attributed to **[[Laozi]]** (author of the *Daodejing*, c. 6th–4th c. BCE), early Daoism posited the ineffable *Dao* (the Way) as the spontaneous, non-teleological origin of all things, advocating *wu wei* (effortless action or non-coercive responsiveness).
* **Early Confucian Humanism**: **[[Confucius|Kongzi]]** (551–479 BCE) in the *Analects* relocated ethical order from divine mandate to cultivation of virtue (*ren*, benevolence), ritual propriety (*li*), and filial responsibility (*xiao*).
* **Mohism**: Founded by **[[Mozi]]** (c. 470–391 BCE), pioneering early consequentialism, utilitarian social management, and universal impartial care (*jian'ai*).
* **Zoroastrian Dualism (Persia)**: Zarathustra's Gathas (c. 1000–600 BCE) established a rigorous cosmic and ethical dualism between truth/order (*Asha*) and falsehood/deceit (*Druj*), demanding ethical human agency in cosmic history.

## 2. Metaphysics & Ontology

Metaphysics examines the fundamental nature of reality, existence, and the categories of being.

### Key Problems & Domains
* **Substance and Attributes**: What is substance? Is substance composite or simple? Examined from Aristotle's primary substance to [[Baruch Spinoza|Spinoza's]] monism in [[Ethics (Spinoza)|*Ethics*]] and Leibniz's monadology.
* **Universals and Particulars**: Do abstract entities exist independently (Platonic Realism), as concepts in minds (Conceptualism), or merely as names (Nominalism)?
* **Modality and Possible Worlds**: What grounds necessity, possibility, and contingency? Debated between David Lewis's modal realism, Saul Kripke's model-theoretic essentialism, and deflationary fictionalism.
* **Time, Change, and Persistence**: A-theory (tensed, dynamic passage) versus B-theory (tenseless, block universe); endurantism versus perdurantism.
* **Personal Identity**: What makes a person the same across time? The psychological continuity thesis (Locke, Parfit in [[The Teletransporter]]) versus bodily animalism (Olson) and Buddhist non-self (*Anatman*).

## 3. Epistemology & Theory of Knowledge

Epistemology interrogates the nature, scope, sources, and justification of knowledge.

### The Problem of Justification
Since Plato's *Theaetetus*, knowledge has traditionally been analyzed as Justified True Belief (JTB). In 1963, Edmund Gettier demonstrated that JTB is insufficient:
1. **The Regress Problem**: How can a belief be justified without appealing to an infinite chain of justifications?
2. **Foundationalism**: Justification rests upon basic beliefs that require no further justification (Cartesian self-evident truths or empiricist perceptual givens).
3. **Coherentism**: Justification is a function of mutual coherence within an interlocking web of beliefs (Neurath's boat, Quine's web).
4. **Foundherentism**: [[Susan Haack|Susan Haack's]] synthesis, modeling justification on a crossword puzzle: experiential evidence anchors clues while intersecting entries provide mutual support.
5. **Pragmatist Epistemology**: [[John Dewey|John Dewey]] and [[Richard Rorty|Richard Rorty]] replace correspondence theories of truth with [[Warranted Assertibility]]—beliefs as instruments for problem-solving within human inquiry.
6. **Transcendental Epistemology**: Immanuel Kant in the [[Critique of Pure Reason (Kant)|*Critique of Pure Reason*]] establishes synthetic *a priori* judgments and the transcendental deduction of the categories.

## 4. Philosophy of Mind & Cognitive Science

The philosophy of mind addresses the ontology of mental phenomena, consciousness, and their relation to the physical substrate.

### The Mind-Body Dialectic
* **Cartesian Dualism**: The mind is an immaterial thinking thing (*res cogitans*); the body is extended matter (*res extensa*).
* **Identity Theory (Type Physicalism)**: Mental states are identical with physical brain states (Smart, Place, Armstrong).
* **Functionalism**: Mental states are defined by their functional and causal roles, not their physical implementation (Putnam, Fodor).
* **The Hard Problem of Consciousness**: Coined by David Chalmers, distinguishing between "easy problems" (mechanistic reporting of stimuli) and the question of *why* cognitive processing is accompanied by subjective experience (*qualia*).
* **Illusionism & Eliminative Materialism**: Dennett and the Churchlands argue that subjective qualia and folk psychology are cognitive illusions or obsolete explanatory models.

### Key Thought Experiments
* **[[Marys Room]]**: Frank Jackson's knowledge argument against physicalism—can complete physical knowledge capture the experience of red?
* **[[The Chinese Room]]**: John Searle's demonstration that syntactic symbol manipulation cannot produce semantic understanding.
* **[[Philosophical Zombies]]**: Chalmers' thought experiment probing whether an exact physical replica lacking subjective experience is metaphysically conceivable.
* **[[The Brain in a Vat]]**: Hilary Putnam's semantic externalist refutation of radical skepticism and Robert Nozick's truth-tracking epistemological model.
* **[[Molyneuxs Problem]]**: The question of cross-modal sensory transfer upon the restoration of sight, bridging rationalist, empiricist, and cognitive accounts.

## 5. Value Theory: Ethics & Meta-Ethics

Value theory encompasses normative ethics, meta-ethics, and applied dilemma analysis.

### Normative Frameworks
1. **Consequentialism & Utilitarianism**: The moral worth of an action is determined solely by its outcomes. Formulated by Jeremy Bentham (hedonistic calculus) and John Stuart Mill (qualitative utilitarianism). In contemporary channel lore, pushed to its speculative extreme in [[The Pleasure Box]].
2. **Deontological Ethics**: Actions are intrinsically right or wrong based on duty and moral law, independent of consequences. Exemplified by Immanuel Kant's Categorical Imperative in [[Movements/Deontology|Deontology]].
3. **Virtue Ethics**: Morality centers on character development and practical wisdom (*phronesis*) aiming at human flourishing (*eudaimonia*), from Aristotle to Philippa Foot and Alasdair MacIntyre.

### Meta-Ethics: The Nature of Moral Claims
* **Moral Realism**: Moral facts exist objectively and mind-independently.
* **Moral Anti-Realism & Error Theory**: J.L. Mackie's argument from queerness—moral judgments claim objectivity but are systematically false.
* **Expressivism & Non-Cognitivism**: Moral statements do not state facts but express affective attitudes (Ayer's "boo/hurrah" emotivism, Blackburn's quasi-realism).
* **[[Humes Guillotine]]**: David Hume's demonstration of the logical breach between descriptive factual premises (*is*) and prescriptive normative obligations (*ought*).

## 6. Political, Social & Legal Philosophy

Political philosophy analyzes the justification of state authority, distributive justice, rights, and liberty.

* **Social Contract Theory**: From the state of nature to civil society (Hobbes' Leviathan, Locke's natural property rights, Rousseau's general will).
* **Distributive Justice**: [[John Rawls|John Rawls]]' landmark formulation in [[A Theory of Justice (Rawls)|*A Theory of Justice*]] (1971) introduced the [[The Veil of Ignorance|Veil of Ignorance]] and the Difference Principle. Robert Nozick's libertarian counter (*Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, 1974) defended historical entitlement and minimal state non-interference.
* **Power, Discipline, and Biopolitics**: [[Michel Foucault]] traced the transition from sovereign punitive power to panoptic disciplinary surveillance and biopolitical regulation of populations in [[Discipline and Punish (Foucault)|*Discipline and Punish*]].
* **Critical Theory & Frankfurt School**: [[Theodor Adorno]], Max Horkheimer, Jürgen Habermas, and Axel Honneth critiquing instrumental reason and cultural commodification in [[Dialectic of Enlightenment (Adorno & Horkheimer)|*Dialectic of Enlightenment*]].

## 7. Philosophy of Language, Logic & Mathematics

Language and logic investigate meaning, truth-conditions, formal validity, and the limits of symbolic expression.

### The Linguistic Turn
* **Sense and Reference**: Gottlob Frege distinguished between the *Sinn* (mode of presentation) and *Bedeutung* (referent) of expressions.
* **Definite Descriptions**: Bertrand Russell's theory of descriptions resolving puzzles of non-referring terms ("The present King of France is bald").
* **Early vs. Late Wittgenstein**: In [[Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (Wittgenstein)|*Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*]] (1921), language is conceived as a truth-functional picture of states of affairs bounded by Proposition 7. The *Philosophical Investigations* (1953) abandoned this for language-games, meaning-as-use, and forms of life.
* **Conversational Maxims & Implicature**: H.P. Grice's [[Grices Maxims|Cooperative Principle and Conversational Maxims]] bridging what is said and what is meant; the hermeneutic imperative of [[The Principle of Charity]].
* **Sense, Paradox, and the Event**: Gilles Deleuze in [[Logic of Sense (Deleuze)|*The Logic of Sense*]] (1969) articulates an incorporeal surface theory of sense through Stoic logic and Carrollian paradox.
* **Deconstruction & Arche-Writing**: [[Jacques Derrida]] in [[Of Grammatology (Derrida)|*Of Grammatology*]] (1967) dismantles Western logocentrism and the metaphysics of presence.
* **Two Dogmas of Empiricism**: W.V.O. Quine attacked the analytic-synthetic distinction and verificationist reductionism, arguing for semantic holism.
* **Speech Act Theory**: J.L. Austin and John Searle categorized performative utterances (illocutionary and perlocutionary force).

## 8. Continental Traditions & Critical Theory

Continental philosophy broadly emphasizes historical situatedness, phenomenology, existential freedom, and critiques of systemic rationality.

* **Phenomenology**: Edmund Husserl's call to return "to the things themselves" through the phenomenological reduction (*epoche*); [[Maurice Merleau-Ponty|Maurice Merleau-Ponty's]] phenomenology of the lived body in [[Phenomenology of Perception (Merleau-Ponty)|*Phenomenology of Perception*]].
* **Existentialism & Fundamental Ontology**: [[Martin Heidegger|Martin Heidegger's]] analytic of *Dasein* in [[Being and Time (Heidegger)|*Being and Time*]]; [[Jean-Paul Sartre|Jean-Paul Sartre's]] radical freedom in [[Being and Nothingness (Sartre)|*Being and Nothingness*]]; [[Simone de Beauvoir|Simone de Beauvoir's]] situated ethics in [[The Ethics of Ambiguity (Beauvoir)|*The Ethics of Ambiguity*]]; [[Søren Kierkegaard|Søren Kierkegaard's]] leap of faith in [[Fear and Trembling (Kierkegaard)|*Fear and Trembling*]]; [[Albert Camus|Albert Camus's]] revolt in [[The Myth of Sisyphus (Camus)|*The Myth of Sisyphus*]]; [[Peter Wessel Zapffe|Peter Wessel Zapffe's]] evolutionary tragedy in [[The Last Messiah (Zapffe)|*The Last Messiah*]].
* **Post-Structuralism & Deconstruction**: [[Gilles Deleuze|Gilles Deleuze]] and Félix Guattari dismantling arborescent thought; [[Michel Foucault|Michel Foucault's]] genealogies of power; [[Jacques Derrida|Jacques Derrida's]] deconstruction of binary hierarchies in [[Of Grammatology (Derrida)|*Of Grammatology*]].
* **Critical Theory**: [[Theodor Adorno|Theodor Adorno]] and Max Horkheimer's critique of the culture industry and instrumental reason in [[Dialectic of Enlightenment (Adorno & Horkheimer)|*Dialectic of Enlightenment*]].
* **Accelerationism & Speculative Realism**: [[Nick Land|Nick Land]] and the CCRU modeling runaway capitalist deterritorialization as cybernetic intelligence escaping human oversight.

## 9. World & Comparative Philosophy

Philosophy flourishes across global civilizational traditions whose frameworks cross-examine Western paradigms:

* **Classical Indian Philosophy (*Darshana*)**:
  * *Orthodox (Astika)*: Nyaya (epistemology and logic), Vaisheshika (atomism), Samkhya (dualism of Purusha and Prakriti), Yoga, Mimamsa, and Vedanta (Advaita non-dualism of Shankara, Vishishtadvaita of Ramanuja).
  * *Heterodox (Nastika)*: Early Buddhism (dependent origination, anatta, four noble truths); Jainism (anekantavada, non-absolutism/multiplex reality); Charvaka (pure materialism and skepticism).
  * *Madhyamaka*: [[Nagarjuna|Nagarjuna's]] rigorous dialectic of emptiness (*sunyata*) demonstrating that all phenomena lack intrinsic nature (*svabhava*).
* **Classical Chinese Philosophy**:
  * *Daoism*: Spontaneous order and radical non-coercion in Laozi and [[Zhuangzi]].
  * *Confucianism & Neo-Confucianism*: Moral cultivation from Mengzi and Xunzi through Zhu Xi and Wang Yangming's unity of knowledge and action.
  * *Legalism (Fajia)*: Shang Yang and Han Feizi's systemic statecraft of institutional incentives and administrative power.
* **Islamic Golden Age Philosophy (*Falsafa*)**:
  * The translation movement and synthesis of Aristotelianism and Neoplatonism by Al-Kindi and Al-Farabi.
  * **[[Ibn Sina]] (Avicenna)**: Formulated the Flying Man thought experiment, the distinction between essence and existence, and the Necessary Existent proof.
  * **[[Al-Ghazali]]**: *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* (*Tahafut al-Falasifa*), pioneering occasionalism and critical limits of rationalist theology.
  * **[[Ibn Rushd]] (Averroes)**: *The Incoherence of the Incoherence*, championing Aristotelian rationalism and influencing Western scholasticism.
