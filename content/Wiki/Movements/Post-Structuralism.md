# Movement: Post-Structuralism

<div className="dropcap">

**Post-Structuralism** designates an intellectual movement that emerged in France during the late 1960s, preeminently associated with [[Gilles Deleuze]], [[Michel Foucault]], [[Jacques Derrida]], [[Jean-François Lyotard]], and [[Félix Guattari]]. Arising as an internal critique of structuralism (which modeled human culture on fixed, binary linguistic laws), post-structuralism interrogated totalizing philosophical systems, the stability of the signifier, humanist subjectivity, and transcendent claims to objective historical truth.

</div>

---

## 1. The Break with Structuralism

Structuralism (Saussure, Lévi-Strauss, early Lacan) viewed cultural practices, myths, and languages as governed by universal, invariant structures of binary opposition. Post-structuralists rejected this formal determinism:

1. **The Instability of Meaning**: Signifiers do not point directly to stable extra-linguistic signifieds. Meaning is an endless, deferred chain of differences (Derrida's *différance*).
2. **Critique of the Subject**: The autonomous, unified Cartesian cogito is not a universal foundation, but an ideological and historical construct produced through discursive regimes.
3. **Immanence and Difference**: Subordinating difference to identical categories (species under genus) obscures the productive, generative power of **pure difference** (Deleuze).
4. **The Entanglement of Power and Knowledge**: Knowledge is never politically neutral; every regime of truth is inseparable from apparatuses of power and discipline (Foucault).

## 2. Key Frameworks & Thinkers

### Gilles Deleuze (1925–1995): Difference and Assemblage
In *Difference and Repetition* (1968) and his collaborative works with Félix Guattari (*Anti-Oedipus*, *A Thousand Plateaus*), Deleuze dismantled the traditional "dogmatic image of thought":
* **The Rhizome**: An open, non-hierarchical, horizontal network with multiple entrances and exits, contrasted with the centralized, tree-like "arborescent" model of Western metaphysics.
* **Assemblages (*Agencements*) & Lines of Flight**: Complex constellations of bodies, technologies, desires, and enunciations that coalesce temporarily before being destabilized by deterritorializing lines of flight (*lignes de fuite*).
* **Univocity of Being**: Being is said in a single, identical sense of all things, but what it affirms is radical, divergent difference.

### Michel Foucault (1926–1984): Power/Knowledge & Biopolitics
Foucault analyzed how modern institutions discipline the body and construct subjectivity:
* **The Archaeological & Genealogical Methods**: Tracing the historical conditions (*episteme*) under which concepts emerge, mutate, and exercise authority.
* **Disciplinary Power & Panopticism**: In *Discipline and Punish* (1975), Foucault charted the shift from spectacle punishment to diffuse, internalizing surveillance that molds human bodies into "docile" instruments.
* **Biopolitics**: The modern governance of life itself—populations managed through birth rates, public health, statistics, and demographic security.

### Jacques Derrida (1930–2004): Deconstruction
Derrida demonstrated in *Of Grammatology* (1967) that Western metaphysics is dominated by **Logocentrism**—the privilege of spoken presence over written absence:
* Deconstructive reading locates the internal contradictions and margins of texts, demonstrating that binary hierarchies (speech/writing, nature/culture, male/female) depend constitutively upon the very terms they suppress.

## 3. Political & Contemporary Legacy

Post-structuralism profoundly influenced critical race theory, post-colonialism (Said, Spivak), queer theory (Butler), and posthumanism ([[Rosi Braidotti|Braidotti]]). In the 1990s, the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (CCRU) at Warwick, led by [[Nick Land]], adapted Deleuzo-Guattarian nomadology and schizoanalysis into the apocalyptic philosophy of **[[Accelerationism]]**.
