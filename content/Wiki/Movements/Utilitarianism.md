# Movement: Utilitarianism

<div className="dropcap">

**Utilitarianism** is a major tradition within normative ethics and political philosophy, founded in its modern classical form by Jeremy Bentham and [[John Stuart Mill]], and refined by Henry Sidgwick, Peter Singer, and [[Derek Parfit]]. Utilitarianism is a species of consequentialism, maintaining that the moral value of actions, policies, and institutions is determined exclusively by the net aggregation of well-being, happiness, or utility they produce.

</div>

---

## 1. Core Principles

Classical utilitarianism is characterized by four intersecting commitments:

1. **Consequentialism**: Actions are evaluated solely by the state of affairs they bring about; intentions and intrinsic acts possess no independent moral weight.
2. **Welfarism / Hedonism**: The relevant consequence to maximize is well-being. For Bentham, this was pure pleasure over pain; for Mill, qualitative intellectual and moral flourishing; for contemporary preference utilitarians, the satisfaction of individual preferences.
3. **Aggregationism**: The total moral good is calculated by summing the well-being of all affected sentient beings.
4. **Impartiality (Agent-Neutrality)**: Everyone's happiness counts equally. As Bentham expressed: *"Each to count for one, and none for more than one."*

---

## 2. Classical Formulations

### Jeremy Bentham (1748–1832): The Quantitative Calculus
In *An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation* (1789), Bentham proclaimed nature has placed mankind under two sovereign masters: pain and pleasure. He proposed the **Felicific Calculus** to mathematically weigh actions across seven variables: intensity, duration, certainty, propinquity, fecundity, purity, and extent. Bentham rejected qualitative distinctions: *"Prejudice apart, the game of push-pin is of equal value with the arts and sciences of music and poetry, if the game of push-pin furnish more pleasure."*

### John Stuart Mill (1806–1873): Qualitative Pleasures & Liberty
Bentham's quantitative model was attacked as a "doctrine worthy only of swine." Mill defended the theory in *Utilitarianism* (1861) by introducing qualitative hierarchies of pleasure:
* **Higher Pleasures**: Intellectual, artistic, and moral pursuits.
* **Lower Pleasures**: Mere bodily, sensory gratification.
* As Mill memorably argued: *"It is better to be a human being dissatisfied than a pig satisfied; better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied."*
* In *On Liberty* (1859), Mill formulated the **Harm Principle**, arguing that maximizing long-term social utility requires absolute protection of individual liberty and freedom of speech, so long as it does not cause harm to others.

## 3. Structural Varieties

* **Act Utilitarianism**: The principle of utility is applied directly to each particular choice in the moment.
* **Rule Utilitarianism**: Actions are evaluated based on their conformity to a general moral rule, which itself has been selected because its universal adoption maximizes overall utility.
* **Preference Utilitarianism**: Proposed by R.M. Hare and Peter Singer, defining utility as the satisfaction of preferences or desires rather than psychological states of pleasure.
* **Negative Utilitarianism**: Prioritizing the absolute minimization of suffering over the promotion of positive pleasure (Popper).

---

## 4. Canonical Criticisms

Utilitarianism is the central sparring partner of twentieth-century moral philosophy:
* **The Separateness of Persons**: [[John Rawls]] argued in *A Theory of Justice* (1971) that utilitarianism conflates all individuals into a single aggregate chooser, permitting the rights of a minority to be violated for the convenience of the majority.
* **The Utility Monster**: Robert Nozick proposed an entity that converts resources into happiness far more efficiently than normal humans; utilitarianism would obligate us to sacrifice everything to feed it.
* **The Integrity Objection**: Bernard Williams argued that demanding absolute consequential impartiality alienates people from their deepest moral projects and personal relationships.
* **Deontological Constraints**: Thought experiments such as [[The Trolley Problem]] (Footbridge variant) suggest our moral intuitions strictly forbid using an innocent person as a mere instrument to save others.

## 5. In Phil Chat

In Philchat, utilitarianism is famously subjected to speculative thought experiment analysis in the chronicle of **[[The Pleasure Box]]** (Volume I). 

Proposed by [[jere|Jere]], the Pleasure Box posited an artificial intelligence or conscious container set to maximal ecstatic euphoria. Because its infinite positive utils mathematically dwarf all human misery combined, the room spent hours debating whether the creation of the Box permits all earthly crimes to proceed unaddressed. [[janne|Janne]] famously interrupted the utilitarian algebra by asking *"why not just do heroin"*, while Hugh Chungus declined the engineering contract.
