# Advaita Vedanta

<div className="dropcap">

**Advaita Vedanta** (Sanskrit: अद्वैत वेदान्त; literally, *"Non-dualism of the End of the Vedas"*) is the most influential orthodox (*astika*) school of Hindu philosophy. Grounded in the exegetical synthesis of the **Upanishads**, the **Bhagavad Gita**, and the **Brahma Sutras** (the *Prasthānatrayī*), Advaita was decisively systematized in the eighth century CE by the philosopher-theologian **Adi Shankara** (c. 788–820 CE)[^shankara-ref].

</div>

The fundamental thesis of Advaita is captured in Shankara's celebrated verse summary:

$$\text{"Brahma satyam jagan-mithyā jīvo brahmaiva nāparaḥ"}$$
$$\text{(Brahman alone is real; the empirical world is illusory; the individual self is non-different from Brahman.)}$$

Against rival dualist (*Dvaita*) and qualified non-dualist (*Vishishtadvaita*) systems, Advaita establishes an absolute metaphysical monism: reality is devoid of internal distinctions (*svagata-bheda*), differences between members of the same class (*sajātīya-bheda*), and differences from other classes (*vijātīya-bheda*).

[^shankara-ref]: See Swāmi Gambhīrānanda, trans., *Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya of Śaṅkarācārya* (Calcutta: Advaita Ashrama, 1965). See [[Bibliography#Shankara-Brahmasutra]].

## 1. The Core Identity: *Atman* is *Brahman*

Advaita turns upon the identity of two concepts:
1. ***Brahman***: The ultimate, unconditioned, infinite reality; the ground and efficient-material cause of the universe. Brahman is characterized essentially as ***Sat-Chit-Ananda***:
   * **Sat**: Pure, unconditioned Being / Truth.
   * **Chit**: Pure, non-intentional Consciousness / Awareness.
   * **Ananda**: Infinite, unadulterated Bliss.
2. ***Atman***: The true, innermost self of the individual—the witness consciousness (*sakshin*) that observes the waking, dreaming, and deep-sleep states without being modified by them.

In everyday unenlightened existence, human beings mistakenly identify their selfhood with the physical body, sensory organs, emotions, or intellect (*ahamkara*). Advaita demonstrates through the four "Great Sayings" (*Mahavakyas*) of the Upanishads that the inner witness (*Atman*) is none other than the cosmic ground (*Brahman*):
* ***Tat Tvam Asi***: "That Thou Art" (*Chandogya Upanishad* 6.8.7).
* ***Aham Brahma Asmi***: "I am Brahman" (*Brihadaranyaka Upanishad* 1.4.10).
* ***Ayam Atma Brahma***: "This Self is Brahman" (*Mandukya Upanishad* 1.2).
* ***Prajnanam Brahma***: "Consciousness is Brahman" (*Aitareya Upanishad* 3.1.3).

## 2. *Maya* and *Adhyasa*: The Mechanism of Cosmic Illusion

If Brahman is the sole non-dual reality, how does the pluralistic universe of stars, planets, bodies, and suffering arise? Shankara explains this through **Maya** (the cosmic illusion) and **Adhyasa** (superimposition)[^adhyasa-ref]:

```
                       THE METAPHOR OF ROPE AND SNAKE
               ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
               │         TRUE SUBSTRATUM: THE ROPE            │
               │             (Brahman / Reality)              │
               └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                      │
                     Superimposition (Adhyasa) via Ignorance (Avidya)
                                      │
               ┌──────────────────────▼───────────────────────┐
               │        SUPERIMPOSED APPARITION: SNAKE        │
               │        (The Plural World of Names/Forms)     │
               │                                              │
               │  - Creates real fear, sweating, and flight   │
               │  - Sublated instantly upon light and seeing  │
               └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

In the famous analogy, a traveler walking at twilight sees a coiled rope on the path and mistakes it for a venomous snake. The traveler experiences genuine terror, a racing pulse, and flees. Yet the snake possesses no independent reality: it is a **superimposition** (*adhyāsa*) projected by ignorance onto the real substratum of the rope. 

Similarly:
* The plural empirical universe (*Jagat*) is superimposed onto pure Consciousness (*Brahman*) by beginningless ignorance (*avidya*).
* **Maya** operates through two distinct powers (*shakti*):
  1. *Avarana-shakti* (the veiling power): Obscures the true non-dual nature of Brahman.
  2. *Vikshepa-shakti* (the projecting power): Projects the false illusion of multiplicity, space, and time.

[^adhyasa-ref]: Formulated in the *Adhyasa-Bhashya* (preface to Shankara's commentary on the Brahma Sutras).

## 3. The Three Orders of Reality (*Sattatraya*)

To prevent its non-dualism from collapsing into crude subjective idealism or nihilism, Advaita introduces a rigorous tripartite epistemological ontology:

| Level of Reality | Name | Description | Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paramarthika** | **Absolute Reality** | Pure Brahman; unconditioned, eternal, non-dual, free from space and time. | Never sublated; the ultimate truth. |
| **Vyavaharika** | **Empirical Reality** | The conventional everyday world of physical laws, science, ethics, and ritual. | Valid for all practical purposes until liberation (*Moksha*). |
| **Pratibhasika** | **Apparent / Illusory Reality** | Dreams, optical illusions (mirages, rope-snake), hallucinations. | Sublated immediately upon waking or correct perception. |

Advaita does not claim that chairs, tables, and fellow human beings are non-existent figments of personal imagination. At the **Vyavaharika** level, the physical world is governed by lawful causality and empirical regularity. It is designated as *mithyā* (anirvacaniya: neither strictly real like Brahman, nor totally non-existent like a square circle), retaining full practical validity until sublated by direct non-dual realization.

---

## 4. Epistemology: The Six *Pramanas*

Advaita adopts the sophisticated epistemology of the Bhatta school of Mimamsa, accepting six valid means of knowledge (*pramanas*):
1. **Pratyaksha**: Direct sensory perception.
2. **Anumana**: Rational logical inference.
3. **Upamana**: Analogy and comparison.
4. **Arthapatti**: Postulation or presumption (e.g., inferring that a living person who is not at home is outside).
5. **Anupalabdhi**: Non-cognition (the valid perception of an absence).
6. **Shabda**: Verbal testimony of authoritative texts (specifically the Upanishads for metaphysical knowledge).

---

## 5. Liberation: *Moksha* and *Jivanmukti*

In Advaita, liberation (***Moksha***) is not the attainment of a distant celestial realm after death, nor is it the creation of a new spiritual state. Because the self is *already* Brahman, liberation is simply the **dispelling of ignorance** (*avidya-nivritti*) through direct, intuitive knowledge (*jnana*).

This entails the possibility of ***Jivanmukti***: **liberation while still embodied in physical life**:
* The enlightened sage (*jivanmukta*) continues to live, eat, and speak in the world, sustained by residual past karma (*prarabdha karma*), like a potter's wheel that continues spinning for a time after the potter stops pushing it.
* Internally, the sage remains entirely liberated from fear, sorrow, ego, and desire, resting permanently in the tranquil awareness of universal non-duality.
