# A Theory of Justice

> **John Rawls**, *A Theory of Justice* (Harvard University Press, 1971; Revised Edition, 1999)[^rawls-tj-ed].

<div className="dropcap">

Published in 1971 following a decade dominated by utilitarian social engineering and behavioral positivism, John Rawls' *A Theory of Justice* revived systematic normative political philosophy in the Anglo-American world. Elevating traditional social contract theory (Locke, Rousseau, Kant) to a higher plane of abstraction, Rawls develops **Justice as Fairness** (*Gerechtigkeit als Fairneß*): a deontological political conception demonstrating that free and equal moral persons would rationally choose principles of justice that guarantee fundamental basic liberties and prioritize the welfare of society's least-advantaged citizens.

</div>

---

## I. The Foundational Polemic: The Critique of Utilitarianism

Rawls opens with a resounding deontological declaration:

$$\text{"Justice is the first virtue of social institutions, as truth is of systems of thought...}$$
$$\text{Each person possesses an inviolability founded on justice that even the welfare of society as a whole cannot override."} \quad (\text{TJ } 3)$$

### The Fatal Defect of Classical Utilitarianism
* **Failure to Respect the Separateness of Persons**: Classical utilitarianism merges all individuals into one through the device of the Impartial Spectator. By aggregating happiness across society, utilitarianism permits the permanent subjugation, exploitation, or deprivation of a minority if the net arithmetic sum of utility outweighs their suffering.
* **Teleological Subordination of the Right to the Good**: Utilitarianism defines the Good independently (as pleasure or preference satisfaction) and then defines the Right merely as that which maximizes the Good. Rawls inverts this: **The Right is strictly prior to the Good** (*The Priority of the Right*). Principles of justice constrain what desires and life-plans are permissible from the outset.

## II. The Analytical Apparatus: The Original Position & The Veil of Ignorance

To discover what principles free and equal persons would agree upon, Rawls constructs a hypothetical device of representation: **The Original Position** (*status quo ante*).

```
                              THE ORIGINAL POSITION
                         ┌───────────────────────────────┐
                         │      RATIONAL CONTRACTORS     │
                         │   (Mutual Disinterest, Maximin)│
                         └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                                        │
                         ┌──────────────▼────────────────┐
                         │     THE VEIL OF IGNORANCE     │
                         │  (Complete Particular Amnesia) │
                         └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                                        │
           ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                         ▼
    INFORMATION STRIPPED                                   INFORMATION RETAINED
• Place in society & social class                     • General laws of human psychology
• Race, gender, ethnicity, generation                 • Principles of economic theory
• Natural talents, IQ, physical strength              • Circumstances of justice (moderate scarcity)
• Conception of the good & life-plan                  • Society requires social primary goods
```

### 1. The Veil of Ignorance (*Schleier des Nichtwissens*)
To eliminate the distorting effects of brute natural luck and historical contingency, contractors are placed behind a thick **Veil of Ignorance**. They do not know:
* Their socioeconomic class, wealth, or status.
* Their race, gender, sexual orientation, or generational cohort.
* Their distribution of natural endowments (intelligence, artistic talent, athletic vigor).
* Their particular conception of the good (their religion, philosophical worldviews, risk tolerance).

They do know, however:
* General sociological and economic laws.
* That human beings need **Primary Social Goods** (*Grundgüter*): rights, liberties, opportunities, income, wealth, and the social bases of self-respect.
* **The Circumstances of Justice** (Hume's condition: moderate scarcity and conflicting claims).

### 2. Rational Choice under Extreme Uncertainty: The Maximin Rule
Because the contractors do not know their probabilities of ending up in any particular social stratum, and because the worst-off outcome could be catastrophic (lifelong slavery or starvation), standard expected-utility calculations cannot be rationally deployed.

Instead, parties rationally adopt the **Maximin Criterion** (maximizing the minimum payoff):
$$\text{Rank alternatives by their worst possible outcomes, and select that option}$$
$$\text{whose worst outcome is superior to the worst outcome of all other options.}$$

## III. The Two Principles of Justice

Rawls deduces that parties in the Original Position would unanimously select two fundamental principles of justice:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{\bf First Principle (Equal Basic Liberties):} & \quad \text{Each person has an equal right to the most extensive scheme} \\
& \quad \text{of equal basic liberties compatible with a similar scheme for all.} \\
\\
\text{\bf Second Principle (Social \& Economic):} & \quad \text{Social and economic inequalities must satisfy two conditions:} \\
\text{\it (a) Fair Equality of Opportunity:} & \quad \text{They must be attached to offices and positions open to all} \\
& \quad \text{under conditions of fair equality of opportunity.} \\
\text{\it (b) The Difference Principle:} & \quad \text{They must be to the greatest benefit of the least-advantaged members.}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 1. The Lexical Priority Rules (*Lexikalische Ordnung*)
Rawls strictly forbids trade-offs between liberty and economic prosperity through two lexical priority rules:

1. **The Priority of Liberty**: The First Principle has absolute lexical priority over the Second Principle. A basic liberty (freedom of speech, assembly, conscience, personal property, political suffrage) can be limited or restricted *only for the sake of liberty itself*, never for economic efficiency or aggregate wealth.
2. **The Priority of Fair Opportunity over the Difference Principle**: Fair equality of opportunity has absolute lexical priority over the Difference Principle. A society cannot purchase higher income for the poor by closing educational and professional offices to disadvantaged groups.

### 2. The Difference Principle (*Das Unterschiedsprinzip*)
The Difference Principle represents an egalitarian masterstroke:
* Natural talents and genetic endowments are **arbitrary from a moral point of view**. No one "deserves" their high IQ, robust health, or advantageous family environment.
* Rawls does not mandate drab leveling or biological handicapping. Instead, natural endowments are treated as a **common social asset**:

$$\text{Those who have been favored by nature may gain from their good fortune}$$
$$\text{only on terms that improve the situation of those who have lost out.}$$

Inequalities (such as higher salaries for skilled surgeons or inventors) are legitimate *if and only if* those incentives stimulate economic productivity that directly elevates the baseline condition of the least-advantaged citizen class.

## IV. Reflective Equilibrium (*Überlegungsgleichgewicht*)

How are the conditions of the Original Position themselves justified? Rawls introduces the method of **Reflective Equilibrium**:

```
                         REFLECTIVE EQUILIBRIUM
         ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
         │          CONSIDERED MORAL CONVICTIONS              │
         │   (e.g., "Racial discrimination is unjust",        │
         │          "Religious persecution is wrong")         │
         └─────────────────────────▲──────────────────────────┘
                                   │
                           MUTUAL PRUNING &
                              ADJUSTMENT
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────▼──────────────────────────┐
         │          THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES OF JUSTICE         │
         │       (Equal Liberty, Difference Principle,        │
         │               Original Position Design)            │
         └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Reflective equilibrium is a non-foundationalist, coherentist process of mutual pruning:
1. We start with our firmest considered judgments of justice (e.g., that religious intolerance or slavery is abhorrent).
2. We propose theoretical principles that explain and systematize these judgments.
3. Where discrepancies arise, we iterate back and forth—sometimes modifying the theoretical description of the contract situation, sometimes revising our particular judgments—until principles and considered convictions coincide in a stable, harmonious equilibrium.
