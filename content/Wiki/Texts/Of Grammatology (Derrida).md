# Of Grammatology

> **Jacques Derrida**, *De la grammatologie* (1967)  
> Standard English translation: Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak (Johns Hopkins University Press, 1976; 40th anniversary edition, 2016)[^derrida-og-ref].

<div className="dropcap">

Published in Paris in 1967 by Les Éditions de Minuit, *Of Grammatology* is Jacques Derrida's masterwork that launched the worldwide philosophical movement of **deconstruction**. Setting out to establish the theoretical grounds of a historical science of writing ("grammatology"), Derrida quickly discovers that writing cannot become an ordinary scientific discipline because it undermines the foundational presuppositions of Western scientific rationality itself. Interrogating Ferdinand de Saussure, Claude Lévi-Strauss, and Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Derrida demonstrates that Western metaphysics from antiquity to structural linguistics has been blinded by **logocentrism**—the illusion that spoken voice possesses immediate, unmediated presence to thought.

</div>

## 1. The Core Diagnosis: Logocentrism & Phonocentrism

Derrida diagnoses the defining hierarchy of Western philosophy:

```
                     THE CLASSICAL HIERARCHY OF LANGUAGE
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PRIVILEGED TERM: SPEECH (PHONĒ)          │ DEGRADED TERM: WRITING (GRAPHĒ)          │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Immediacy, living breath (*pneuma*)    │ • Distance, absence, and mechanical delay│
│ • Intimacy with the speaker's conscious  │ • Dead letter, orphaned transcript       │
│   intention (self-presence)              │ • Threat to authentic memory (Plato)     │
│ • Natural, internal, and spiritual       │ • Artificial, external, and corrupting   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Western thought has persistently treated writing as a mere phonetic mirror or dangerous exterior shadow of speech. In Plato's *Phaedrus*, writing is condemned as a drug (*pharmakon*) that destroys genuine interior memory by offering an artificial crutch. In Rousseau, writing is an unnatural corruption of pure rustic voice. In Saussure's *Course in General Linguistics*, writing is excluded as an external trap that distorts the pure linguistic system of acoustic signs.

Derrida demonstrates that this exclusion is logically impossible: **the very characteristics used to degrade writing (absence of the author, repeatability across space and time, spacing, difference) are the necessary preconditions for ANY language to function, including spoken speech.**

## 2. Structural Architecture of the Treatise

The treatise is organized into two major divisions:

### Part I: Writing before the Letter
1. **The End of the Book and the Beginning of Writing**: The traditional concept of the "Book" implies a closed, complete, and unified totality authored by a sovereign consciousness (or God). "Writing," by contrast, designates an open, infinite, differential network without a closed boundary or theological anchor.
2. **Linguistics and Grammatology**: Reading Ferdinand de Saussure. Saussure recognizes that in language *there are only differences without positive terms*, yet he recoils from his own insight by declaring spoken sound to be the natural essence of language and banishing writing as an external parasitic contagion.
3. **Of Grammatology as a Positive Science**: Grammatology cannot settle down as an ordinary academic department, because to think the origin of writing requires deconstructing the concept of the "origin" itself.

### Part II: Nature, Culture, Writing
1. **The Age of Rousseau**: A microscopic reading of Rousseau's *Essay on the Origin of Languages* and *Confessions*.
2. **The Violence of the Letter: From Lévi-Strauss to Rousseau**: Derrida reads Claude Lévi-Strauss's *Tristes Tropiques* (the famous "Writing Lesson" with the Nambikwara chief). Lévi-Strauss idealizes the indigenous tribe as an innocent, peaceful culture of pure speech before the violent contamination of European writing. Derrida proves that the tribe already possessed structural arche-writing (genealogies, classifications, prohibitions): Lévi-Strauss's nostalgia for pure spoken innocence is an ethnocentric myth.
3. **"That Dangerous Supplement" (*Ce dangereux supplément*)**: The analysis of Rousseau's confession of autoeroticism and pedagogical substitutes.

```
                      THE PARADOX OF THE SUPPLEMENT
                      ┌────────────────────────────┐
                      │    ORIGINAL NATURE         │
                      │  (Supposedly complete)     │
                      └─────────────┬──────────────┘
                                    │
                        Requires an addition/crutch
                                    │
                      ┌─────────────▼──────────────┐
                      │     THE SUPPLEMENT         │
                      │  (Writing, masturbation,   │
                      │   culture, artifice)       │
                      └─────────────┬──────────────┘
                                    │
                      Reveals that the original was
                      INCOMPLETE from the very start!
```

## 3. Foundational Theoretical Concepts

### 1. Arche-Writing (*L'Archi-écriture*)
Arche-writing is not physical ink on paper (empirical writing). It is the anterior condition of possibility for both speech and script: the fundamental structure of **spacing** (*espacement*), punctuation, and temporal interval without which no sign could be distinguished from another.

### 2. The Trace (*La Trace*)
Every sign is defined not by what it positively contains, but by its difference from other signs. Therefore, every present sign contains the invisible, silent **trace** of the absent signs that it is not. Presence is never pure; it is always haunted by non-presence.

### 3. *"Il n'y a pas de hors-texte"*
In Section 2 of Part II, Derrida writes the most contested sentence of modern philosophy:
> *"Il n'y a pas de hors-texte."* (There is no outside-text / There is nothing outside of the text.)

Derrida explains what this means:
* It does not mean that the physical world, hunger, trees, or wars do not exist.
* It means that there is no magical escape hatch from signification: we cannot leap outside of language and conceptual systems to apprehend a "pure reality" in pristine nakedness. Every experience of reality is mediated, contextual, and textually structured.
