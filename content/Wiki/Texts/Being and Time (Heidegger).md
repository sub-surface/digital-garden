# Being and Time

> **Martin Heidegger**, *Sein und Zeit* (1927)  
> Standard translations: John Macquarrie and Edward Robinson (Blackwell, 1962); Joan Stambaugh, revised by Dennis J. Schmidt (SUNY Press, 2010)[^heidegger-bt-ed].

<div className="dropcap">

Published in Edmund Husserl's *Jahrbuch für Philosophie und phänomenologische Forschung* in the spring of 1927, *Being and Time* (*Sein und Zeit*) is arguably the most revolutionary work of twentieth-century European ontology. Heidegger's stated objective is nothing less than the radical destruction of the history of Western metaphysics to reawaken the forgotten **Question of Being** (*die Seinsfrage*). Decrying twenty-five centuries of philosophical confusion in which Being (*Sein*) was reduced to mere entities (*Seiendes*), Heidegger demonstrates that Being can only be investigated through a phenomenological analytic of that unique entity that has an understanding of Being: **Dasein** (the human being).

</div>

---

## I. Foundational Premise: The Ontological Difference

Heidegger opens with an epigraph from Plato's *Sophist* (244a): *"For manifestly you have long been aware of what you mean when you use the expression 'being'; we, however, who used to think we understood it, have now become perplexed."*

### 1. The Oblivion of Being (*Seinsvergessenheit*)
Western metaphysics from Plato and Aristotle through Descartes, Kant, and Hegel has suffered from the **oblivion of Being**:
* Dogmatic prejudice treats "Being" as the most universal concept, indefinable, and self-evident.
* In treating Being as self-evident, philosophers consistently commit the cardinal error of **ontotheology**: reducing Being (*Sein*) to a supreme entity or object (*Seiendes*—such as the Platonic Form, the Aristotelian Prime Mover, the Christian Creator, the Cartesian Cogito, or the Kantian Object of Experience).

### 2. The Ontological Difference
$$\text{The Being of entities is not itself an entity.} \quad (\text{SZ } 6)$$
* **Ontic (*ontisch*)**: Pertains to entities, facts about beings, and empirical properties (the realm of positive sciences: biology, physics, sociology).
* **Ontological (*ontologisch*)**: Pertains to Being itself: the conditions of intelligibility that allow any entity to show up as an entity at all.

## II. The Inquirer: Dasein

To investigate Being, we must interrogate an entity that already possesses a pre-ontological understanding of Being. That entity is **Dasein** (literally, "Being-there"):

$$\text{"Dasein is an entity which does not just occur among other entities...}$$
$$\text{Dasein is ontically distinguished by the fact that, in its very Being, that Being is an issue for it."} \quad (\text{SZ } 12)$$

* **The Priority of Existence**:
  $$\text{"The 'essence' of Dasein lies in its existence."} \quad (\text{SZ } 42)$$
  Unlike a manufactured tool whose essence precedes its existence, Dasein has no fixed nature. Dasein *is* what it does in projecting itself toward possibilities.
* **Mineness (*Jemeinigkeit*)**: Dasein is in each case *mine*. It can choose itself, win itself, or lose itself (the possibility of **Authenticity**, *Eigentlichkeit*, versus **Inauthenticity**, *Uneigentlichkeit*).

## III. Division One: The Preparatory Fundamental Analytic of Dasein

### 1. Being-in-the-world (*In-der-Welt-sein*)
Heidegger decisively dismantles the Cartesian subject-object divide:

```
        CARTESIAN DUALISM                       HEIDEGGERIAN FUNDAMENTAL ONTOLOGY
┌──────────────┐   EPISTEMIC    ┌──────────────┐      ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ RES COGITANS │   CHASM (?)    │ RES EXTENSA  │      │       BEING-IN-THE-WORLD        │
│ Isolated Mind├───────────────►│Physical World│      │          (INSEPARABLE)          │
└──────────────┘                └──────────────┘      │  Dasein ◄──Care/Practice──► World│
                                                      └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

Dasein is not an encapsulated, isolated mind (*res cogitans*) gazing out across an epistemic gulf at physical objects (*res extensa*). Dasein is fundamentally and inextricably **Being-in-the-world**: an embedded, active coping within a meaningful nexus of relations.

### 2. Equipment (*das Zeug*) and Modes of Being
How do entities within the world show up to Dasein in everyday life?

| Mode of Being | German Term | Mode of Encounter | Practical Meaning |
|---------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Ready-to-hand** | ***Zuhandenheit*** | Practical, pre-reflective coping | The hammer in active carpentry; transparently absorbed in an overarching task-network |
| **Present-at-hand** | ***Vorhandenheit*** | Detached, theoretical scrutiny | The hammer when the head flies off; isolated as a physical object with mass and dimensions |

* For traditional epistemology (Descartes, Locke), *Vorhandenheit* is primary: reality consists of physical matter possessing spatio-temporal properties, upon which value is later subjectively projected.
* Heidegger reverses this: **Ready-to-hand (*Zuhandenheit*) is ontologically primary**. We encounter things first as equipment in a web of significance (*Bedeutsamkeit*): a hammer is "in-order-to" drive a nail, in-order-to build a roof, for-the-sake-of shelter. Theoretical presence-at-hand is a deficient, derivative mode that occurs only when equipment breaks down.

### 3. The "They" (*das Man*) and Everyday Inauthenticity
In everyday existence, Dasein does not live as an autonomous individual. Dasein is submerged in **The "They"** (*das Man*):
* We enjoy ourselves as *they* enjoy; we read, see, and judge about literature and art as *they* see and judge; we recoil from the "great mass" as *they* recoil.
* This everyday falling (*Verfallen*) manifests through:
  * **Idle Talk (*Gerede*)**: Surface chatter that closes off genuine understanding.
  * **Curiosity (*Neugier*)**: Restless desire for novelty without ever staying to comprehend.
  * **Ambiguity (*Zweideutigkeit*)**: Everything looks as if it were genuinely understood, when at bottom it is not.

### 4. Attunement, Understanding, and Anxiety (*Angst*)
* **Attunement (*Befindlichkeit*) & Mood (*Stimmung*)**: We are never without a mood. Mood is not a subjective psychological state, but the ontological disclosure of our **Thrownness** (*Geworfenheit*): Dasein finds itself already delivered over into a world it did not choose.
* **Fear (*Furcht*) vs. Anxiety (*Angst*)**:
  * Fear has a specific, determinate object within the world (a snarling dog, an approaching car).
  * **Anxiety has no determinate entity as its object**: in *Angst*, the totality of equipmental significance collapses. The world slips away into total insignificance. In this vertigo, Dasein encounters the naked **Nothing** (*das Nichts*) and is brought face-to-face with its radical freedom to choose itself.

## IV. Division Two: Dasein and Temporality

### 1. The Question of Wholeness and Death (*Sein-zum-Tode*)
Can Dasein ever be grasped as a whole? As long as Dasein exists, it is constantly "ahead-of-itself," oriented toward outstanding possibilities. Once it reaches its end, it is no longer Dasein.

Heidegger resolves this by defining death not as a biological event (*Ableben*), but as an ontological mode of being: **Being-towards-death** (*Sein-zum-Tode*):
$$\text{Death is Dasein's ownmost (eigenste), non-relational (unbezügliche),}$$
$$\text{and not-to-be-outstripped (unüberholbare) possibility.} \quad (\text{SZ } 250)$$

* In everyday inauthenticity, *das Man* evades death: "One dies eventually, but for the moment, not me."
* In **Authentic Anticipation (*Vorlaufen*)**, Dasein resolutely confronts death as the absolute impossibility of all possibilities. This shatters the illusions of *das Man* and individualizes Dasein to its own authentic existence.

### 2. Anticipatory Resoluteness (*vorlaufende Entschlossenheit*)
Prompted by the **Call of Conscience** (*der Ruf des Gewissens*)—which is nothing other than Dasein in its anxiety calling to itself out of its fallenness—Dasein enters into **Resoluteness** (*Entschlossenheit*): seizing its historical, thrown situation with clear-sighted courage.

### 3. Ecstatic Temporality (*Zeitlichkeit*)
In the climactic chapters of Division Two, Heidegger deduces the meaning of Care (*Sorge*):

$$\text{The primordial ontological meaning of Care is Temporality.} \quad (\text{SZ } 326)$$

Temporality is not an infinite, linear sequence of uniform "now-points" flowing from past to future (the clock-time of presence-at-hand). Primordial temporality is **Ecstatic** (from Greek *ekstasis*, stepping-outside-itself):

```
                                  PRIMORDIAL ECSTASES
                           ┌───────────────────────────────┐
                           │      FUTURE (ZUKUNFT)         │
                           │   Ahead-of-itself in choice   │
                           └──────────────┬────────────────┘
                                          │
                  ┌───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
                  │                                               │
   HAVING-BEEN (GEWESENHEIT)                              PRESENT (GEGENWART)
    Thrownness / Facticity                               Disclosing the Situation
(Assuming one's historical ground)                        (Making entities present)
```

The future has ontological priority: Dasein is primarily that which comes toward itself out of its possibilities, retrieving its having-been (*Gewesenheit*) to act decisively in the present moment of vision (*Augenblick*).
