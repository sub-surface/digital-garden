# Fear and Trembling

> **Søren Kierkegaard** (writing as *Johannes de silentio*), *Frygt og Bæven: Dialektisk Lyrik* (1843)  
> Standard translations: Howard V. Hong and Edna H. Hong (Princeton University Press, 1983); Alastair Hannay (Penguin Classics, 1985)[^kierkegaard-ft-ref].

<div className="dropcap">

Published in Copenhagen in October 1843 under the pseudonym *Johannes de silentio* ("John of Silence"), *Fear and Trembling* (*Frygt og Bæven*) is Søren Kierkegaard's most electrifying and enigmatic masterpiece. Subtitled *A Dialectical Lyric*, the work interrogates the terrifying biblical narrative of the *Akedah* (Genesis 22)—God's commandment to the patriarch Abraham to take his beloved, long-awaited son Isaac to the heights of Mount Moriah and slaughter him as a burnt offering. Through this horrific ordeal, Kierkegaard mounts a devastating assault on the rationalist moral philosophy of [[Immanuel Kant]] and [[G.W.F. Hegel]], demonstrating that authentic religious faith cannot be reduced to universal human ethics, but requires an agonizing, solitary **Leap into the Absurd**.

</div>

---

## 1. Overview & Historical Context

*Fear and Trembling* was composed during a period of acute emotional crisis following Kierkegaard's unilateral dissolution of his engagement to Regine Olsen. The biblical sacrifice of Isaac functioned as a coded, deeply personal allegory: just as Abraham was commanded to surrender the object he loved most in the world, Kierkegaard felt divinely called to sacrifice his marriage to fulfill his solitary authorial destiny.

At the same time, the book was a direct philosophical attack on Hegelianism. In Hegel's *Philosophy of Right*, the highest realization of human reason is the **Ethical Life** (*Sittlichkeit*)—the alignment of individual conscience with the universal laws of the family, civil society, and the state. Anyone who places private revelation above the civic ethical law is, for Hegel, an irrational fanatic or a criminal. *Fear and Trembling* was written to explode this synthesis, proving that Abraham is either the Father of Faith or a common murderer.

## 2. Structural Architecture

The treatise is structured into poetic preludes followed by three formal philosophical "Problema":

```
                            FEEAR AND TREMBLING
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PRELUDE & TUNING UP (Stemning)                                              │
│ • Four imaginative variations of the journey to Mount Moriah                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ EULOGY ON ABRAHAM (Lovtale over Abraham)                                     │
│ • Praising Abraham's greatness: not for worldly power, but for faith        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PRELIMINARY EXPECTORATION (Foreløbig Udgydelse)                             │
│ • The Knight of Infinite Resignation vs. The Knight of Faith                │
│ • The Double Movement of Faith (Virtue of the Absurd)                       │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PROBLEMA I                                                                  │
│ • Is there a Teleological Suspension of the Ethical?                        │
│ • The Tragic Hero (Agamemnon, Jephthah, Brutus) vs. Abraham                 │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PROBLEMA II                                                                 │
│ • Is there an Absolute Duty to God?                                         │
│ • The ethical as the universal vs. the single individual above the universal│
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PROBLEMA III                                                                │
│ • Was it ethically defensible for Abraham to conceal his undertaking?       │
│ • The silence of faith: why Abraham cannot speak or make himself understood │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ EPILOGUE                                                                    │
│ • Faith cannot be surpassed or inherited; each generation must start anew   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. Core Axioms & Deductions

1. **The Ethical as the Universal**:
   For Kant and Hegel, morality is universal: it applies equally to all rational agents at all times. Duty consists in subordinating one's private particular inclinations to the universal moral law.
2. **The Paradox of Faith**:
   Faith introduces a radical reversal of the Hegelian hierarchy:
   $$\text{The Single Individual is higher than the Universal.} \quad (\text{FT } 55)$$
   The individual, having once been immersed in the universal ethical law, steps outside it to establish a private, unmediated, absolute relation to the Absolute God.
3. **The Dialectic of the Absurd**:
   Abraham's faith is not mere general optimism or psychological confidence. Abraham believes that God's promise (that Isaac will father a great nation) remains true, *even while he raises the knife to butcher Isaac*. He believes **by virtue of the absurd**—holding together two logically irreconcilable propositions because with God all things are possible.

## 4. Key Arguments & Landmark Formulations

### 1. The Knight of Infinite Resignation vs. The Knight of Faith
Johannes de silentio distinguishes two spiritual figures:
* **The Knight of Infinite Resignation**:
  * Gives up everything they love in this finite world (wealth, love, child) out of duty or philosophical wisdom.
  * Achieves serene, stoic peace through renunciation. They possess spiritual dignity, but remain sorrowful, detached, and severed from earthly happiness.
* **The Knight of Faith**:
  * Makes the initial movement of infinite resignation—fully surrendering Isaac to God.
  * But then, instantaneously, makes the second **movement of faith**: believing that he will receive Isaac back whole in this life!
  * Outwardly, the Knight of Faith looks completely ordinary—like an unassuming tax collector or butcher enjoying a roast Sunday dinner. He fully delights in the finite world because he receives every moment as a miraculous gift *by virtue of the absurd*.[^kierkegaard-ft-ref]

```
                     THE DOUBLE MOVEMENT OF FAITH
     FINITE LIFE ──────────────► INFINITE RESIGNATION ──────────────► FINITE REGAINED
  (Love of Isaac)               (Renouncing Isaac to God)             (Isaac returned
                                                                   by the Absurd!)
```

### 2. Problema I: The Teleological Suspension of the Ethical
Can an ethical obligation ever be suspended for a higher goal (*telos*)?
* **The Tragic Hero (e.g., Agamemnon)**: Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter Iphigenia so the Greek fleet could sail to Troy. While horrifying, Agamemnon's action remains inside the ethical: he sacrificed a lesser private duty (to his daughter) for a higher universal duty (to the Greek state). Agamemnon can weep openly, and his society understands and mourns with him.
* **Abraham**: Abraham has no higher social duty. He is not saving the state or obeying civic law; he is acting solely on a private divine command. In universal ethical terms, Abraham's intended act is plain, monstrous **murder**. Therefore, Abraham either represents a **Teleological Suspension of the Ethical**, or he is damned.

### 3. Problema III: The Agony of Silence
Why did Abraham keep silent on the three-day journey to Moriah, hiding the plan from Sarah, Eleazar, and Isaac?
* Abraham *cannot* speak. If he said, "God has commanded me to kill Isaac," people would hear only the words of a madman or a monster.
* Language operates through the universal; it communicates what is common to all human beings. Because Abraham stands in a unique, solitary relation above the universal, his experience is **incommunicable**. He cannot explain or justify himself. He must walk in absolute silence, trembling with solitary dread.

## 5. Aporias, Paradoxes & Internal Tensions

* **The Problem of Demonic Delusion**: If an individual can suspend the universal moral law based on an unprovable private divine revelation, how can we distinguish a genuine Knight of Faith from a psychotic fanatic or a religious terrorist? Kierkegaard acknowledges this terror: there is no objective external criterion. The Knight of Faith lives in constant, unresolvable trembling (*Angst*).
* **The Pseudonymous Detachment**: Johannes de silentio explicitly confesses that *he himself does not have faith*: *"I can make the movement of infinite resignation... but I cannot make the movement of faith; it makes my head swim."* The text is an anatomy of faith written by an admirer who cannot cross the threshold.

---

## 6. Critical Objections & Historical Reception

1. **The Kantian Objection**: In *Religion within the Boundaries of Mere Reason* (1793), Immanuel Kant addressed Abraham directly: if a voice commands you to kill your son, you can be absolutely certain that the voice is evil and not God, because moral duty is self-evident while private supernatural voices are epistemically untrustworthy.
2. **Emmanuel Levinas's Ethical Critique**: In *Proper Names*, Levinas argued that Kierkegaard's teleological suspension dangerously subordinates ethics to religious ecstasy. For Levinas, the ethical demand of the Other's face is itself the absolute: nothing can suspend our duty not to murder.
3. **Jacques Derrida's Deconstruction**: In *The Gift of Death* (1992), Derrida extended Kierkegaard's insight to all human decision-making: every time we choose to fulfill a duty to one specific person, we are inevitably sacrificing and ignoring our infinite duties to all other human beings—we are all, in a sense, Abrahams on Mount Moriah.
