# Bibliography & Citation Index

<div className="dropcap">

The **Bibliography & Citation Index** serves as the master bibliographic repository for the Philchat Wiki. It records canonical editions, standard academic pagination standards (Stephanus, Bekker, Akademie), and authoritative translations cited across wiki entries. 

</div>

Wiki articles cite sources in right-margin Tufte sidenotes using standard citation keys defined in this index. See [[Citation-Guide|The Wiki Citation Guide]] for formatting rules and guidelines on handling unsourced claims.

---

## 1. Classical & Ancient Philosophy

### Plato
* **[Plato-Republic]**: Plato. *The Republic*. Edited by G.R.F. Ferrari, translated by Tom Griffith. Cambridge Texts in the History of Political Thought. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2000. (Standard citation by Stephanus numbers, e.g., *Resp.* 514a–520a for the Allegory of the Cave).
* **[Plato-Theaetetus]**: Plato. *Theaetetus*. Translated by M.J. Levett, revised by Myles Burnyeat. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 1990. (Cites *Theaet.* 201c–210d for the definition of knowledge as justified true belief).
* **[Plato-Complete]**: Plato. *Complete Works*. Edited by John M. Cooper and D.S. Hutchinson. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 1997.

### Aristotle
* **[Aristotle-NE]**: Aristotle. *Nicomachean Ethics*. Translated by Terence Irwin. 2nd ed. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 1999. (Standard citation by Bekker numbers, e.g., *NE* I.7, 1097b22–1098a20 for the function argument).
* **[Aristotle-Metaphysics]**: Aristotle. *Metaphysics*. Translated by C.D.C. Reeve. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 2016. (Cites *Metaph.* IV.3, 1005b19 for the Principle of Non-Contradiction).
* **[Aristotle-DeAnima]**: Aristotle. *De Anima* (On the Soul). Translated by Christopher Shields. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 2016. (Cites *De An.* II.1, 412a27 for the hylomorphic definition of the soul).

### Pre-Socratics & Early World
* **[Diels-Kranz]**: Diels, Hermann, and Walther Kranz, eds. *Die Fragmente der Vorsokratiker: Griechisch und Deutsch*. 6th ed. Berlin: Weidmann, 1951–1952. (The definitive standard citation scheme for Pre-Socratic philosophers; cited by DK chapter, section 'A' for biographical testimonia, 'B' for authentic verbatim fragments, e.g., DK 28 B 8 for Parmenides' poem on Being, or DK 22 B 12 for Heraclitus on river-stepping).
* **[KRS-Presocratics]**: Kirk, G.S., J.E. Raven, and M. Schofield. *The Presocratic Philosophers: A Critical History with a Selection of Texts*. 2nd ed. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1983. (Standard Diels-Kranz [DK] fragment references).
* **[Olivelle-Upanisads]**: Olivelle, Patrick, trans. *The Early Upanisads: Annotated Text and Translation*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1998. (Includes *Brihadaranyaka* and *Chandogya*).
* **[Laozi-Daodejing]**: Laozi. *Daodejing*. Translated by Philip J. Ivanhoe. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 2002.
* **[Zhuangzi-Watson]**: *The Complete Works of Zhuangzi*. Translated by Burton Watson. New York: Columbia University Press, 2013. (Cited by chapter name and Watson pagination, e.g., Chapter 2 "齐物论" [Equalizing All Things] for the butterfly dream).
* **[Slingerland-Analects]**: Confucius. *Analects: With Selections from Traditional Commentaries*. Translated by Edward Slingerland. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 2003.
* **[Garfield-Nagarjuna]**: Nagarjuna. *The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way: Nagarjuna's Mulamadhyamakakarika*. Translation and commentary by Jay L. Garfield. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1995.
* **[Shankara-Brahmasutra]**: Shankara. *Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya*. Translated by Swāmi Gambhīrānanda. Calcutta: Advaita Ashrama, 1965. (Standard commentary establishing Advaita Vedanta non-dualism).

## 2. Medieval & Islamic Philosophy

* **[IbnSina-Healing]**: Avicenna (Ibn Sina). *The Metaphysics of The Healing* (*al-Shifa'*). A parallel English-Arabic text translated, introduced, and annotated by Michael E. Marmura. Provo: Brigham Young University Press, 2005.
* **[AlGhazali-Incoherence]**: Al-Ghazali. *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* (*Tahafut al-Falasifa*). A parallel English-Arabic text translated by Michael E. Marmura. Provo: Brigham Young University Press, 2000.
* **[IbnRushd-Tahafut]**: Averroes (Ibn Rushd). *Tahafut al-Tahafut* (*The Incoherence of the Incoherence*). Translated by Simon van den Bergh. 2 vols. London: Luzac & Co., 1954.
* **[Aquinas-ST]**: Thomas Aquinas. *Summa Theologiae*. Translated by Fathers of the English Dominican Province. 5 vols. New York: Benziger Brothers, 1948. (Standard citation by Part, Question, Article, e.g., *ST* I, q. 2, a. 3 for the Five Ways).

## 3. Early Modern & Enlightenment Philosophy

* **[Descartes-CSM]**: Descartes, René. *The Philosophical Writings of Descartes*. Translated by John Cottingham, Robert Stoothoff, and Dugald Murdoch. 3 vols. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1985. (Standard citation by Adam-Tannery [AT] volume and page).
  * *Meditations on First Philosophy* (1641), AT VII; CSM II, 1–62.
* **[Spinoza-Gebhardt]**: Spinoza, Baruch. *Spinoza Opera*. Edited by Carl Gebhardt. 4 vols. Heidelberg: Carl Winters, 1925. (Standard academic citation: G I–IV).
* **[Spinoza-Curley]**: Spinoza, Baruch. *The Collected Works of Spinoza*. Edited and translated by Edwin Curley. 2 vols. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1985–2016.
  * *Ethica, ordine geometrico demonstrata* (1677), cited by Part, Definition (d), Axiom (a), Proposition (p), Scholium (s), Corollary (c). E.g., *Ethics* Ip14 for the uniqueness of divine substance.
* **[Locke-Essay]**: Locke, John. *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. Edited by Peter H. Nidditch. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1975. (Standard citation by Book, Chapter, Section, e.g., II.viii.9 for primary/secondary qualities).
* **[Leibniz-AG]**: Leibniz, G.W. *Philosophical Essays*. Translated and edited by Roger Ariew and Daniel Garber. Indianapolis: Hackett Publishing, 1989.
* **[Hume-Treatise]**: Hume, David. *A Treatise of Human Nature*. Edited by David Fate Norton and Mary J. Norton. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2000. (Standard citation: *Treatise* Book.Part.Section.Paragraph, e.g., *THN* 1.3.6.1 for induction).
* **[Hume-Enquiry]**: Hume, David. *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*. Edited by Tom L. Beauchamp. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1999. (Standard citation: *EHU* Section.Paragraph).
* **[Kant-Akademie]**: Kant, Immanuel. *Kants gesammelte Schriften* (Akademie-Ausgabe). Berlin: Walter de Gruyter, 1900–.
* **[Kant-CPR]**: Kant, Immanuel. *Critique of Pure Reason*. Translated and edited by Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood. The Cambridge Edition of the Works of Immanuel Kant. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1998. (Standard citation by first [A, 1781] and second [B, 1787] original pagination, e.g., *CPR* A51/B75: "Thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind").
* **[Kant-GMM]**: Kant, Immanuel. *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*. Translated by Mary Gregor. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1998. (Standard citation by Akademie pagination: Ak. 4:421 for the categorical imperative formula of universal law).

## 4. 19th-Century Philosophy

* **[Hegel-Phenomenology]**: Hegel, G.W.F. *Phenomenology of Spirit*. Translated by Terry Pinkard. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2018. (Standard citation by paragraph number, e.g., §178–196 for lordship and bondage).
* **[Marx-Capital-I]**: Marx, Karl. *Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Volume I*. Translated by Ben Fowkes. London: Penguin Books, 1976. (Cites Chapter 1, Section 4 for commodity fetishism).
* **[Mill-Utilitarianism]**: Mill, John Stuart. *Utilitarianism*. Edited by Roger Crisp. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1998.
* **[Nietzsche-KSA]**: Nietzsche, Friedrich. *Kritische Studienausgabe*. Edited by Giorgio Colli and Mazzino Montinari. 15 vols. Berlin: de Gruyter, 1980.
* **[Nietzsche-Genealogy]**: Nietzsche, Friedrich. *On the Genealogy of Morality*. Translated by Carol Diethe, edited by Keith Ansell-Pearson. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2006. (Standard citation by Essay and Section number, e.g., *GM* I.10 for ressentiment).

* **[Dewey-Logic-1938]**: Dewey, John. *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry*. In *The Later Works of John Dewey, 1925–1953*, Vol. 12. Edited by Jo Ann Boydston. Carbondale: Southern Illinois University Press, 1986. (Primary source for [[Warranted Assertibility]]).
* **[Dewey-Experience-1925]**: Dewey, John. *Experience and Nature*. In *The Later Works of John Dewey, 1925–1953*, Vol. 1. Carbondale: Southern Illinois University Press, 1981.
* **[Rorty-PMN-1979]**: Rorty, Richard. *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature*. Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1979. (Foundational neo-pragmatist anti-representationalism).
* **[Rorty-CP-1982]**: Rorty, Richard. *Consequences of Pragmatism: Essays 1972–1980*. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 1982.
* **[Haack-Evidence-1993]**: Haack, Susan. *Evidence and Inquiry: Towards Reconstruction in Epistemology*. Oxford: Blackwell, 1993. (Primary formulation of foundherentism and the crossword puzzle metaphor).
* **[Haack-Manifesto-1998]**: Haack, Susan. *Manifesto of a Passionate Moderate: Unfashionable Essays*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1998.

## 6. Analytic Philosophy, Language & Mind

* **[Frege-Sinn-1892]**: Frege, Gottlob. "Über Sinn und Bedeutung." *Zeitschrift für Philosophie und philosophische Kritik* 100 (1892): 25–50. Translated as "On Sense and Reference" in *Translations from the Philosophical Writings of Gottlob Frege*, edited by Peter Geach and Max Black. Oxford: Blackwell, 1952.
* **[Russell-Denoting-1905]**: Russell, Bertrand. "On Denoting." *Mind* 14, no. 56 (1905): 479–493.
* **[Wittgenstein-TLP-1921]**: Wittgenstein, Ludwig. *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*. Translated by C.K. Ogden. London: Kegan Paul, 1922. (Cited by proposition number, e.g., 7: "Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent").
* **[Wittgenstein-PI-1953]**: Wittgenstein, Ludwig. *Philosophical Investigations*. Translated by G.E.M. Anscombe, P.M.S. Hacker, and Joachim Schulte. 4th ed. Oxford: Wiley-Blackwell, 2009. (Cited by section number, e.g., §43: "the meaning of a word is its use in the language").
* **[Quine-Dogmas-1951]**: Quine, Willard Van Orman. "Two Dogmas of Empiricism." *The Philosophical Review* 60, no. 1 (1951): 20–43.
* **[Quine-Word-1960]**: Quine, Willard Van Orman. *Word and Object*. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 1960. (Formulation of the indeterminacy of translation and gavagai).
* **[Gettier-1963]**: Gettier, Edmund L. "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" *Analysis* 23, no. 6 (1963): 121–123.
* **[Rawls-TJ-1971]**: Rawls, John. *A Theory of Justice*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1971. (The original position and veil of ignorance).
* **[Nozick-ASU-1974]**: Nozick, Robert. *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*. New York: Basic Books, 1974. (The experience machine and entitlement theory).
* **[Kripke-NN-1980]**: Kripke, Saul. *Naming and Necessity*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1980.
* **[Nagel-Bat-1974]**: Nagel, Thomas. "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" *The Philosophical Review* 83, no. 4 (1974): 435–450.
* **[Jackson-Qualia-1982]**: Jackson, Frank. "Epiphenomenal Qualia." *The Philosophical Quarterly* 32, no. 127 (1982): 127–136. (Formulation of Mary the color scientist).
* **[Jackson-Mary-1986]**: Jackson, Frank. "What Mary Didn't Know." *The Journal of Philosophy* 83, no. 5 (1986): 291–295. (Refinement of the knowledge argument against physicalism).
* **[Searle-MBI-1980]**: Searle, John R. "Minds, Brains, and Programs." *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 3, no. 3 (1980): 417–424. (Formulation of the Chinese Room).
* **[Searle-Social-1995]**: Searle, John R. *The Construction of Social Reality*. New York: Free Press, 1995. (Analysis of institutional facts, constitutive rules, and deontic powers).
* **[Anscombe-MMP-1958]**: Anscombe, G.E.M. "Modern Moral Philosophy." *Philosophy* 33, no. 124 (1958): 1–19. (Founding text of contemporary virtue ethics; critiques divine law obligation without a lawgiver).
* **[Foot-Trolley-1967]**: Foot, Philippa. "The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect." *Oxford Review* 5 (1967): 5–15. (Original formulation of the trolley driver problem and negative vs positive duties).
* **[Foot-Virtues-1978]**: Foot, Philippa. *Virtues and Vices and Other Essays in Moral Philosophy*. Berkeley: University of California Press, 1978.
* **[Foot-Natural-2001]**: Foot, Philippa. *Natural Goodness*. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 2001. (Naturalistic grounding of moral evaluation in the life-form of human species).
* **[Ross-Right-1930]**: Ross, W.D. *The Right and the Good*. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1930. (Formulation of prima facie duties in deontological intuitionism).
* **[MacIntyre-AfterVirtue-1981]**: MacIntyre, Alasdair. *After Virtue: A Study in Moral Theory*. Notre Dame: University of Notre Dame Press, 1981. (Narrative unity of human life, practices, and traditions).
* **[Ayer-LTL-1936]**: Ayer, A.J. *Language, Truth and Logic*. London: Victor Gollancz, 1936. (Canonical British exposition of logical positivism, emotivism, and verificationism).
* **[Carnap-Metaphysics-1932]**: Carnap, Rudolf. "Überwindung der Metaphysik durch logische Analyse der Sprache." *Erkenntnis* 2 (1931): 219–241. (The elimination of metaphysics through logical syntax analysis).
* **[Mackie-Evil-1955]**: Mackie, J.L. "Evil and Omnipotence." *Mind* 64, no. 254 (1955): 200–212. (Logical problem of evil and the inconsistent triad).
* **[Plantinga-God-1974]**: Plantinga, Alvin. *The Nature of Necessity*. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1974. (The Free Will Defense against the logical problem of evil).
* **[Rowe-Evil-1979]**: Rowe, William L. "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism." *American Philosophical Quarterly* 16, no. 4 (1979): 335–341. (Evidential problem of gratuitous suffering and the fawn in the forest).
* **[Parfit-RP-1984]**: Parfit, Derek. *Reasons and Persons*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1984.
* **[Chalmers-Conscious-1996]**: Chalmers, David J. *The Conscious Mind: In Search of a Fundamental Theory*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1996. (Formulation of the Hard Problem).
