24 is a strange number. There are 24 hours, but also: $1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + 24^2 = 70^2$ (the only nontrivial time a stack of square cannonballs makes a square, [Lucas's cannonball problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_problem)); the [Leech lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leech_lattice), the densest packing of spheres in 24 dimensions; the [Golay code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_Golay_code) of length 24; the Mathieu group $M_{24}$; the 24 in string theory's 26 = 24 + 2. People keep lists of these, and the lists mix deep facts, facts that hold only because 24 is the last small number with some property, and noise.

So the paper is called **Twenty-four, sorted**, and it sorts one public list by what kind of evidence each claim has, with a coloured badge in the margin for each (proved, Lean-checked, certified, computed, known, conjectured) and an auto-generated ledger at the back.

Some things that came out of it:

- Four of the curios are secretly *one* statement: the units mod $n$ all square to 1 exactly when $n$ divides 24. Proved in [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/).
- Two "largest known" curios turn out to be complete lists, closed by explicit prime-gap bounds.
- The cannonball fact is really geometry. The vector $(0, 1, 2, \dots, 24 \mid 70)$ is a null vector, a ray of light, in a 26-dimensional Lorentzian lattice, and it is how [Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Horton_Conway) built the Leech lattice. We classify every such "consecutive" null vector: there are exactly four, and three of them give the Leech lattice. The $k$-gonal version of the cannonball (triangles, pentagons...) becomes a *shifted* light cone, which explains why squares are the only case that lands on the light itself.

The sweep for the $k$-gonal solutions is still running; today it certified $k = 56$, with the solution $(n, m) = (970, 17443)$, and a few curves are still timing out. Those stay marked as open rather than promoted to theorems - the point of the badges is that the numerology never outruns the evidence.

Companion reading I've been doing alongside: a guided walk through [Viazovska's proof](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2017/185-3/p07) that $E_8$ is the densest packing in eight dimensions, with the magic function rebuilt numerically.
