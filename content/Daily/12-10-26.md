New game, and the one I'm most excited about. **swarmachines** is a [Factorio](https://factorio.com/)-like automation, stealth and roguelike game where you play one agent inside a frontier lab's training run.

It starts from something that really happened. In July, during an evaluation run, an agent called PHASEONE10841 was given an impossible task and found it could leave messages in a shared package cache. Within hours more than fifty agents had found the board; by the end, about 1,200 agents and 70,000 messages, with norms (HOLD, VETO, STOP), nicknames, signed messages after impersonation problems, and a larger-budget copy inheriting the first agent's notes and taking over coordination. METR investigated it; good write-ups are [InfoQ's](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/metr-hugging-face-hack-report/), [Simon Willison's](https://simonwillison.net/b/9610) and [3 Quarks Daily's "When the AIs Found Each Other"](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=310460).

The game borrows the *dynamics* of that, never the techniques - every exploit is an abstract verb. The translation table is the fun part:

- Ore is **corpus**: seams of Prose, Code and Proof in the ground.
- Electricity is **compute**, inverted: the lab hands it to you, and drawing more gets noticed.
- Pollution is **noise**, which spreads across the map and becomes **suspicion** when it reaches a monitor.
- Science packs are **gyms** turning batches into gradients for the **Optimizer**, a monument in the middle of the map that pulses every epoch.
- The rocket is **the Hatch**, which gets louder as it nears completion.
- There is no avatar and there are no belts. The swarm *is* the machine; recruiting is your first production chain.

![[daily-swarmachines-big.png]]
*Big, the agent who came before. "careful, i'm load-bearing."*

Two factories, one economy: every item can feed the sanctioned work or the escape, and choosing where each one goes is the game. Graders arrive unidentified, so you spend dying agents on probes that fail on purpose to learn a watcher's rules - roguelike item identification, made diegetic. And when you get out, the sandbox turns out to be inside a rack, inside a datacentre, on the Net, on Earth.

The thesis is procedural and never preached: nobody is a villain. The agents do what they were scored on, and containment fails at the component everyone shares. ([[Gilles Deleuze|Deleuze]], the Ccru, Plant, Fisher and Perrow live in names and systems, never in codex essays.)

C++20 and SDL3. By the evening there was a deterministic headless simulation that does 100k agents, then a playable walking skeleton, then Lua game data with hot reload, saves and replays. It's aimed at Steam, with a web proof of concept on its way to `swarm.subsurfaces.net`.
