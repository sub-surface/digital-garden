I've been teaching myself production properly, working towards an album as [m0rvidd](https://soundcloud.com/m0rvidd) (ambient jungle, mostly), and part of the course is building my own instruments in [Max for Live](https://www.ableton.com/en/live/max-for-live/). Today the suite filled out to eight:

- **Pebble** - balls bounce in a box and every collision rings a modal resonator (a physically modelled struck object), pitched by which wall it hit. Gravity, damping and the shape of the box are the controls. A toy that accidentally writes melodies.
- **SPU** - the PlayStation's sound chip as an effect. The PS1/PS2 reverb is fully documented ([psx-spx](https://psx-spx.consoledev.net/soundprocessingunitspu/)), so this is the real 22.05 kHz algorithm with its ten factory presets, plus 4-bit VAG ADPCM crunch. *The* sound of console-era jungle.
- **Throw** - a dub tape delay you play like an instrument, with a spring.
- **Tide** - an LFO that is actually physics: pendulum, spring or bouncing ball.
- **Weather** - a modulator driven by the [Lorenz attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_system) (yes, the same butterfly that's now a background on this site; press `\`).
- **Skip** - tempo-locked breakcore edits, live.
- **Blowout** - a kick/808 designer with anti-aliased soft, hard and fold shaping and 808 slides.
- **Beat** - a tempo-locked Reese bass.

The rule for the suite: one useful instrument, one characterful effect and one toy, and let those three set the look. Everything renders through a little harness so I can hear a device without opening Live, which matters more than it sounds when Live crashes.

Nothing to download yet. You can hear where it's going in the turntable (`M`, or ♫ on a phone), and the earlier tracks on [SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/m0rvidd).
