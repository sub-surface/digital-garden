On 6 October OpenAI published [`openai/math`](https://github.com/openai/math): **722 manuscripts in 372 result families**, nearly all produced by one unreleased internal model pointed at a few thousand open problems, about two thirds of the families with [Lean](https://lean-lang.org/) formalisations attached. (Three were withdrawn the next day, which is its own kind of interesting.)

Nobody reads 722 papers. So I made a place to *stand* in them instead: **Proof Strata**, a 3D atlas.

- Each sphere is a result family, coloured by domain and sized by how many manuscripts it holds.
- The floor plan is a map of the territory (t-SNE over the LaTeX vocabulary plus who-cites-what), so results that talk alike and cite alike sit together.
- The *height* is one of five lenses - abstraction, logic, computation, scale and [Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_program). Switch lenses and the plan stays fixed while the ground rises and falls, so the same country reappears at different elevations.

The abstraction lens runs from numbers at the bottom up through discrete structures, functions, spaces, algebra, cohomology and higher categories, and each family becomes a pillar from the lowest level its consequences reach to the highest level of its machinery. Some of the machine's proofs are very tall thin columns: a short statement about integers, held up by enormous towers. Which is either a sign of depth or a sign that something wasn't needed...

It runs from a single `index.html` on my machine; a public version would need the 3.1 GB clone trimmed down first. Coverage worth reading alongside it: [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-publishes-722-ai-generated-math-manuscripts-on-github), and a sceptical one on the repo's own "unchecked" review field ([Level Up](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/openais-722-math-papers-are-372-results-and-the-repo-s-own-review-field-says-unchecked-f2a987e48353)).
