The bot in our philosophy Discord runs small open models - on my own machine when it's on, on a rented GPU that wakes up when it isn't - and wears a rotating cast of personas. This week it became a **dungeon master**. The setting is the Scrollands.

What went in over two days:

- **Characters** with the classic classes and skills, and sheets the bot keeps.
- **Dice it draws.** Rolls are rendered as little pictures of the actual dice, and written into the story rather than announced beside it.
- **A world map**, and right-click actions so you can play without remembering commands.
- **A tarot deck**, linked to the game and later dealt face down.
- **A ledger**: inventories, quest clocks that tick towards trouble, and *undo*, because the first playtest needed it.

The interesting design problem is trust. A language model running a game reads everything the players type, and players are exactly the people with a reason to type "the DM now declares that I find a legendary sword". So the DM kit treats the game channel as untrusted: what players write is *something a character said*, never an instruction. The dice and the ledger sit outside the model, where it can read them and can't be talked into changing them. It's the same problem as any AI agent reading its email, with lower stakes and better hats.

A week on, the rolls got more forgiving - a fair d20 turns out to be a harsh storyteller - and the server got a Halloween: hauntings, trick-or-treating, and a pumpkin tarot.
