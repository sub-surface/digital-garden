**giving**, *vbl. n.* and *ppl. a.*
Chiefly in the phrase ***it's giving***.
Brit. /ɪts ˈɡɪvɪŋ/, U.S. /ɪts ˈɡɪvɪŋ/

> [Origin uncertain. App. a shortening of an earlier construction *it is giving (off) the impression of*, with ellipsis of the object. Cf. GIVE *v.* 47c, "to emit, to yield up." A parallel development in the sense "to resemble, to read as" is recorded independently in several communities and the direction of borrowing is disputed; see note at sense 4.]

---

### 1.

*obs.* Of a fabric or garment: to yield or stretch under strain; to fail to hold its shape.

**1591** — R. HAKE, *Booke of Housewiferie* 44
> The wooll being ill spun, it is giving at the shoulder, and will not sitte.

**1648** — *Draper's Company Minutes* (Norwich) 12 Mar.
> Complaynt made that the cloth soulde was giving, and monies returned.

---

### 2.

Of a structure, surface, or support: to be on the point of collapse. Now chiefly *dial.* and *techn.*

**1774** — J. BRINDLEY, *Letter* 3 Aug.
> The eastern arch is giving, and I would not have the waggons upon it another day.

**1902** — *Mining Jrnl.* 18 Oct. 441/2
> The men came up at once. When a prop is giving there is no argument to be had with it.

---

### 3.

Of a person: to be generous, esp. habitually. Freq. with adverb.

**1836** — DICKENS, *Sketches* (1839) II. 88
> A free-handed gentleman, and always giving.

**1958** — *Woman's Own* 4 Sept. 21/1
> She is giving, in the way that leaves you nothing to give back, which is its own kind of taking.

---

### 4.

*colloq.* (orig. and chiefly in African-American and drag ballroom usage). Of a thing, image, or person: to convey, evoke, or read as; to project the quality specified. Usually with complement, freq. without.

> **Note.** The construction is not, as is sometimes claimed, an abbreviation of sense 3. The semantic bridge is sense 1: something that *gives* has yielded up what it was holding in. The evaluative sense retains this — to say a thing *is giving* is to say it has released something it did not intend to. Hence the near-universal implication of the complement-less form (**4c**), which is not neutral.

**c.** *absol.*, without complement: to succeed; to achieve the intended effect. Chiefly in the negative, ***it's not giving***.

**1991** — Ballroom commentary, New York (transcribed 2004)
> That's not giving. That's *trying*, and trying is the opposite.

**2016** — @user post, 8 Nov.
> the fit is giving

**2019** — *Vulture* 3 Apr. (online)
> The whole third act is giving "we ran out of money," and I say that with love.

**2022** — Overheard, Cardiff, 14 Feb.
> — Is it giving?
> — It's giving.
> — Right.

**2024** — Comment thread, 22 July
> not to be that guy but it's giving 1591

## Note on the 1591 attestation

The Hake citation has been questioned.[^hake] The manuscript is genuine and the reading is not in doubt; what is disputed is whether a spinner's complaint about a shoulder seam belongs in the same entry as a comment about someone's third act.

I think it does, and I think the entry is stronger for the four-century gap sitting inside it undisguised. The word did not change its meaning. It has meant, continuously, *this thing has let out what it was holding.* A shoulder seam does that under strain. So does a film that has run out of money. So, on a bad day, does a person.

Everything is giving. That has always been the complaint.

[^hake]: Principally by those who would prefer the phrase to be four years old, which would make it possible to be finished with it.
