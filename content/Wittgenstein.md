# Wittgenstein

Wittgenstein matters to me because he keeps reminding me that philosophical confusion is often a symptom of language gone slightly crooked, not a sign that reality has become impossible.

I like the discipline in that. It feels close to writing practice: looking at the ordinary thing until it stops pretending to be simple. He connects naturally to [[Philosophy of Mind]] and [[Writing/Attention & Difference|On Attention]], especially where meaning depends on use, practice, and forms of life rather than private certainty.

He is also a good corrective to my more expansive impulses. Sometimes the right move is not to elaborate, but to notice the sentence that has started doing too much.
