*To be read responsively. The reader takes both parts, as is traditional.*

---

**Q.** What is the inbox?

**R.** The inbox is the record of what has been asked of me, kept by another, and shown to me daily whether I consent or not.

**Q.** Who keeps it?

**R.** I do not know. I have never met the keeper. The keeper does not sleep and does not observe the sabbath.

**Q.** How many are unread?

**R.** Forty-seven thousand, three hundred and fourteen.

**Q.** Is this a great number?

**R.** It is a number that has stopped being a number. Below one hundred it was a number and it accused me. Above ten thousand it became weather.

---

**Q.** What is Inbox Zero?

**R.** Inbox Zero is the state in which nothing is asked of me that I have not answered.

**Q.** Has any person attained it?

**R.** Some claim it. They are found, on inspection, to have three inboxes, or an assistant, or a system of folders into which the unanswered are moved and called answered.

**Q.** Is the folder not the same as the inbox?

**R.** It is exactly the same as the inbox. This is the first mystery.

---

**Q.** By what means, then, might the inbox be emptied?

**R.** By works, or by grace.

**Q.** What is the way of works?

**R.** To answer each in turn, beginning at the oldest, until none remain.

**Q.** Is the way of works sufficient?

**R.** It is not. In the time taken to answer one hundred, one hundred and forty arrive. The way of works is a race run against a thing that is not running.

**Q.** What is the way of grace?

**R.** *Select all. Mark as read.*

**Q.** Is that not a lie?

**R.** It is not a lie. Nothing has been claimed. The counter has been set to zero and the messages remain exactly where they were, unopened, unrefused. Grace does not undo the debt. It declines to display it.

**Q.** Is this permitted?

**R.** It is permitted. There is no authority to forbid it. This is the second mystery, and the more difficult one, because it means the accusation was never external.

---

**Q.** What of the messages themselves?

**R.** Of the forty-seven thousand: some forty-four thousand are notifications of things I did not do, from services I no longer use, about accounts I cannot access. Perhaps nine hundred are receipts. Perhaps four hundred are newsletters I subscribed to in a spirit of self-improvement and have never opened, each one a small monument to a better version of me who was going to read about architecture.

**Q.** And the rest?

**R.** Eleven are from people who wanted something from me and gave up.

**Q.** Do you know which eleven?

**R.** No. That is why I cannot mark them as read.

---

**Q.** What, then, is to be done?

**R.** Nothing is to be done. The inbox is not a task. It is a place where time is stored in the wrong format.

**Q.** How shall we live?

**R.** Badly, and with the notification badge visible, as our fathers did before us.

**Q.** Amen?

**R.** Forty-seven thousand, three hundred and eighteen.
