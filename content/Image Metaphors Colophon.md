**Leon (answers, round 7):**

On "anything may do anything": We're still slightly misunderstanding but that's ok - "anything may do anything" is a statement about radical contingency but, in parallel, about the radical autonomy of all things to do what they will do. The emergence of some event (a particular object doing something) only happens when our particular object actually does that thing, it needs the object to take that act in order to obtain. However, the facticity of some occurance does not eliminate the possibility that it did not occur - which is not to say "it could never not happen" as you seem to be taking it, since there's an eternal fact about the emergence of the event that was true *of the time* that it *may not have happened* - so even when we have events, when we recognise actualities, we also recognise that they may not have happened, that it retains the possibility that it did not occur - otherwise we are collapsing the two tracks of possible and real, which never happens. The actual, the events which occur, do not displace or negate the truth of the possibility that they did not occur. This has lots of interesting implications and relations (and confuses lots of people as its kinda unintuitive, people want actuality to negate possibility of things happening otherwise), but one of the important ones is fending off pragmatist objections of infallibilism about knowledge (that having knowledge is about having knowledge of actual things, clear and distinct ideas, true beliefs) since we always retain the possibility that our knowledge is mistaken, that events did not occur etc. We recognise the freedom inherent to all things to do otherwise.
