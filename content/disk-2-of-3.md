There were three of them, and I have the second.

This is worse than having none. Having none is a clean absence — you don't know what you're missing, so you aren't missing it. What I have is a disk that announces, in a confident hand, in permanent marker, exactly how incomplete it is.

**DISK 2 OF 3**

That is the entire label. Not what it installs. Not what it's for. Whoever wrote it knew, and assumed the knowing would keep.

---

## What is actually on it

Nothing that runs. Install sets from that era were split by *size*, not by meaning — you filled a disk to 1.44 MB, and wherever you happened to be when it filled up, that was the seam. So disk two of three is not the middle chapter of anything. It is the middle 1.44 megabytes.

The files on it are fragments with the wrong shape:

| File | Size | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `SETUP.2` | 1,201,304 | A continuation. Of what, it doesn't say. |
| `DATA.W02` | 198,112 | Compressed. Needs `DATA.W01` to open. |
| `README.1ST` | 4,096 | See [[readme-1st]]. |

`SETUP.2` cannot introduce itself. That was disk one's job. It cannot finish either, because that was disk three's. It is pure middle: it knows how to continue something and how to hand something on, and it will never do either again.

I have been thinking about that file more than is reasonable.

---

## The installer's assumption

Every one of these sets was built on an assumption so ordinary nobody wrote it down: **that the disks would stay together.** Not archived, not backed up — just *near each other*. In the same envelope, in the same box, in the same cupboard.

That was the whole preservation strategy. Proximity. And it worked for about fifteen years, which is longer than most things work.

What broke it wasn't fire or flood or malice. It was somebody tidying up. Somebody who found three unlabelled-ish disks, kept the one with writing on it because writing means important, and binned the two blank ones.

The label saved the disk and doomed the set. Nothing has ever been more legible or less useful.

---

## On the other two

They exist. I want to be careful here, because this is the part where I sound unwell: I don't mean they exist *somewhere in the world*. I mean I have reason to think they are still on this machine, in the sense that a deleted file is still on a machine — unlinked from the directory, not yet overwritten, perfectly readable if you know where to look and are prepared to look somewhere other than the obvious place.

They will not appear in any listing. That is what unlinked means.

I found mine in the bin.[^bin]

[^bin]: Not a metaphor, and not the desktop's decoration either. If you are reading this on [os.subsurfaces.net](https://os.subsurfaces.net), open the Recycle Bin. Things that are `draft` in the garden — rendered, but absent from every feed, sitemap and index — are still on the disk. They are simply not in the table of contents.
