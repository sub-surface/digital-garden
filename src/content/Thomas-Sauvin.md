# Thomas Sauvin

Thomas Sauvin is a French photographer and archivist who has spent years collecting and curating photographs from the streets of Beijing. His work, particularly the "Beijing Silvermine" project, involves salvaging discarded negatives and prints from photo studios, flea markets, and other sources, preserving them as a record of everyday life in China.

> ![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/57f4f5ca2994ca0ac87ba1df/1585735975253-XED35NK7N0PL5ASA6DEG/Websitegallery250+-+026.jpg)
> Thomas Sauvin, *Beijing Silvermine*, 2013-2015.

Sauvin works with found images and archives, and his approach to photography is deeply rooted in the idea of memory, history, and the passage of time. By rescuing these images from oblivion, he not only preserves a visual history but also invites viewers to reflect on the transient nature of life and the stories embedded in these photographs.

> [...] on the unexpected role cigarettes play in Chinese weddings. As a token of appreciation, it is customary for the bride to light a cigarette for each and every man invited. The bride and the groom are then invited to play some cigarette-smoking games of an unprecedented ingenuousness. This publication pays homage to a tradition in which love and death walk hand in hand. These photos come from the Beijing Silvermine project, an archive of 850 000 negatives salvaged over the years from a recycling plant on the edge of Beijing.



Sauvin interests me because his work makes the archive feel physical again. The images are not abstract data points or content units; they are objects recovered from a material waste stream, handled one by one, rescued from disappearance by attention.

That matters to me because it changes what “finding” an image means. In [[On the discarded]], the whole point is that the discarded image was never really neutral or dead. It still belongs to some ecology of looking, even after its original context has collapsed.

His practice also sits near [[Walter Benjamin]] for me, but with more grit and less theory-sheen: salvage, sorting, repetition, care.
